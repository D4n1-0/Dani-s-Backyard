# Chapter 01 – Adam and Eve

## Summary

A routine maritime patrol aircraft over the Celtic Sea detects an impossible shimmer on the water — [[The Anomaly]], a vertical curtain of light connecting modern Earth to the [[Trench Crusade Earth|Trench Crusade]] timeline. On the far side, a dreadnought of the Faithful [[Crown of England]] and its escorts confront the same impossible window. Over twelve hours, through signal lamps, VHF radio, and finally a face-to-face meeting at [[The Portal Threshold]], two worlds that diverged in 1099 take their first measure of each other.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: [[Atlantic Ocean|The Celtic Sea]] — A Wall of Light

**[TIME]: Day 1 — 18 June 2026, 08:32 Zulu**  
**[POV]: Flight Lieutenant [[Alice Hartley]]*

The first hint was not a sight but a scream of static across the maritime patrol frequency, a rising whine that bled into the 406 MHz guard channel and died in a whisper. Then the forward-looking infrared bloomed white, as if the sea itself had caught fire.

"Sierra One, I've got a heat spike. Bearing two-six-zero, range twenty-eight miles. Magnitude… off scale," the tactical coordinator said, voice careful with disbelief. "Not a ship fire. Not a flare. It's a curtain, ma'am. A wall."

[[Alice Hartley|Hartley]] banked the Poseidon gently, the grey Atlantic tilting beneath her wings. And there it was. The ocean wore a scar of light. A vertical shimmer, a mile wide, rising from the water like a sheet of aurora that had forgotten to stay in the sky. It climbed at least a hundred metres, its edge a sharp, unnatural line. Below, the sea itself was bisected; the same swell rolled through the curtain with an oily distortion, wave crests passing into somewhere else.

She descended to one thousand feet, rules of engagement hammering in her skull: *observe and report.* But observation was suddenly very difficult, because on the far side of that impossible veil, moving slowly through a fog that did not belong to this world, were ships. Old ships. She could see the battleship-grey hull of a dreadnought, twin turrets fore and aft, masts like cathedral spires festooned with signal halyards and great black banners. Behind it, smaller escorts: a three-funnelled cruiser, a low-slung destroyer. Coal smoke smeared a pale, unfamiliar dawn.

"Are you seeing this?" she breathed, not caring who answered.

The crew had gone silent. On the thermal, [[The Anomaly|the portal]] gave a signature that was less heat than a wound in physics. On radar, nothing. But visually, the ships were filling the curtain's view, closer now, as if they too had just stumbled upon a miracle.

Then the voice of the TACCO cut in, flat and controlled, the way one reads the last rites. "Guernsey One to UK Air Defence. Priority flash. Possible contact with non-terrestrial craft. One large combatant, two escorts, active steaming lights. Bearing from my position…"

[[Alice Hartley|Hartley]] let him speak. Below, the flanks of the dreadnought caught the first true daylight of her world, and the name painted in gold leaf across its stern, written in an English she knew but a script older than her nation, gleamed through the wet morning air.

*HMS Resolute.*

No aircraft carrier, no frigate, no submarine in His Majesty's current fleet bore that name. And no ship of her world had ever worn a thirty-foot crucifix welded to its bridge with the unmistakable gloss of fresh human blood.

### Scene 2: *[[HMS Resolute]]* — The Faithful Response

**[TIME]: T+0 hours — the same instant, Year of Our Lord 1914, Feast of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross**  
**[POV]: Captain [[Alistair Pembroke]]*

Dawn had come in ribbons of crimson and ash-grey, the sky bleeding light into a fretful sea. [[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] stood on the open bridge wing, gloved hands resting on the teak rail, his breath ghosting in the chill. The smell of coal smoke and anointed iron was as constant as his heartbeat. This was a watch like any other on the Atlantic picket line, where the [[Heretic Legion|Heretic fleet]] from captured Gibraltar might show its foul prow at any hour. The ship's chaplain had just completed the office of Matins, and the scent of beeswax and chrism still clung to the bridge.

Then the light changed.

Not a shell-burst, not a star-shell. A wall of rippling silver-blue unreality unfolded directly across their course, a curtain half-sunk into the sea. It stretched beyond the limits of vision to port and starboard, silent and impossibly thin, like a slice of eternity pulled across the world. Through it, [[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] could see water — the same water, wave meeting wave in an oily confusion — but beyond that water, a sky that was completely, dreadfully wrong. The clouds were paler, the light harder, as if God had changed the hue of creation. And in that alien sky, a strange cruciform bird was banking, its wings rigid, its tail emblazoned with a blue-white-red roundel he did not recognise. No propeller. No pilot visible. Yet it flew with the certainty of a hawk.

"All hands to quarters!" The order was out of his mouth before his mind caught up. Bells rang below. Gun crews scrambled, the heavy breeches of the twelve-inch turrets groaning open. The signals yeoman was already at his side, face bleached. "Captain — the escorts report a vision, an apparition, they see it too. *Lancelot* and *Halberd* are falling into line astern."

"Helm, bring us two points to starboard. Steady as she goes. Maintain coal but stand by for emergency flank." [[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] raised his telescope, the brass cold against his eye. He did not look at the flying machine; he looked beyond it. Far across the water on the other side, at the limit of clarity through the shimmer, he saw shapes. Low, grey, angular. Modern ships of a design that chilled his soul with their austerity — no smoke, no holy icons, no masts, just a faceted tower amidships and a sleek hull. One of them was turning, a sharp, purposeful curve that would have snapped a cruiser of his own era in half.

"Chaplain," he said quietly. [[Father Beauchamp]] stepped forward, his face a mask of holy terror. "Perform the Rite of Discernment. Now. I will know if this is infernal glamour or something else entirely."

The priest began to pray in the old Latin, sprinkling salt and blessed water into the wind. [[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] whispered an older prayer himself. *St. Michael the Archangel, defend us in battle.* Then he told his signals officer to hoist a single string of flags and flash a lamp message, in the universal code of the sea.

"UNKNOWN VESSELS. THIS IS HIS MAJESTY'S SHIP RESOLUTE. IDENTIFY YOURSELVES OR BE FIRED UPON."

He had no faith they would understand. But he had even less faith that the silence would hold.

### Scene 3: [[Northwood Headquarters]] — The Command Post

**[TIME]: T+1 hour 48 minutes — 18 June 2026, 10:20 Zulu**  
**[POV]: Commander [[Sarah Vance]]*

The feed from "Guernsey 01" had been upgraded to the main threat board. The admiral was on his feet, one hand pressed to his earpiece as the White House and NATO Brussels screamed for data. [[Sarah Vance|Vance]] sat at the analytical desk, scrolling through frames of imagery, her cup of cold coffee forgotten beside a stack of SITREP printouts.

"[[The Anomaly|Portal]] is stable. One thousand metres wide, vertically two hundred metres, centred at mean sea level. No detectable radiation signature beyond a wideband electromagnetic disruption consistent with a coronal mass ejection. It is not a weapon we understand." The MOD science advisor, a civilian from Dstl who had been pulled straight from a conference on quantum metrology, was almost cheerful. "And the ships on the other side are, for all intents and purposes, a British *Dreadnought*-class battleship with highly anachronistic modifications. We're seeing active steam propulsion, big-gun armament, but the superstructure is… baroque. Excessive religious symbology. We also have three escort vessels corresponding to early 1900s torpedo-boat destroyers and a protected cruiser. No electronics, no radio frequency traffic we can detect."

*[[Sarah Vance|Vance]] had already spoken to the captains of [[HMS Dragon|HMS Dragon]]*, *[[HMS Defender|Defender]]*, and *[[HMS Duncan|Duncan]]* — the three Type 45 destroyers that were the closest major surface assets. They had been exercising off Plymouth. Now they were steaming west at thirty knots, their Sea Viper missiles cold but ready, their Wildcat helicopters fuelled. Their orders were unambiguous: *establish visual and signal contact, do not provoke, do not cross the threshold.* A wider exclusion zone was being assembled, submarines from Faslane moving to deep-water stations.

"The other side has raised a challenge," an intelligence lieutenant said, putting a transcript onto her screen. "Garbled Morse and signal flags, but linguists confirm it's English. Archaic syntax, liturgical English idiom. They're asking for identity and warning of fire."

"Then we answer them," [[Sarah Vance|Vance]] said, rubbing the bridge of her nose. "Have *[[HMS Dragon|Dragon]]* — she's the lead — transmit our standard hail on VHF Channel 16, in the clear, and repeat with signal lamp in plain text. Text is: 'This is Royal Navy warship Dragon. We come in peace. We are a vessel of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. We intend no hostile act. Please communicate intentions.' Keep it simple. And for the love of God, nobody fire a missile."

She watched the feed as the three sleek destroyers, grey ghosts beneath a low overcast, closed the distance to the impossible. The dreadnought on the other side now had its guns trained not quite at them, but near enough. Its signal lamps were winking furiously.

*What if they're not mad?* she thought. *What if they're living something worse than we ever imagined?*

She didn't yet know the half of it.

### Scene 4: *[[HMS Dragon]]* — The Exchange

**[TIME]: T+6 hours 14 minutes — 18 June 2026, 14:46 Zulu**  
**[POV]: Captain [[James Hawthorne]]*

The portal was a permanent second sun now, a shimmering fence across the sea. From *[[HMS Dragon|Dragon]]*'s bridge, [[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]] could see the other world with the clarity of a polished window. The dreadnought *[[HMS Resolute|Resolute]]* sat exactly five thousand yards on the far side, matched in position by the two escorts, a trinity of coal and steel and obsessive faith. The cruiser's signal lamp was blinking again, its cadence the steady rhythm of a bygone century.

In the hours since their arrival, [[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]]'s crew had exchanged a carefully scripted series of messages. The language was formal, loaded with phrases like "by the Grace of God" and "Adversary" and "Faithful Crown." At first, he'd assumed they faced a highly theatrical hostile power. But the deeper the exchange went, the more unsettling the truth became. They believed — truly, deeply believed — that they were the front line in a war against literal Hell.

"They've asked again, sir," Lieutenant Calloway said, handing over a signal pad. "Are we 'sanctified by the blood of Christ and free of infernal taint.' Direct quote."

[[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]] read the words, and the weight of them settled in his chest. "Have we given them any reason to think otherwise?"

"No, sir. But they're insisting. Their chaplain has performed some kind of… ritual. They want to know if we acknowledge the Trinity."

He looked out the window at the dreadnought. Its funnels were sending up a steady column of black smoke, but the ship had not altered position. The twin twelve-inch turrets remained trained slightly off-axis, a gesture of strained neutrality. On *[[HMS Defender|Defender]]* and *[[HMS Duncan|Duncan]]*, the situation was identical. An uneasy triangle of modern steel facing a wall of holy dreadnought.

"Send: 'We acknowledge the God of peace. We are not your enemies. We request a face-to-face parley at the portal boundary, with one unarmed boat from each side.' Use the simplest English. And add 'God save the King.' Maybe that'll buy us some trust."

As the lamp blinked its message across the division of worlds, [[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]] saw a flurry of activity on the dreadnought's bridge wing. A man in a dark uniform — the captain, he presumed — stared back through a long glass. Then the lamp replied: "WE AGREE. ONE BOAT. WE SHALL SEND A PRIEST. PRAY YOU DO NOT LIE."

[[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]] exhaled. Twelve hours of standoff were about to be cracked open by a rowing boat.

### Scene 5: *[[HMS Resolute]]* — The Priest's Crossing

**[TIME]: T+8 hours 02 minutes — 18 June 2026 / 17 June 1914, mid-morning on the far side**  
**[POV]: Captain [[Alistair Pembroke]]*

The priest was ready. [[Father Beauchamp]] had donned the armoured vestments reserved for boarding actions, the chasuble stitched with silver-thread prayers of warding. He held a processional crucifix of [[Orichalcum]] steel — the precious metal that could turn aside shells and spells alike — and a leather-bound copy of the *Canticles of Antioch*. The boat crew were all volunteers, men who had survived the Black Grail coast raids and seen things that made a hole in the sea seem mild by comparison.

[[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] watched them lower the launch, the falls creaking. On the other side of the curtain, a similar boat was being put into the water from one of the strange vessels, a small rigid-hulled craft that moved without oars, though they had cut the engine as a gesture of truce. The crew in that boat wore neat blue camouflage, life vests, and no visible weapons except a holstered sidearm. They looked efficient, secular, terrifyingly ordinary.

"Captain, the Adversary is cunning," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] had said before descending. "But in all the divinations I have cast, there is no brimstone on that wind, no whisper of the Tyrants. These men are not of Hell. They are not of our Earth at all. I feel only a great abyss of ignorance — and a profound loss."

[[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] did not reply. He only ordered the signal lamp to flash one final message: "IN THE NAME OF THE ALMIGHTY, WE MEET AT THE THRESHOLD."

The launch pulled away, oars dipping into the grey sea. On the other side, the rubber boat nudged toward the shimmering boundary, a woman in the bow holding a bright lamp and a portable radio. The two boats halted ten yards from the curtain, face to face through the undulating light, so close they could have seen the colour of each other's eyes.

### Scene 6: [[The Portal Threshold]] — First Contact

**[TIME]: T+12 hours — 18 June 2026, 20:32 Zulu**  
**[POV]: Lieutenant Commander [[Rebecca Osei]]*

The curtain of [[The Anomaly|the portal]] was cold against her face, not physically, but as a presence. It hummed at the edge of hearing, a frequency that made her fillings ache. Twelve hours of training and protocols had collapsed into this single moment of raw human contact.

On the other side, a wooden boat sat on the same heaving sea, its crew wearing oilskins and expressions of militant piety. The man at the bow, a priest in battle-stained robes, raised his crucifix, not as a threat but as a test. [[Rebecca Osei|Osei]]'s orders were clear: make no religious gesture, but offer no offence. She simply stood, empty hands open.

The priest's voice, thick with an accent that had vanished from England four hundred years ago, boomed across the threshold. "In nomine Patris, et Filii, et Spiritus Sancti. If you be mortal men and women, answer truly. Do you serve the Most High God, or have you truck with the Adversary?"

[[Rebecca Osei|Osei]] had been briefed on the question, and she answered the only way that held integrity. "We are servants of our country, not of any hell. We did not open this door. We only came to understand it. Our King is called Charles, and he wishes only peace."

A long pause. The priest exchanged a look with the officer beside him. Then the officer, a young man with a scarred jaw and haunted eyes, said, "Charles… That name is not in our chronicles. You are the future, or a dream. But we have seen dreams before that bled real blood."

He drew a heavy service revolver from his belt, its cylinder glinting in the grey light, then slowly set it down in the bottom of the boat. "Very well. We will talk. But if a single demon or heretic crosses into our world from yours, we will burn the sea to ash."

[[Rebecca Osei|Osei]] allowed herself a breath. The standoff had not ended; it had merely opened its first chapter. Behind her, *[[HMS Dragon|Dragon]]*, *[[HMS Defender|Defender]]*, and *[[HMS Duncan|Duncan]]* held station, their Sea Viper missiles still silent. Ahead, the dreadnought *[[HMS Resolute|Resolute]]* stood like a temple afloat, its guns dark but not safe.

The portal hummed between two worlds. And in that hum, she heard the first notes of a history that neither Earth had ever imagined.

## Key Decisions

- Both sides exercise restraint despite the unprecedented nature of the contact. Neither fires a shot.
- [[James Hawthorne|Captain Hawthorne]] sends "God save the King" as a trust-building gesture, which aligns with the Faithful's royalist worldview.
- [[Alistair Pembroke|Captain Pembroke]] orders the Rite of Discernment before any engagement, confirming the absence of infernal taint.
- The parley is established at [[The Portal Threshold]] with one unarmed boat from each side, creating a neutral zone between worlds.
