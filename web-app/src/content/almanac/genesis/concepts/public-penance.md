# Public Penance

## Summary

The [[Crown of England|Faithful Crown]]'s system of public corporal punishment for doctrinal deviation — stocks, public admonishments, forced recantations — administered by the [[Office of the Propagation of Virtue]].

## Details

In the Trench Crusade timeline, the war against the [[Heretic Legion]] has rendered heresy a military threat as much as a spiritual one. A single unpunished deviation from doctrine is considered a vulnerability that the Adversary can exploit — a crack in the Faithful front through which infernal influence can seep. The Office of the Propagation of Virtue maintains doctrinal purity through a system of public penance: offenders are placed in stocks at public squares, ordered to recite formal recantations, or marched to correction through the streets. Punishments are calibrated to the severity of the offence — hours in the stocks for speaking out of turn during theological debate, days for questioning Synod doctrine, weeks or worse for genuine heresy.

## Context

To the Faithful, public penance is not cruelty — it is necessity. Eight centuries of continuous holy war have taught them that mercy untethered from vigilance is an invitation to the Adversary. The punishments are public because the repentance must be public: the community must see the error corrected, the doctrine reaffirmed, the crack sealed. No one stops to look at a man in the stocks — not because they are afraid, but because they agree. This is normal. This is Tuesday.

To modern observers like [[Malcolm Renfield|Sir Malcolm Renfield]], public penance is the most visible evidence that the Crown has confused mercy with weakness. The Faithful do not see what they are doing. Eight centuries of war have taken more than land and lives — they have taken the ability to distinguish between justice and cruelty.
