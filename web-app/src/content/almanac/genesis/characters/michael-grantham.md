# Michael Grantham

## Description

Former Army colonel, built from scar tissue and short sentences. Hawkish, direct, intolerant of ambiguity. Annotates threat assessments with a red pen. His jaw sets in a particular angle when he's found something he doesn't like — a tic his staff have learned to read as the prelude to a difficult meeting.

## Background

Served as an infantry officer in Iraq and Afghanistan before entering politics. Rose to the rank of colonel, then crossed the floor at a moment when his party needed military credibility. Appointed Defence Secretary in the National Unity Government. Brings a soldier's perspective to a Cabinet of civilians — he sees the Faithful not as a diplomatic curiosity but as a potential threat whose capabilities and intentions must be assessed with military rigour.

## Stance on the Anomaly

Grantham argues for military contingency from the start: threat assessment, rules of engagement, and a clear chain of command for whatever follows. He seizes on the Heretic Legion briefing as the primary concern — if Hell is a territorial power on the other side, it could become a threat on this side. He is not wrong, but his hawkishness risks escalating tensions the diplomatic channel is trying to manage.

## Relationships

- [[Stephen Davenport]] — Respects Davenport's decisiveness but suspects the PM's secularism leaves him strategically blind to the nature of the threat.
- [[William Ashford]] — Professional adversary. Grantham sees Ashford's diplomatic caution as dangerously naive when dealing with a theocracy that blesses its ammunition.
- [[Alex Merrick]] — Trusted colleague. Grantham and Merrick share a military vocabulary and an instinct for operational clarity.
