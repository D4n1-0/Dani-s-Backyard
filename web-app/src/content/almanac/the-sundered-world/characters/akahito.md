# Akahito

## Description

Seventeen years old. Tall for his age but carries himself smaller — shoulders slightly hunched, the habit of a boy who learned early not to take up too much space. His hands are perpetually stained with grease from the airfield workshop — a part-time job after school, one that pays better than the state-assigned student tracks and lets him stay close to the engines he understands better than people. He has a quiet voice and a slower way of speaking, as if every word is weighed before release.

## Background

Born in the same Valdran precinct as [[Asahi]] — a precinct so orderly that children learned to count before they learned to read, taught by parents who understood that numbers could save your life. When Asahi's family was selected for transmigration at age ten, Akahito didn't cry. He walked her to the furniture truck, gave her his best skipping stone, and stood on the corner until the truck was a speck in the distance. Then he went home and put her stone on his windowsill where it stayed for six years.

His own transmigration came two years later — a lottery draw no one in his family could have predicted or prevented. The destination was [[Kedros Garrison]], a coastal border town on the eastern frontier. Akahito didn't know, when he boarded the train, that [[Asahi]] had been sent to the same town six years earlier. He found out three days after arriving, when he saw her name on a transmigrant registry in the census office — and spent the next two years looking for her in every face on every street, never quite believing the random indifference of the census lottery could have delivered him to the same shore she'd washed up on.

Now he works part-time at the airfield mechanical workshop after school, repairing biplane engines, learning every engine stroke by heart, and helping newly arrived transmigrant families unpack in the evenings while he waits for the day he might see her again.

## Relationships

- [[Asahi]] — childhood best friend from the precinct, separated by transmigration. He has spent six years looking for her face in crowds. He never stopped.
- Father — a railway clerk who filled out the transmigration forms like everyone else. When the lottery drew their number, he didn't argue. He just packed.
- The airfield mechanics — older men who taught him engines, who don't ask questions, who notice when he goes quiet and know it means he's seen a drone.

## Current Status

Works at the Kedros airfield mechanical workshop. Lives in a transmigrant tenement three streets from the rail depot. The train with the tanks goes past his window every night. Last week, it stopped.
