# Tone & Themes

## Core Tension

Two worlds collide across a gate on the Yellow Sea: the **modern 21st-century geopolitical order** versus the **AOT world** — a cruel, deterministic universe where an oppressed island nation has just learned the entire world wants them dead, and their only deterrent is 600,000 Wall Titans capable of global omnicide.

The modern world must confront a moral universe it has never faced: how do you negotiate with a people who possess WMDs they didn't ask for, a people whose very biology makes them weapons, a people who have suffered genocide rhetoric from every other nation on their planet?

## Themes

- **First Contact as Moral Rorschach** — every modern nation sees the AOT world through its own geopolitical lens. Is Paradis a victim nation deserving protection, or a rogue state harbouring apocalyptic weapons? Is Marley a colonial oppressor or a desperate empire clinging to hegemony?
- **Determinism vs. Agency** — the AOT universe is bound by the Paths, the Vow, the Curse. The modern world introduces an external variable — can it break the cycle, or will it be consumed by it?
- **The View from the Walls** — the Eldians have just learned their world is infinite and hostile. Now a *second* world appears. What does this do to a people already radicalising?
- **Titan Biology as Geopolitical Crisis** — a race that can be weaponised through spinal fluid. A Founder that can erase memories or flatten continents. How does modern international law, human rights, and military doctrine handle that?
- **Inverted Earth, Inverted Morality** — the literal north-south inversion mirrors the moral inversions: the "monsters" inside the walls are victims; the "civilised" Marleyans run concentration camps. The modern world must navigate a hall of mirrors.

## Mood

Bleak, politically dense, philosophically charged. Like Genesis, the tone is grounded military-first-contact — no heroes with capes, just soldiers, diplomats, spooks, and desperate people making impossible choices. The wonder of the gate is immediately swallowed by the horror of what it reveals.
