# Chapter 05 – The Gathering Storm

## Summary

Prime Minister [[Stephen Davenport]]'s policy of cautious secular engagement survives barely twenty-four hours before the international community forces its way into the room. The [[United States]] — briefed via Five Eyes — demands immediate joint military command and a presidential visit to the portal. [[France]], alerted by its own satellite assets, presents a legal challenge: the portal is in international waters under [[UNCLOS]], and no single nation can claim jurisdiction. [[Anna Strauss|Chancellor Strauss]] of [[Germany]] explains that the [[Grundgesetz]] — written in reaction to the last century's catastrophes — has no vocabulary for holy war; any military commitment requires a Bundestag vote that could take weeks and may not pass. She offers scientists and diplomats instead, proposing an [[International Civilian Agency for Portal Affairs]]. An emergency [[NATO Headquarters|NATO]] session in Brussels brings all four positions into collision, producing no resolution. Then a cable arrives from the [[Principality of New Antioch|Office of the Propagation of Virtue]]: *Verify before you venerate. We will send our own inquisitor.* The first crack appears in the united [[Faithful]] front. The [[United Kingdom (Modern)|UK]] has lost control of the narrative, and somewhere to the south, in a corner of a world Davenport cannot see, the [[Iron Sultanate]] is watching.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: [[Northwood Headquarters]] — The Video Call

**[TIME]: Day 5 — 2026-06-22, 05:00 Zulu**
**[POV]: Third Person — [[Stephen Davenport|Davenport]]*

The secure video link to Washington had been established at four in the morning, because the White House had demanded an immediate briefing and Davenport had learned, in fourteen months of coalition government, that you did not keep the President of the [[United States]] waiting.

He stood before the screen in the command conference room at Northwood, still in the same suit he had worn to the COBR meeting the day before. The tie was loose. The coffee was cold. Around him, the permanent military staff moved in the low-lit hush of an operations centre that had not stopped working since the portal opened — analysts refreshing satellite passes, signals intelligence officers annotating transcripts of Faithful communications, a young lieutenant updating the threat board with coloured markers that were not yet standardised because no one had decided what the colour code should be.

The screen flickered and resolved.

[[Robert Cole|The President of the United States]] sat in the Situation Room in Washington, shirt-sleeved, tie loosened, the seal of his office visible over his left shoulder. His National Security Advisor hovered at the edge of the frame — a thin woman with a clipboard and the expression of someone who had been briefed at three in the morning and had not liked what she heard. Around them, the Situation Room displayed its own threat boards, its own satellite passes, its own annotations — and Davenport understood, with a cold clarity, that the Americans had been running their own analysis since before the [[United Kingdom (Modern)|UK]] had even answered their first query.

"Mr. Prime Minister," the President said. His voice was flat, mid-Atlantic, the voice of a man who had spent his career in boardrooms and had adapted the register for war rooms. "I'm going to be direct. My intelligence community has been watching your 'NATO exercise' story disintegrate for the past seventy-two hours. We have our own satellite imagery of [[The Anomaly]]. We have our own signals intercepts of the radio traffic in and around your cordon. We know about the delegation. We know about the crossing. And we know that your government has been running this as a unilateral operation without the involvement of your closest ally."

"The initial contact was made by UK assets," Davenport said. "We did what any sovereign state would do when faced with an unknown phenomenon in its vicinity — we secured the area and assessed the situation before bringing in partners."

"Your 'vicinity' is the [[Atlantic Ocean|North Atlantic]]," the President said. "That's not a UK lake. It's an international waterway. And the phenomenon you're sitting on has implications for every nation on this planet — including mine."

Davenport did not take the bait. "We were not excluding our allies. We were establishing facts before we could brief them coherently. You have that briefing now."

"I have fragments," the President said. "I have a cover story that collapsed before it was announced. I have a UK government that has been in contact with a parallel civilisation for five days and has not once picked up the secure phone to the White House. And I have a sovereign state — the [[Crown of England]] — that appears to believe its medieval monarchy has a claim on modern British territory." He leaned forward. "What I don't have is a clear picture of your intentions, your command structure, or your endgame."

Davenport was silent for a moment. The President was not wrong. The UK had delayed, and the delay had cost them the trust of their most important ally. He had known this would happen — had known it the moment [[Hugh Renwick|Renwick]] had advised against bringing the Americans in before the COBR meeting — but knowing it and paying for it were different things.

"Mr. President," he said, "here is what I know. The portal connects our world to a parallel Earth where a variant of human civilisation has been fighting a war with a force it calls the [[Heretic Legion]] for eight centuries. That civilisation is theocratic, medieval in many respects, but armed with materials and technologies that we do not understand. They have extended an invitation for diplomatic contact. I have accepted that invitation. And I have set a policy of cautious secular engagement — treat them as a foreign power, not a divine instrument."

"And if they treat *you* as a divine instrument?" the President said. "What then? [[Peter Chisholm|Your Archbishop]] gave a very interesting interview to the British press — off the record, I'm told, but our embassy in London has good sources. He thinks you might be the crew of the ark, not the cargo. Is that your policy?"

"His Grace the Archbishop speaks for the Church of England," Davenport said carefully, "not for Her Majesty's Government. My policy is secular engagement. No theological entanglement."

"But you don't control the theology," the President said. "No one does. That's the problem. This — this thing — it's not going to stay in your waters. It's not going to stay in your control. It's going to be the defining event of the century, and every nation on Earth is going to want a piece of it. You can't manage that alone."

"I'm not asking to manage it alone. I'm asking for partnership — not takeover."

The President's jaw tightened. "I want a joint military command. I want my people on the ground at your [[Northwood Headquarters|Northwood]] facility. And I want to see this portal myself — in person, within the next two weeks."

Davenport had expected this. "A presidential visit to an active interdimensional contact zone would be unprecedented. The security implications alone —"

"Are my problem, not yours."

"Mr. President, if you arrive at the portal unilaterally, you will signal to the [[Crown of England|Faithful Crown]] that the UK does not speak for its own territory. You will undercut the diplomatic framework we are building before it has even been established. And you will invite every other nation with satellite capability to demand equal access."

The President's eyes narrowed. "So what do you propose?"

"A [[NATO Headquarters|NATO]] framework," Davenport said. "A standing council — the North Atlantic Council meets in emergency session, establishes a multinational coordination body, and the UK retains operational command of the contact zone under that mandate. Your people are embedded at every level. Your President makes a state visit — but through NATO, not around it. Through the alliance, not against it."

The President was silent for a long moment. His National Security Advisor leaned in and whispered something Davenport could not hear. The President nodded once.

"I'll take that to my team," he said. "But let me be clear, Stephen. If I feel — even for a moment — that the UK is using the NATO framework to delay, to obstruct, or to preserve a unilateral advantage that no longer exists, I will act independently. I have that authority, and I will use it."

"You've made that clear."

"I have. One more thing." The President's voice shifted — less official, more human. "I've read the reports on the [[Heretic Legion]]. If half of what your intelligence assessment describes is accurate, we are not prepared for what might come through that portal. None of us are. So when I say I want my people on the ground, it's not because I don't trust you. It's because I'm afraid of what's on the other side."

The screen went dark.

Davenport stood alone in the dim light of the conference room, the afterimage of the President's face lingering on the blank screen. The coffee in his hand had gone cold. He drank it anyway.

### Scene 2: [[Cabinet Office Briefing Rooms|Cabinet Office]] — The French Briefing

**[TIME]: Day 5 — 2026-06-22, 08:00 Zulu**
**[POV]: Third Person — [[Stephen Davenport|Davenport]]*

The call from [[France]] came at eight in the morning, relayed through the Foreign Office from the French embassy in London. Davenport took it in the Cabinet Office, with [[William Ashford|Ashford]] seated across the table and a transcript machine recording every word.

The French Foreign Minister, [[Étienne Morel]], did not waste time on pleasantries. He was a spare, intellectual man — a former professor of international law who had entered politics late and carried the precision of the lecture hall into every conversation.

"Mr. Prime Minister," Morel said, "France is aware of [[The Anomaly]]. We have been aware since before your government communicated with [[NATO Headquarters|NATO]]. Our satellites detected the distortion patterns on the first day. We have our own analysis, our own assessment, and our own concerns."

"We were in the process of —" Davenport began.

"Your process is not the issue," Morel cut in. "The issue is jurisdiction. Our legal advisers have reviewed the position of the Anomaly against the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea. The portal is located approximately one hundred and fifty nautical miles from the nearest landmass — well beyond the twelve-nautical-mile territorial limit of any coastal state. Under [[UNCLOS]], it is in international waters. No single nation can claim sovereignty over it."

Ashford leaned forward. "Foreign Minister, the Anomaly is within the UK's Exclusive Economic Zone —"

"An EEZ confers sovereign rights over natural resources, not sovereignty over the water column itself," Morel said, with the patient precision of a man reciting settled law. "You have rights to the seabed and the fisheries. You do not have the right to exclude other nations from a phenomenon that is neither a resource nor a structure but a — a discontinuity in the fabric of reality. [[France]] does not accept that the [[United Kingdom (Modern)|United Kingdom]] holds unilateral jurisdiction over the portal, nor over the terms of contact with the civilisation on the other side."

"What civilisations does France propose?" Davenport said. "It's a two-sided contact. The [[Crown of England]] approached us, not the United Nations."

"The Crown of England approached the vessel of a NATO member state that happened to be on station," Morel said. "If a French frigate had been first, they would have approached us. The distinction is temporal, not legal. France insists on equal representation in whatever international framework governs future contact."

Davenport exchanged a glance with Ashford. Ashford gave a slight nod — the diplomat's signal for *he's not wrong, but we don't have to concede gracefully*.

"You'll have a seat at the table," Davenport said. "That was never in question. NATO is convening an emergency session. France will be fully represented."

"Good. But I want to be clear about something else." Morel's voice shifted — the precision remained, but something harder surfaced beneath it. "We have read the reports from your intelligence services regarding the '[[The Ark|ark theology]]' developed by the [[Faithful]] delegation. You understand, I hope, what that concept implies."

"Enlighten me."

"It implies that our world — our civilisation — is the *cargo* of a divine vessel. That we exist not by our own right but as specimens preserved for a purpose determined by an external authority. France does not accept that framing. We do not accept that any church — from our world or from another — has the right to define our place in any cosmic narrative. The concept of the ark is, in my view, an assault on human dignity. We are not specimens. We are not the animals in someone else's hold."

Davenport was quiet for a moment. He had expected a legal argument from France. He had not expected the philosophical challenge.

"The UK's policy," he said carefully, "is secular engagement. We are not adopting the ark theology. We are not allowing the Faithful's framing to shape our policy. We are treating them as a foreign power with unusual characteristics."

"Your policy is correct," Morel said. "But it is a holding action. The Faithful's theology will not be contained by diplomatic protocols. France will support your secular framework in the NATO session. But we will also insist on a parallel legal process — an international tribunal, or a special session of the UN General Assembly — to establish the jurisdictional and human rights framework for all future contact."

"Human rights?"

"The right of every human being not to be treated as a divine instrument without their consent," Morel said. "The right of every nation not to have its sovereignty subordinated to a foreign church's eschatology. France is a secular republic. Our [[Laïcité|laïcité]] is not negotiable. If the Faithful's theology implies that our secularism is blindness, then our secularism will push back. Hard."

The call ended. Davenport sat back and looked at Ashford.

"Well?"

"The French position is legally defensible," Ashford said. "[[UNCLOS]] does not cover interdimensional portals, but the default position under international law is that no state has sovereignty over a phenomenon in international waters. They have a point. And Morel's personal intervention suggests the President of France is taking this very seriously."

"More than seriously," Davenport said. "Philosophically. He's not arguing about procedure. He's arguing about meaning."

"He's French. They do that."

Davenport almost smiled. Almost.

### Scene 3: [[Cabinet Office Briefing Rooms|Cabinet Office]] — The German Call

**[TIME]: Day 5 — 2026-06-22, 09:30 Zulu**
**[POV]: Third Person — [[Stephen Davenport|Davenport]]*

The German Chancellor called from Berlin, and her voice was the first one Davenport had heard all day that sounded like it was carrying the weight of the news.

[[Anna Strauss]] was in her second term — a centrist from the centre-right, a lawyer by training, a woman who had built a career on the proposition that [[Germany]]'s post-war constitution was the most carefully constructed framework for liberal democracy in the world. She believed this because she had studied it, defended it, and lived through the years when its guarantees had been tested by terrorism, by migration, by economic crisis. She had never been tested by a portal to Hell.

"Mr. Prime Minister," she said, "I will not pretend that Germany has the answers to this crisis. We do not. But I want you to understand our constraints before the [[NATO Headquarters|NATO]] session, so that you do not mistake constitutional reality for political unwillingness."

"I'm listening."

"You are aware, I assume, of Article 87a of our [[Grundgesetz|Basic Law]]."

"Parliamentary deployment authorisation. Yes."

"Then you understand the first constraint. The Bundeswehr is a parliamentary army. Every deployment — every single deployment — requires a Bundestag mandate that specifies the scope, the duration, the rules of engagement, and the maximum number of troops. This is not a formality. It is the legal foundation of our post-war democracy. We do not send soldiers into combat without a vote."

"And the scenario we face — does it fit any existing mandate?"

Strauss paused. "No. It does not. The existing legal frameworks cover collective defence under NATO, peacekeeping under the UN, humanitarian intervention, and domestic disaster relief. None of them — none of them — covers 'contact with a parallel theocratic civilisation that believes our world is a divine vessel.' Our lawyers have been working on this since your initial briefing. There is no precedent. There is no existing authorisation that can be stretched to cover what you are asking."

"So you need a new mandate."

"I need a new legal framework," Strauss said. "And that requires a Bundestag debate. Which requires committee hearings. Which requires a vote. The process takes weeks under ideal circumstances. These are not ideal circumstances. The opposition will demand hearings. The coalition will demand concessions. The Constitutional Court may be asked to review any deployment for compatibility with the Basic Law. And even if the government wins every procedural battle, the vote itself is not guaranteed."

Davenport was silent, processing. "And if the vote fails?"

"Then Germany cannot contribute military forces to any NATO operation related to the portal. Not because we do not want to. Because our constitution will not allow it."

"There's more, isn't there?"

Strauss was quiet for a moment. "Article 4(3). The right to conscientious objection. It is absolute — no exceptions, no limitations, no circumstances under which a soldier can be compelled to bear arms against their conscience. If the Bundestag authorises a deployment and individual soldiers refuse to participate — because they object to fighting a 'holy war,' because they do not believe the [[Heretic Legion]] is real, because their church tells them this is a sin — the Basic Law protects that refusal. We cannot override it. We cannot punish it. We can only reassign the soldier to non-combat duties."

"You're saying that even if the vote passes —"

"Individual soldiers can still opt out. Yes. And we have no legal mechanism to stop them."

Davenport let the information settle. The German constitution, built on the ashes of the Third Reich, designed to prevent the militarisation of a society that had been militarised to catastrophe, had created a system that could not respond to the kind of threat the portal represented. The very safeguards that made German democracy strong were about to make it strategically paralysed.

"What can Germany offer?" he asked.

"What we have always offered," Strauss said. "Science. Diplomacy. Engineering. We have the best materials scientists in Europe. We have a foreign service that has managed complex multilateral crises for seventy years. We have engineers who can build infrastructure in the most challenging environments on Earth. We cannot send soldiers to fight a war we do not understand, under a constitution that does not recognise the authority we would need to deploy them. But we can send experts. We can send negotiators. We can send every resource we have except the one you need most."

She paused. "And I would like to propose something. An [[International Civilian Agency for Portal Affairs]] — a non-military body, independent of any single government, that manages contact, research, and protocol across the portal. Not a NATO command. Not a UN mission. A civilian agency, staffed by scientists and diplomats and legal experts, with a mandate to understand the portal before anyone decides what to do about it."

Davenport considered this. "And who would control it?"

"Everyone," Strauss said. "And no one. That is the point. The portal does not belong to the [[United Kingdom (Modern)|UK]]. It does not belong to the [[United States|US]]. It belongs to the human species. An international civilian agency — with representation from every nation that wants a seat, with transparency obligations, with a scientific rather than a military mandate — would give us time. Time to understand what we are dealing with before we decide whether to fight it, befriend it, or flee from it."

"It would also slow everything down," Davenport said. "The Americans will not accept a civilian agency. They want command."

"I know," Strauss said. "But command and wisdom are not the same thing. And I am not certain, Mr. Prime Minister, that the world is ready to make wise decisions about this portal. We have not even agreed on what it is. We cannot agree on who should speak to it. How can we possibly agree on what to do with it?"

Davenport had no answer to that. The call ended. He sat in the silence of the Cabinet Office, staring at the notes he had not taken, and felt the weight of a constitution written in a language he did not speak, in a country whose history had prepared it for every catastrophe except this one.

### Scene 4: [[NATO Headquarters|NATO Brussels]] — The Session

**[TIME]: Day 6 — 2026-06-23, 14:00 Zulu**
**[POV]: Third Person — [[Stephen Davenport|Davenport]]*

The [[NATO Headquarters|NATO headquarters in Brussels]] had never seen a session quite like this one.

Davenport had flown in that morning, direct from Northwood, on a government jet that had been hastily converted to a mobile command post. Beside him, [[Michael Grantham|Grantham]] had spent the flight annotating threat assessments with increasing agitation. [[Alex Merrick|General Merrick]] had sat in silence, watching the cloud cover over the Channel, his thousand-yard stare fixed on something that was not the horizon.

The North Atlantic Council chamber was full — thirty-two delegations, interpreters, legal advisers, military attachés, and the Secretary General at the head of the table, a Dutch diplomat who had spent his career managing crises that now seemed laughably ordinary. The screens around the room displayed satellite imagery of the portal, the naval cordon, the estimated positions of Faithful vessels. A map of the [[Atlantic Ocean|Celtic Sea]] glowed in blue and grey, the portal marked in red — a wound in the surface of the world that no one knew how to close.

Davenport spoke first. He gave the briefing he had prepared, stripped of theology, stripped of speculation, focused on the facts as they were known: a portal, a delegation, a parallel civilisation, a diplomatic invitation. He described the [[Crown of England|Faithful Crown]], the [[Heretic Legion]], [[The Ark|the ark theology]] without endorsing it. He described his policy of cautious secular engagement.

When he finished, the session dissolved into the collision he had known was coming.

The US Secretary of State — [[Robert Cole|the President]] had not come in person, which told Davenport something about the limits of American commitment — spoke first. "The [[United States]] believes this is a matter of collective defence under Article 5. An interdimensional threat of unknown capability has appeared within the NATO area of responsibility. We should establish a unified command structure, under a Supreme Allied Commander, with full authority over all military assets in the contact zone."

France's NATO ambassador, [[Jean-Paul Delacroix]], responded before the Secretary of State had finished. "[[France]] does not agree that Article 5 is triggered. There has been no armed attack against any member state. There has been contact — unprecedented contact, yes — but contact is not aggression. And a unified command structure that places US commanders at the head of an operation in European waters is not a decision this council can take without a full legal review of the jurisdictional questions involved."

"What jurisdictional questions?" the US representative demanded.

"The Anomaly is in international waters," Delacroix said. "Under [[UNCLOS]], no single nation — and no single alliance — can claim exclusive authority over it. The North Atlantic Council has no mandate to establish a command structure over a phenomenon that lies outside the territory of any member state. France proposes a working group on jurisdiction, to report within thirty days."

"Thirty days is not acceptable," Grantham said, his voice cutting across the chamber. "We have a delegation from a theocratic military power camped at Northwood, a [[Heretic Legion]] that may or may not be able to cross the portal, and a world on the other side that has been fighting a literal war with Hell for eight centuries. We do not have thirty days to form a working group."

"Then what do you propose, Mr. Defence Secretary?" Delacroix asked coolly. "A unilateral [[United Kingdom (Modern)|UK]] command under the cover of a NATO flag? Because that is what this sounds like."

The German representative, a Foreign Office official who had been authorised to speak for [[Anna Strauss|Chancellor Strauss]], raised a hand. "[[Germany]] supports the principle of international coordination. We are not in a position to commit to any military structure at this time. Our constitutional framework requires —"

"We know what your constitution requires," the US Secretary of State interrupted. "We've read your brief. But this is a crisis. Governments find ways to act in a crisis."

"Democracies find ways to deliberate in a crisis," the German representative said, with the quiet dignity of a man who had been interrupted before. "That is what distinguishes them from the autocracies on the other side of the portal. Germany will not bypass its constitution. We will contribute scientific and diplomatic resources to any international framework. We will not contribute troops."

The session continued for two more hours. Positions circulated. Compromises were proposed and collapsed. The Secretary General suggested a phased approach — a coordination committee first, a command structure later, a full council review in sixty days. No one was satisfied. No one had the votes to force a decision.

In the end, they agreed on a statement. It said nothing. It committed no one to anything. It was the diplomatic equivalent of a pause — a breath taken before a plunge that no one was ready to take.

Davenport walked out of the chamber with Grantham and Merrick, the corridors of NATO HQ bright and sterile around them, the flags of thirty-two nations hanging silent in the atrium.

"That was a disaster," Grantham said.

"That was diplomacy," Davenport said.

"Same thing."

Merrick said nothing. But his silence, in its own way, was the loudest comment of all.

### Scene 5: [[Northwood Headquarters]] — The Cable

**[TIME]: Day 6 — 2026-06-23, 18:00 Zulu**
**[POV]: Third Person — [[Stephen Davenport|Davenport]]*

The cable arrived at Northwood six hours after the NATO session ended, transmitted through a channel the [[Faithful]] delegation controlled and the [[United Kingdom (Modern)|UK]] could not intercept.

Davenport was still in transit back from Brussels when [[William Ashford|Ashford]] called his mobile — a breach of protocol that told him everything he needed to know about the message's importance.

"Prime Minister, you need to come to Northwood directly. The Faithful delegation has received a communication from their command structure. It changes things."

Davenport arrived at Northwood at dusk. The operations centre was humming with the quiet urgency of a watch change, the shift briefings conducted in low voices against the hum of servers and cooling systems. Ashford met him in the corridor, a sheaf of paper in his hand, his face unreadable.

"The delegation received it about an hour ago. It came through a communication system we don't understand — some kind of directed energy transmission, according to our signals people. We can't intercept it. We can't block it. We can only receive what they choose to forward to us."

He handed Davenport the paper.

It was a diplomatic cable, formal in structure, bearing the seal of the [[Office of the Propagation of Virtue]] — a book and a sword crossed behind a crowned flame. The recipient was Captain [[Alistair Pembroke]], commanding officer of HMS *Herald*, with a copy to [[Father Beauchamp]]. The sender was the Office's director of theological assessment, [[Principality of New Antioch|New Antioch]].

The text was brief.

*The Synod's declaration has been received. The ark hypothesis has been noted. It is the view of this Office that the Crown's theological assessment may be premature. The souls of the new world remain unexamined by any authority other than a single Rite performed under field conditions. The Office directs you to suspend all theological conclusions pending a formal inquisition.*

*Verify before you venerate. We will send our own inquisitor.*

Davenport read it twice.

"Who is the inquisitor?"

Ashford shook his head. "The cable doesn't say. It doesn't even give a name. Just that one is coming."

"And the [[Crown of England|Faithful Crown]]? [[King Robert the Longsword|King Robert]]? Does he know [[Principality of New Antioch|New Antioch]] is overruling his Synod?"

"We don't know. Pembroke is — uncomfortable. I spoke to him after the message was translated. He said the Office of the Propagation of Virtue has its own mandate. It answers to the Patriarch of New Antioch, not directly to the Crown. And the Patriarch, apparently, has a reputation for theological independence."

"The first crack," Davenport said.

"Sir?"

"The [[Faithful]]. They look like a united front — a theocratic monolith, King Robert speaks, the Synod agrees, the Church obeys. But they're not. New Antioch has its own authority. Its own theology. Its own scepticism. And its own agenda." He looked at the cable again. "Verify before you venerate. They don't trust their own King's assessment. They want to see for themselves."

"Is that good for us or bad?"

Davenport considered the question. "It's good if it slows down their theological enthusiasm. It's bad if the inquisitor arrives with a mandate to challenge everything the delegation has built. We have established a relationship with Pembroke and [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]]. We don't know who the inquisitor is, or what they believe, or whether they will see our secularism as a barrier or a provocation."

He looked at the seal of the Office of the Propagation of Virtue — the book and the sword, the crowned flame.

"Tell me we have someone in our intelligence services who understands Faithful ecclesiastical politics."

"We have [[Hugh Renwick|Renwick]]," Ashford said. "And Renwick has a team. But they've been working on this for less than a week. They're writing the manual as they go."

"Then they'd better write faster."

### Scene 6: [[10 Downing Street]] — Losing Control

**[TIME]: Day 6 — 2026-06-23, 22:00 Zulu**
**[POV]: Third Person — [[Stephen Davenport|Davenport]]*

Davenport returned to Downing Street late. The house was quiet — the last of the staff had gone home, the duty clerk had been dismissed, and only the policeman at the door and the overnight secretary in the outer office remained. The corridors of the upper floors were dark, the portraits of former prime ministers watching him pass with the placid indifference of the dead.

He sat in the private study, a small room on the first floor that had been his refuge since the coalition government was formed. The desk was stacked with red boxes. The intelligence digest lay unopened. A map of the [[Atlantic Ocean|North Atlantic]] was pinned to the wall — the portal's position marked in red, the naval cordon in blue, the exclusion zone in a grey that had been chosen because no one could agree on what colour meant "do not cross, we have no idea what we're doing."

He did not open the boxes. He did not reach for the digest. He sat in the leather chair and let the silence of the empty house settle around him, and tried to understand how much control he had lost in the past forty-eight hours.

The [[United States|Americans]] wanted command. They had not taken it yet — they had accepted the [[NATO Headquarters|NATO]] framework as a compromise — but Davenport had seen [[Robert Cole|the President]]'s face on the screen. The US would not wait forever. If the NATO process stalled, if the [[United Kingdom (Modern)|UK]] seemed weak, if the [[Crown of England|Faithful Crown]] made a move that the US interpreted as a threat, the Americans would act. They had the capability. They had the will. And they had a President who believed — perhaps correctly — that the UK could not manage this alone.

The [[France|French]] wanted equal representation. They would get it, because Davenport could not stop them, but the French position was not simply about procedure. [[Étienne Morel|Morel]]'s horror at [[The Ark|the ark]] concept — *an assault on human dignity* — was real. France would fight the theological framing of the portal not as a diplomatic manoeuvre but as a matter of principle. And [[Laïcité|laïcité]], the French doctrine of secularism, was more absolute than anything the UK had ever attempted. It would bring France into direct conflict with the Faithful Crown. That conflict was coming. Davenport could see it on the horizon like a storm front.

[[Germany]] could not move. The most powerful economy in Europe, the most capable military on the continent, and its hands were tied by a constitution designed to prevent the very thing the crisis required. [[Anna Strauss|Strauss]] had been honest about it — devastatingly honest — but honesty did not change the facts. Germany would watch from the sidelines, offering scientists and lawyers, while the nations that could act made decisions that would affect every human being on the planet.

And the [[Faithful]] themselves were fracturing. The cable from [[Principality of New Antioch|New Antioch]] was a warning that the united theocratic front was an illusion. The [[Crown of England]] had its own interests. The [[Principality of New Antioch]] had its own authority. The [[Office of the Propagation of Virtue]] had its own agenda. And the Sultanate — the [[Iron Sultanate]], the third great power of the TC world, about which the UK knew almost nothing — was watching.

Davenport picked up the intelligence digest and opened it to the page flagged with a yellow marker.

His eye caught a brief notation in the margins of the Faithful delegation's communications log — a note from a signals analyst, flagged for attention:

*Courier vessel despatched from the Crown's fleet anchorage, 1914-09-18 TC (Day 5). Bearing south-southeast. Destination logged as "the Southern Reaches." CoE command has not briefed our liaison on the purpose of this movement.*

The Southern Reaches. Davenport did not know what that meant. Neither, he suspected, did the signals analyst. But he knew a data point when he saw one. The Faithful were communicating with someone — some power, some ally, some broker — that the UK had not yet identified.

He put the digest down.

The narrative was no longer his. The [[United Kingdom (Modern)|UK]] had lost the monopoly on information the moment the Americans picked up the satellite signature. It had lost the monopoly on access the moment France demanded a seat at the table. It had lost the monopoly on framing the moment New Antioch sent its cable. The story of the portal was now a story the world would write together — and the world was divided, fractious, and as unprepared as he was.

He looked at the map. The red mark in the [[Atlantic Ocean|Celtic Sea]]. The blue cordon. The grey exclusion zone. The empty ocean to the south, where a Faithful courier ship was sailing toward a destination he could not name.

*We have not even asked what they want*, he thought. *We have only asked who gets to decide.*

The thought stayed with him as the night deepened, as the city outside carried on in its ordinary rhythm — taxis and buses and late-night revelers and all the small, stubborn routines of a civilisation that did not yet know how profoundly it had been displaced. [[The Ark|The ark]] was open. The waters were rising. And the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom sat alone in a quiet room, watching a map he could no longer read, waiting for news he could no longer control.

## Key Decisions

- The US President demands joint military command and a presidential visit; Davenport concedes a NATO framework as a compromise, retaining UK operational control in name but ceding de facto partnership.
- France presents a legal challenge under UNCLOS: the portal is in international waters, no single nation holds jurisdiction. France insists on equal representation and a parallel legal process, including a human rights framing of the ark theology.
- [[Anna Strauss|Chancellor Strauss]] informs Davenport that Germany cannot constitutionally commit troops — Article 87a (parliamentary deployment authorisation) and Article 4(3) (absolute conscientious objection) create a legal barrier that would take weeks or months to resolve. She offers scientific and diplomatic support and proposes an [[International Civilian Agency for Portal Affairs]].
- The NATO session produces no substantive agreement — only a statement of continued consultation. The four national positions (US command, French legalism, German paralysis, UK control) remain in collision.
- The [[Office of the Propagation of Virtue]] in New Antioch sends a cable to the Faithful delegation: "Verify before you venerate. We will send our own inquisitor." This asserts New Antioch's theological independence from the Crown and fractures the united Faithful front.
- Davenport realises the UK has lost control of the narrative — the information monopoly, the access monopoly, and the framing monopoly have all been broken within forty-eight hours.
- A Faithful courier vessel has been despatched to "the Southern Reaches" — the first hint of wider TC-world diplomacy involving the [[Iron Sultanate]].
