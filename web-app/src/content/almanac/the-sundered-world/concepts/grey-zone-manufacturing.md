# Grey-Zone Manufacturing

## Summary

Grey-Zone Manufacturing is the term for industrial and military production that technically complies with the letter of the [[Kaelen Mandate]] while violating its spirit — state-owned factories dispersed across nominally independent homesteader collectives, mercenary companies that swell army counts without triggering caps, and dual-use technologies that edge toward forbidden capabilities without crossing the line.

## Details

### Common Exploits

- **Dispersed Production:** The [[Iron Concordat]] maintains state-owned arms factories across "independent" homesteader collectives. Each collective is legally a separate entity producing materiel for its own "defence," but the output flows to the Concordat's army. Kaelen has not intervened — the legal fiction holds, barely.
- **Mercenary Loophole:** Free Companies — stateless mercenary units — can hire out to belligerents without counting against the 250,000 troop cap. The [[Free Cities of the Amber Coast|Amber League]]'s combined city guards technically form a 300,000-strong force, but each city is a sovereign entity. This fiction has been tolerated for over a century, though no one knows why.
- **Dual-Use Airships:** The [[Technate of Vorr]]'s "stratospheric meteorological airship" programme is officially civilian — weather research at extreme altitudes. Its ability to loiter near [[Silent Chorus]] drone altitudes is a "coincidence."
- **Microwave Telegraphy:** Vorr's experimental microwave relay network operates at frequencies just below radar bands — technically not radar, but the distinction would be academic if Kaelen chose to contest it.
- **Monocoque Biplanes:** Vorran fighters use metal monocoque fuselages — a single structural shell, technically a monoplane body — but retain biplane wings. The Mandate bans monoplanes; the fuselage is not a "plane." This argument has not yet been tested.

### The Ratchet Effect

Each successful grey-zone exploit raises the stakes. The Concordat's dispersed factories worked for a decade, so they built more. Vorr's microwave network functioned for five years, so they expanded it. Each nation watches others push boundaries and feels pressure to match them — a slow ratchet toward the moment someone crosses an invisible line and triggers a response.

### The Observers' Silence

The most unnerving aspect of grey-zone manufacturing is Kaelen's selective enforcement. Some violations — [[The Sunken Kingdom of Myrr|Myrr]]'s submarines — are met with overwhelming force. Others — the Amber League's combined guards — are tolerated. No one knows the criteria. This uncertainty is itself a form of control: it makes every grey-zone decision a gamble with unknown odds.

## Context

Grey-zone manufacturing is the engine of the Sundered World's military economy and its greatest source of anxiety. The factions are not merely competing with each other — they are competing to see who can push closest to the line without crossing it, and no one knows where the line is.
