# Alex Merrick

## Description

General, Chief of the Defence Staff. Career soldier, stiff-backed, late fifties. Commanded in Afghanistan and Iraq. Has the thousand-yard stare of a man who learned, through long and bitter experience, that the first casualty of every briefing is clarity. Speaks with a flat, uninflected tone — a weather report, not a speech.

## Background

Rose through the British Army over three decades of active service. Known for operational pragmatism and an allergy to political spin. Appointed CDS on the strength of a reputation for telling prime ministers what they needed to hear, not what they wanted to hear. He has read Captain [[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]]'s after-action reports and the intelligence assessments of Faithful military capability. What he has found disturbs him — not because it is unbelievable, but because it suggests a category of threat his forces are not equipped to counter.

## Stance on the Anomaly

Merrick's position is purely operational: the Anomaly represents an unsecured border with a power whose capabilities and intentions are unknown. He does not ask whether Hell is real — he asks what resources and rules of engagement he will have if it turns out to be. His demands are practical: a clear chain of command, unambiguous rules of engagement, and contingency plans for Heretic incursion. He is not a hawk or a dove; he is a soldier who has been handed a problem and needs the tools to solve it.
