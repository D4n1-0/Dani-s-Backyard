# Chapter 1 – The Gate

## Summary

On a quiet Saturday evening, the [[Korea Coast Guard]] medium patrol vessel [[KCG 506]] is conducting a routine fisheries patrol on the [[Yellow Sea]]. A watch officer spots an impossible radar return — a perfect rectangle on the sea. The vessel closes to investigate and discovers [[The Gate]]: a hard vertical seam 500 metres wide and 200 metres tall, half-submerged, connecting modern Earth to the inverted ocean of the Attack on Titan world. Both sides are night, but the stars do not match. Before [[Park Do-hyun|Lieutenant Park Do-hyun]] can process what he is seeing, a [[China Coast Guard]] cutter — [[Haijing 2306]] — arrives on the scene, commanded by [[Chen Shaoqiang|Senior Captain Chen Shaoqiang]]. A tense standoff ensues, with neither officer speaking the other's language and neither possessing the authority to resolve what they have found. As dawn breaks over the Yellow Sea, the first living thing crosses the threshold.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: [[KCG 506]] — [[Yellow Sea]] — 21:47 KST

The sea was flat and black and utterly uninteresting. It had been uninteresting for three days, which was the standard duration of a fisheries patrol out of Incheon, and [[Park Do-hyun|Lieutenant Park Do-hyun]] had learned to accept this as the natural condition of his career. He was thirty-one years old, a *Gyeongwi* in the [[Korea Coast Guard]], and he had spent more hours staring at featureless water than he had spent asleep in his own bed.

The [[KCG 506]] was a Taegeuk-class medium patrol vessel — five hundred tons, twenty-eight crew, a single 20mm Sea Vulcan mounted forward that had never been fired in anger. Tonight she was running a grid pattern at eight knots, her searchlight sweeping the calm June sea, looking for Chinese fishing boats operating without licences. There were always Chinese fishing boats. There was always something.

"Sir."

The voice belonged to Senior Petty Officer [[Kang Min-jun]], the watch officer. He was standing at the bridge radar repeater, one hand on the console, his face half-lit by the amber glow of the screen.

[[Park Do-hyun|Park]] looked up from his coffee. "What?"

"I don't know, sir."

That was not a phrase [[Park Do-hyun|Park]] heard often from [[Kang Min-jun]]. He crossed the bridge in three strides and looked at the radar display. At the edge of the sweep, bearing 0-4-7 relative, range approximately fourteen nautical miles, sat a return that should not exist. It was too large for a vessel. Too regular. A clean vertical line bisecting the sea, as though someone had drawn a wall on the ocean with a ruler.

"What is that?" [[Park Do-hyun|Park]] asked.

"I thought it was sea clutter at first, sir. But it's not moving. And the shape..." [[Kang Min-jun]] zoomed the display. The return sharpened. A perfect rectangle. Five hundred metres across. "Sir, that's not a ship."

[[Park Do-hyun|Park]] stared at the screen. Then he looked up, through the bridge windows, out across the black water.

He studied the water in the direction of the return. Nothing obvious — no lights, no smoke, no vessel. But the mist on the horizon was wrong. Thicker than it should be, rising from a sea that had been calm all evening, as though something was warming the water from below.

"Helm," [[Park Do-hyun|Park]] said. "Come to course 0-4-7. All ahead full."

### Scene 2: [[The Gate Threshold]] — 22:14 KST

They closed the distance in twenty-seven minutes. No one spoke.

As [[KCG 506]] drew within two nautical miles, the mist parted around a seam that did not belong in any maritime manual [[Park Do-hyun|Park]] had ever read. The rectangle was vast. Five hundred metres across, two hundred high. Half of it extended below the waterline — the crews could see the cross-section of an alien ocean through the submerged portion, fish they did not recognise swimming in water of a different colour. The other half rose into a night sky that was subtly, terribly wrong.

The boundary between the two worlds was a hard seam, a knife-cut in reality, as though God had sliced open the sea and stitched something else into the wound. On the far side, the same level of darkness — but different stars. Unfamiliar constellations hung where Cygnus and Vega should be. The water on the other side moved with a different swell, a different rhythm. Mist coiled along the cut where the two oceans met — warm Yellow Sea water bleeding into the cold southern Indian Ocean — and the crew breathed air that smelled like a continent none of them had ever visited.

The two oceans had already begun to mix. Where the Yellow Sea met the cold southern Indian Ocean water, a heavy mist coiled along the seam — salty and unfamiliar, carrying an organic tang that smelled like nothing any of them had ever breathed.

[[Park Do-hyun|Park]] stood on the port bridge wing, one hand gripping the rail, the other holding binoculars he had forgotten to lower. Behind him, on the bridge, the radio crackled with Incheon trying to make sense of his report. He had told them *I am looking at something I cannot explain* and then described a doorway between worlds, and the officer on the other end had gone silent for eleven seconds before asking him to repeat his position.

"Sir." [[Kang Min-jun]] was beside him. "What do we do?"

[[Park Do-hyun|Park]] lowered the binoculars at last. His hands were steady. His voice was not.

"We make sure no one goes in," he said. "Until someone tells us otherwise."

### Scene 3: [[Haijing 2306]] — [[Yellow Sea]] — 22:31 CST

Senior Captain [[Chen Shaoqiang]] had been at sea for four days. The [[Haijing 2306]] was a 1,500-ton cutter operating out of Qingdao, a Shucha II-class vessel of the [[China Coast Guard]]'s North Sea Bureau. She carried a crew of forty-two, a twin 37mm forward mount, and a standing mission to assert Chinese sovereignty over the waters Beijing claimed but Seoul disputed.

Tonight's patrol had been uneventful — a South Korean fisheries vessel that had drifted too far south, a Taiwanese freighter that had filed the wrong paperwork. [[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]] had handled both with the practiced indifference of a man who had been doing this for nearly two decades. He was forty-three, a *Shangxiao* in the People's Armed Police, and he had learned long ago that the Yellow Sea was a board game where no one ever won and the pieces never stopped moving.

The call came from his sensor operator at 22:31.

"Captain. Unknown contact bearing 1-9-2. Visual anomaly."

[[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]] stepped to the bridge windows and saw it immediately — a dark rectangle cut into the night, fourteen miles to the south. The seam was visible only as a place where the stars stopped and the mist thickened, an absence shaped like a wound. Perhaps the angle had changed. Perhaps the light was simply stranger than anything in his experience had prepared him to parse.

He did not ask what it was. He asked: "Who else is there?"

"A South Korean patrol vessel, Captain. Small — five hundred tons. They arrived approximately forty minutes ago. They appear to be holding position outside visual range of the anomaly."

[[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]] allowed himself ten seconds of silence. That was all he would permit. Then he straightened his uniform and gave the order.

"Take us in. Battle stations. Do not arm weapons."

His helmsman hesitated. "Sir — the anomaly — "

"I know what I saw," [[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]] said. "I am not going to be the officer who lets the Koreans claim whatever that is. Move."

### Scene 4: [[The Gate Threshold]] — Standoff — 23:02 KST / 22:02 CST

[[Haijing 2306]] arrived with her running lights dark and her silhouette outlined against the cold luminance of the distant stars. She was larger than [[KCG 506]] — nearly three times the displacement — and she cut her approach to 1,500 metres before killing her engines and drifting to station directly opposite the Korean vessel, the anomaly between them like a burning scar on the water.

The radio call came on Channel 16, VHF maritime distress — the channel everyone monitored.

*"ROK Coast Guard vessel, ROK Coast Guard vessel. This is China Coast Guard Haijing 2306. You are operating in waters claimed by the People's Republic of China. State your intentions."*

The voice was Mandarin. Flawless, clipped, a Beijing accent. [[Park Do-hyun|Park]] understood perhaps one word in five, but the tone was unmistakable.

He keyed his own radio.

"China Coast Guard vessel, this is Republic of Korea Coast Guard KCG 506." He spoke in Korean, knowing they would not understand, knowing the protocols demanded he try. "You are approaching a secured area. We request you maintain distance of two nautical miles. Repeat. Maintain distance."

Silence on the channel. Then:

*"I do not understand. Repeat in English."*

[[Park Do-hyun|Park]] switched languages. His English was functional — three years of Maritime English at the Academy, half a dozen joint exercises with the Japanese Coast Guard.

"Haijing 2306. This is KCG 506. You are approaching — " he hesitated, because no protocol existed for this, " — you are approaching an anomalous contact of unknown origin. For safety of navigation. Request you maintain distance."

A new voice came on the channel, speaking English with a heavier accent than the translator's Mandarin. It was [[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]] himself.

*"KCG 506. This is Captain Chen Shaoqiang, Haijing 2306. I am looking at the same thing you are looking at. I do not know what it is. I do not think you know either. We are both here now. Let us not make this worse than it already is."*

[[Park Do-hyun|Park]] looked across the water at the Chinese cutter, and for one moment the absurdity of it all — two men, two ships, two rival nations, standing guard over a hole in the universe — struck him with the force of something physical.

He keyed the radio.

"Captain Chen. I agree. Let us not make it worse."

### Scene 5: [[The Gate Threshold]] — The Long Night — 23:45–05:30 KST

The hours passed in silence.

Neither vessel moved. Neither vessel stood down. The crews rotated watch stations in shifts, the off-duty men crowding the weather decks and bridge wings, staring at the impossible thing. Some prayed. Some took photographs with personal phones that would never reach anyone until they were back in port. Some simply watched, their mouths slightly open, the way people watch a house fire from a safe distance.

[[The Gate]] did not pulse. It did not hum. It simply *was* — a clean wound in the fabric of the world, indifferent to the forty-one uniformed South Koreans and forty-two uniformed Chinese who had been assigned to witness it.

The two oceans continued to mix. The mist along the seam grew thicker as the night wore on — warm Yellow Sea water surrendering its heat to the polar southern Indian Ocean bleeding through from the other side. A slick of something — sediment, microplastics, the chemical signature of the modern world — was already spreading across the AOT-side water, carried by slow currents that neither ocean had ever shared before.

On [[KCG 506]]'s bridge, [[Park Do-hyun|Park]] received a single encrypted transmission from Incheon at 01:14:

**HOLD POSITION. DO NOT ENGAGE. DO NOT APPROACH THE ANOMALY. AWAIT INSTRUCTIONS.**

On [[Haijing 2306]]'s bridge, [[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]] received an identical transmission from Qingdao at 01:17, phrased differently but meaning the same thing.

Both men read their orders. Both men looked across the water at each other's ships. Both men understood, without speaking, that their governments had no idea what to do.

### Scene 6: [[The Gate Threshold]] — Dawn — 05:47 KST

The sky over the Yellow Sea began to pale in the east. The first grey wash of dawn bled into the blackness, turning the horizon to charcoal, then slate, then the thin blue of a June morning. On the AOT side, the same dawn was breaking — an identical grey pallor creeping over the southern Indian Ocean. The seam between them, invisible at midnight, now caught the light: a faint silver line where two worlds shared the same sunrise.

[[Park Do-hyun|Park]] had not slept. He stood on the bridge wing with a fresh cup of coffee that had gone cold forty minutes ago, watching the light shift across the seam. The mist was thinning. The mixing zone had widened — he could see the temperature differential now on the thermal camera feed, a spreading bloom of warm Yellow Sea water fanning out into the AOT ocean like an oil spill.

That was when he saw it.

A single seabird — large, white-bodied, with a wingspan that had to be three metres across — glided out of the AOT-side sky and crossed the threshold. It did not hesitate. It did not slow. It simply flew from one world into the other, and when it hit the Yellow Sea air it shuddered — a visible convulsion — banked hard to starboard, and disappeared into the mist on the Earth side.

A wandering albatross. *Diomedea exulans*. Native to the southern oceans. A bird no one on the Yellow Sea had ever seen.

[[Park Do-hyun|Park]] lowered his binoculars.

On [[Haijing 2306]], [[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]] had seen it too. He stood on his own bridge wing, twenty-two hundred metres away, and watched the white bird vanish into the grey morning. He did not speak. There was nothing to say.

The world had just become larger, and smaller, in the same breath.

## Key Decisions

- Lieutenant [[Park Do-hyun]] orders [[KCG 506]] to hold position and not approach the Gate — establishing the South Korean posture as cautious containment
- Senior Captain [[Chen Shaoqiang]] orders [[Haijing 2306]] to battle stations but does not arm weapons — asserting Chinese presence without escalating to violence
- Both officers receive identical hold-and-wait orders from their respective commands
- The standoff ends the chapter unresolved — the first diplomatic crisis of the Gate era
