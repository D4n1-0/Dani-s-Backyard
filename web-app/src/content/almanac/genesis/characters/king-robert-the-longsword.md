# King Robert the Longsword

## Description
Sovereign of the Faithful Crown of England, holder of the throne that has been contested between Heaven and Hell for eight centuries. His full regnal style runs several lines — "By the Grace of God and the Victory of the Cross, Defender of the Faithful, Lord of the White Cliffs" — but he signs simply "Robert Rex." A warrior-king in the tradition of his crusader ancestors, ruling from the Palace of Whitehall in the Trench Crusade timeline's London. His most public act of the Genesis scenario is the Royal Decree of Hospitality, declaring the modern world "an instrument of Providence."

## Background
King Robert has reigned through decades of holy war. He has commanded fleets from the bridge of a dreadnought, knelt before the Patriarch of New Antioch, and signed declarations of war against Heretic states with a sword laid across the parchment. He is not a theologian, but he understands power — and he understands that the appearance of an untouched, uncorrupted world is either the greatest gift Providence has ever given the Faithful, or the most dangerous trap.

## Personality
- Devout but calculating. His faith is genuine; his use of faith is deliberate.
- Governs through a balance of Crown authority, Synod consensus, and military necessity.
- His decree of hospitality toward the modern world was genuine — but it was also strategic. He is claiming the modern world as a divine instrument before anyone else can claim it for different purposes.
- [[Caius Blackwood|Inquisitor-Admiral Blackwood]] suspects the King is more politically astute than theologically scrupulous.

## Key Act
The Royal Decree of Hospitality — a beautifully written proclamation ordering full welcome to the strangers, declaring them "lost kin" and "an instrument of Providence." The decree is simultaneously: (a) a genuine act of Christian charity, (b) a strategic appropriation of the modern world's legitimacy, and (c) a theological time bomb, because "instrument of Providence" is a phrase that can justify almost anything.

## Relationships
- [[Caius Blackwood]] — his Inquisitor-Admiral. Trusts Blackwood's military judgement but is aware the Admiral has reservations about the theological direction.
- [[Father Beauchamp]] — has read Beauchamp's ark theology. Has not yet publicly endorsed it, but has not rejected it either.
- [[Alistair Pembroke]] — a loyal captain, known to the King through despatches.

## Current Status
On the throne in London (TC), having issued the decree of hospitality. Awaiting the arrival of the modern embassy. The theological implications of the ark doctrine are being debated in the Synod, and Robert is watching — waiting to see which way the wind of Providence blows.
