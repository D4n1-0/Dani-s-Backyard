# Deus Absconditus

## Summary
The theological concept of the "hidden God" — the idea that God's silence, His apparent absence from visible affairs, is not a failure of His power but an expression of His nature. In modern Earth, this is the dominant theological framework: faith requires no proof because proof would negate the freedom to choose.

## Details

The term originates in Scripture — "Truly, you are a God who hides yourself" (Isaiah 45:15) — and was developed in Christian theology by thinkers including Martin Luther and Pascal. In the modern world of the Genesis scenario, it is the default assumption of every believer: God does not intervene visibly. Saints do not appear. Relics are symbols, not weapons. The supernatural is a matter of private belief, not public record.

[[Daniel Foster|Reverend Foster]] articulates the concept to [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] aboard *[[HMS Dragon]]*: the Incarnation, the Cross, and the Resurrection are sufficient revelation. Everything else is "special effects." The silence of God is the space in which genuine faith can exist.

## Context

The contrast with Trench Crusade Earth is absolute. In the TC world, God and the Adversary are both visibly, tangibly present — miracles happen, saints appear, Hell is territorial. The [[Faithful]] see modern Earth's silent God as an absence; modern believers see the TC world's visible God as a coercion.

[[Peter Chisholm|Archbishop Chisholm]] later reframes this contrast: perhaps modern Earth's silence is not absence but design. Perhaps the "hidden God" created a world where faith *must* be chosen freely, without the pressure of visible proof — and that this freedom is precisely what the TC world has lost.
