# Valdran Provision System

## Summary

The Valdran economy is a socialist system with limited capitalist elements — a practical adaptation to the constraints of [[The Census|transmigration]] and the [[Kaelen Mandate|Mandate's]] technological ceilings. Since families are relocated every few years and no long-range banking network exists, wealth cannot be accumulated or transported. The state provides housing, employment, and subsistence as a matter of survival — not ideology, but logistics.

## Details

### Why Socialism Works

Three practical factors make state provision the only viable economic model:

1. **Frequent relocation.** A family can be transmigrated at any time. A Valdran who saves money in one town cannot access it in another — there is no inter-city banking network, no telegraph-based credit system, and no way to transport significant wealth across the borders between precincts and garrison towns. Cash is useless beyond the reach of a single local ledger.

2. **No wealth accumulation.** The only wealth that follows a transmigrant family is whatever fits in a suitcase. Standard-issue furniture, kitchen tools, and clothing are provided at the destination. There is no inheritance to speak of, no generational property, no bank accounts that survive relocation.

3. **Labour continuity.** The census bureau knows exactly who you are and what you do — it placed you in your job, it trained you for your specialization, it tracks every citizen from birth to death. If you're an airfield mechanic, you'll be an airfield mechanic in the next town too. The state doesn't let skilled labour go to waste.

### What the State Provides

- **Housing.** Standard transmigrant tenements — identical two-storey buildings arranged in grids, each apartment furnished with government-issue furniture. The quality is uniform and modest. You don't own your home; you occupy it for as long as the census allows.
- **Employment.** Citizens are trained and assigned according to specialization. The assignment process is slow — bureaucratic approval can take weeks — but it guarantees a wage. The wage is enough to live on and nothing more. Nobody gets rich, but nobody starves.
- **Rationed goods.** Basic food staples, coal for heating, clothing, and medical care are provided through a ration-card system. The ration card is the only currency that crosses town lines — it's linked to your census record, not a local bank.
- **Education and training.** Children attend state schools. At a certain age, aptitude tests determine their specialization track — mechanical, clerical, medical, industrial, administrative. The state decides what you'll be, and trains you for it.

### The Private Margin

Capitalism exists in Valdris, but it is tightly constrained:

- **Small commerce.** Private shops — grocery stores, bakeries, taverns — operate alongside state distribution centres. They accept ration cards and occasionally deal in unrationed goods procured through local networks.
- **Inherited businesses.** A shopkeeper can pass a business to a child — but the child must qualify for a commercial specialization, and the business is subject to state licensing and price controls.
- **The black market.** Anything the state doesn't provide well enough — foreign newspapers, pre-Convergence artifacts, information — trades through informal networks. The black market is widespread and tolerated within limits, though the census bureau's secret police monitor it for political subversion rather than economic crime.

### The Bureaucracy

The Valdran state apparatus is slow by design — not because efficiency is impossible, but because the alternative (arbitrary allocation by local officials) would be worse. Every transfer, assignment, and exception requires paperwork, stamps, and approvals that can take weeks.

- **The Census Bureau** handles population tracking and relocation.
- **The Labour Allocation Office** handles job assignments.
- **The Provision Office** handles housing and ration cards.
- **The Chamber of Magnates** sets policy at the national level; local implementation is handled by precinct councils and garrison administrative boards.

Forms travel between these offices on paper — carbon copies, stamped and filed — because the Mandate forbids the electronic record-keeping that would make it efficient. The system is slow, but it is slow *for everyone*. Favouritism is difficult when every decision requires nine stamped copies.

## Context

The Provision System is not beloved, but it is accepted. Valdrans complain about the bureaucracy the way coastal towns complain about fog — it's there, it's slow, and it's not going anywhere. The system's greatest strength and greatest weakness are the same thing: it treats everyone the same. The welder and the census mathematician receive identical housing, identical rations, identical bureaucratic runaround. Nobody gets rich, but nobody starves. In a world where the census lottery could send you to a coastal garrison town at any moment, that predictability is a kind of wealth in itself.
