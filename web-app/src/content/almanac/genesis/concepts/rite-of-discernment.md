# Rite of Discernment

## Summary

A divination ritual performed by ordained priests of the Faithful Church — specifically the [[Sacred Order of the Dragon]] — to detect the presence or absence of infernal taint and divine grace in a soul, object, or phenomenon.

## Details

The rite is cast through prayer in old Latin, the sprinkling of salt and blessed water (chrism), and focused contemplation. It always ages the caster — the spiritual exertion leaves visible marks: deepened wrinkles, greyed hair, weakened frame. The residue of the chrism remains on the caster's hands for hours after the rite, a silver sheen that fades slowly.

The results are binary by Faithful doctrine: every soul bears either the mark of the Adversary (infernal taint: a cold pressure, a brimstone presence) or the mark of God (divine grace: warmth, order, a sense of being known). **Neutral souls should not exist.**

## The Anomalous Finding

The first Rite, performed by [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] on the Anomaly itself, found no infernal taint — it was not a demonic glamour. The second Rite, performed on [[Rebecca Osei|Lieutenant Commander Osei]], found blankness — neither taint nor grace. The third Rite, performed by a new priest of the Faithful delegation on Captain [[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]] at the formal parley ([[Chapter 07 – The Pilgrims]]), confirmed the same: blankness.

This finding is a profound theological crisis. In eight centuries of Faithful doctrine, every soul has carried one mark or the other. The blank souls of the modern world suggest either that God is larger than the Synods taught, or that the Adversary was never a universal law but a local catastrophe — or that modern humans are something entirely new in creation.
