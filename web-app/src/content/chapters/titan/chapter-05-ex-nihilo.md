# Chapter 5 – Ex Nihilo

## Summary

Five weeks. The [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] has a foothold, a strategic map, and the first native informants. Now it needs to understand what it is facing — not the politics of the AOT world, but the biology of its most terrifying feature. Dr. [[Elias Vogler]]'s deep political debriefing fills in the blanks that drone imagery could not capture: the global trade that makes Marley rich, the internment zones that make it monstrous, and the legend of the Rumbling that hangs over every conversation about [[Paradis Island]]. Coordinated drone surveillance of Paradis captures the political aftershocks of the basement truth — [[Yelena|foreign ships arriving]], a railway rising from the earth, [[Marley]]an patrols being intercepted and sunk by the [[Survey Corps]]. A ground-penetrating radar sortie over the Walls confirms that the Rumbling is not a myth: the Walls are full of Colossal Titans, hundreds of thousands strong. The Quadripartite Committee greenlights a covert mission — an amphibious assault ship and destroyer escort transiting 8,000 kilometres north to capture a live Titan from Paradis before the Survey Corps finishes exterminating them. The capture succeeds, and the biological analysis that follows is devastating: Titans are transformed Eldians, every Subject of Ymir is a biological trigger, and the transformation process violates conservation of mass-energy — matter created ex nihilo from a source modern physics cannot detect. By the chapter's end, [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] holds a living sample, has mapped the Wall Titans, and knows the Eldian population is a biological powder keg. And the committee votes to do nothing — not yet — because no one knows what action would look like.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] — Vogler's World — Day 44–48

The debriefing room was a converted shipping container in the CATF intelligence section, soundproofed and climate-controlled, its walls lined with screens showing the drone imagery that had mapped [[Elias Vogler]]'s entire world before anyone in it knew they were being watched.

[[Tanaka Kenji|Lieutenant Colonel Tanaka Kenji]] sat across the table from Vogler, a tablet between them, a pot of coffee between that. The coffee was American — a drip machine requisitioned from the [[USS Milius (DDG-69)|Milius]]'s wardroom — and Vogler had developed a dependency on it that surprised him. Forty-seven days at sea on ship's coffee had not prepared him for what caffeine could taste like when it was not brewed through boiler-scale and rust.

"Let's start with the trade routes," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said. His Marleyan had improved across twelve days of daily sessions. He no longer needed the translator's tablet for basic conversation. "We have drone imagery of maritime traffic, but we don't know the cargo. We don't know the relationships."

[[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] spread a hand-drawn chart across the table — one of his own maps, annotated in Marleyan script, cross-referenced with the drone imagery that [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]]'s analysts had printed and laminated. The two maps did not quite match. The drone imagery was more precise. The Marleyan charts were more knowing.

"Here." He pointed at the eastern archipelago — Hizuran territory, the inverted Japanese islands. "Hizuru trades with everyone and fights no one. They are the only nation Marley does not threaten with invasion because they are useful. Their diplomats mediate. Their merchants transport. Their engineers — " he paused. "Their engineers build things Marley cannot build itself. Iceburst stone refinement. Precision optics. The materials that make Titan weaponry practical."

"They are neutral."

"They are profitable. Neutrality follows profit, not principle." [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] traced a line from Hizuru to the Marleyan mainland, then south to the Mid-East territories. "The Mid-East purchases Hizuran optics. Marley purchases Hizuran engineers. No one purchases Hizuran loyalty because loyalty is not for sale."

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] made a note. "And Hizuru's relationship with Paradis?"

"Non-existent, officially. The world has a standing policy of non-contact with Paradis. The threat of the Rumbling — " he saw [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]]'s expression, " — you know about the Rumbling."

"We have drone imagery of the Walls. We have your legends. We don't know how much of it is real."

"The Rumbling is a threat that Marleyan schoolchildren are taught before they learn to read. The Colossal Titans inside the Walls will march across the earth and flatten every continent if the Founding Titan commands them. It is the reason the world does not invade Paradis. It is also the reason Marley sends Warrior Units to breach the Walls — to capture the Founder before anyone on Paradis learns how to use it." [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] leaned back. "How many of my government's secrets do you want me to explain in one sitting?"

"All of them. But start with the Eldians."

[[Elias Vogler|Vogler]]'s expression shifted. This was the subject he had been avoiding. "What about them?"

"Everything. The internment zones. The biology. The armbands. Why a continent-spanning empire builds walls around its own cities to keep a specific population inside."

[[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] was silent for a long moment. When he spoke, his voice was quieter.

"Because Eldians are not like other people. I do not mean — " he stopped, frustrated with his own language. "I mean biologically. There is something in Eldian blood that makes Titan transformation possible. It is not a curse. It is not a myth. It is measurable. Every Eldian carries a biological potential that no Marleyan or Hizuran or Mid-Easterner possesses. Marley has built an empire on weaponising that potential. The Warrior Unit recruits Eldian children. The Titan Security Division turns Eldian prisoners into Pure Titans and drops them on enemy formations. The internment zones exist because Marley needs a controlled population of Subjects of Ymir to sustain the Titan programme — and because the Marleyan public has been taught for a century that an Eldian walking freely in the streets is a Titan waiting to happen."

"You sound like you don't believe that last part."

"I am a scientist. I believe in evidence. The evidence is that Eldians are biologically distinct. The question of whether that makes them dangerous — " he paused. "The question is political, not scientific. And politics is not my field."

"But you have an opinion."

"I have an Eldian crew," [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] said. "Eight men and women who saved my expedition three times with nothing but competence and the refusal to die in a frozen ocean. They are not devils. They are engineers. If your committee wants to know whether [[Frieda]] is a threat, the answer is no. If they want to know whether her biology makes her a threat — " he spread his hands. "I am an oceanographer. That is a biologist's question."

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] made another note. The political map was filling in. The biological question remained unanswered — and the committee, he knew, was already discussing how to answer it.

"You mentioned the internment zones are designed to sustain the Titan programme," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said. "How?"

"By manufacturing Titans." [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] said it flatly, the flatness of a man who had learned to speak of atrocity as though it was weather. "Marley takes Eldians — criminals, dissidents, citizens who failed the quarterly loyalty tests — and injects them with Titan spinal fluid. The injection triggers a transformation. The person becomes a Pure Titan. The Pure Titan is deployed against Marley's enemies. This is public policy. It is debated in the Marleyan parliament. The newspapers report on it as a matter of military preparedness."

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] looked at him. "They turn people into weapons. And the people know this."

"They watch it happen. At the port in Liberio, there is an audience deck where citizens can observe the transformation ceremonies. They applaud." He paused. "Eldians are required to attend as well. To witness. To understand what loyalty failure costs."

"How many people are turned into Titans annually?"

"Hundreds. Perhaps thousands. The programme escalated during the Mid-East war. Every Pure Titan you have seen in our drone footage — " [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] looked at the screens on the walls, at the images of shambling giants, " — was once a person. A Subject of Ymir. A man or a woman with a family. A name. And then a syringe. And then a monster."

The coffee had gone cold. Outside the shipping container, the austral winter wind scraped across the volcanic plateau. [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] hummed with the noise of a thousand machines — generators, pumps, the distant thrum of helicopter rotors. [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] sat in the centre of the most advanced military-scientific installation on either Earth, drinking cold American coffee in a shipping container, explaining to a Japanese intelligence officer that his country's government was in the business of turning people into cannibalistic monsters.

"Is there anything else," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] asked quietly, "that you think we should know?"

[[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] looked at the drone imagery one more time. The Walls. The Titans. The armbands visible on the figures in the Liberio streets.

"Marley sent the Warrior Unit to Paradis because the King's technology — the iceburst stone — is worth a fortune," he said. "The Titan weapons they could build with unlimited access to that resource would make them unassailable. The war in the Mid-East is about securing a beachhead closer to Paradis. The war will escalate. Whoever holds Paradis controls the future. And whoever controls the Founding Titan controls Paradis. And the Founding Titan — " he paused, " — is held by a fifteen-year-old boy named [[Eren Yeager]]. Who has just learned that the entire world wants his people dead."

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] closed his notebook. "Thank you, Dr. Vogler."

"I am not sure you should thank me. I am not sure I have given you anything you will be grateful to know."

### Scene 2: [[Paradis Island]] — Drone Surveillance — Day 48–55

The KUS-15 had been orbiting Paradis for forty-three hours when it caught the arrival.

The drone intelligence section aboard the [[R-V Isabu]] ran in four shifts now. [[Tanaka Kenji]] had not slept in eighteen hours. He stood in the converted laboratory module that served as the operations centre — a wall of screens, a bank of consoles, the low hum of climate control systems doing battle with the body heat of twelve analysts — and watched what the drone was watching.

A ship. Approaching Paradis from the east. Flying a flag that was not Marleyan and not Mid-Eastern and not Hizuran — something improvised, a banner that looked like it had been stitched together from mutiny.

"Enhance on the flag," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said.

The image sharpened. The flag was crude — anti-Marleyan iconography, the cross-in-circle device deliberately defaced with what looked like black paint. This was not a Marleyan patrol vessel. This was Marley's enemies, arriving on Paradis uninvited but not unwelcome.

On the ground — visible in the drone's optical turret at 20,000 feet — a reception party waited at the port. Survey Corps soldiers in green cloaks. A figure that the analysts' face-matching algorithm identified as [[Hange Zoë]], the new Commander, standing at the front of the formation. Another figure — short, dark-haired, standing slightly apart from the formation — that the analysts believed, with lower confidence, was [[Eren Yeager]] himself.

The visitors disembarked. Two figures in improvised uniforms. One tall, pale, severe — a woman with short blonde hair who moved with the coiled authority of a person who had killed her way out of one world and into another. The other was darker-skinned, broader-shouldered, carrying what looked like technical instruments — a man with an engineer's posture and a soldier's wariness.

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] did not know their names. He would learn them later, in the Almanac entries that CATF intelligence compiled from intercepted radio traffic and contextual analysis: **[[Yelena]]** and **[[Onyankopon]]**. Defectors from inside the Marleyan military. Leaders of something called the [[Anti-Marleyan Volunteers]] — a faction that, according to the signals intercept, had mutinied against their Marleyan officers and brought their ship to Paradis as an offering.

The diplomatic implications were enormous. Paradis was now in contact with an outside power. The walled island was no longer isolated. The Volunteers would bring knowledge of Marleyan tactics, technology, and troop dispositions — everything Paradis needed to turn from a doomed fortress into a real threat. And CATF would have to factor a fifth faction into its strategic calculus.

"Continue orbiting," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said. "I want updates every thirty minutes. Log everything."

---

The railway came next.

The drone's camera captured it over three successive sorties: a narrow-gauge line being laid from the industrial district of Wall Rose to the eastern coast, running through terrain that had been untamed forest two weeks earlier. Steam shovels. Rails being spiked into sleepers by work crews in civilian clothing. A locomotive — Marleyan design, visibly adapted by Paradisian engineers — making its first test run on a finished section of track at twelve kilometres per hour.

"Infrastructure at wartime pace," the American analyst said. She was a Navy lieutenant named Okonkwo, seconded to the drone section from the ONI, and she had spent her career watching adversarial nations build railways toward contested borders. "They're not building for trade. That gauge is too narrow for freight volume. They're building to move troops and artillery to the coast."

"Because they expect an invasion," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said.

"Because they know the invasion is coming. Marley lost three Titan Shifters at Shiganshina. The Warrior Unit is in pieces. Reiner Braun is the only survivor who made it back to Marley — and even he was defeated. The empire cannot afford to let Paradis consolidate. So Paradis is racing to consolidate before the empire strikes."

"Race against what?"

"Against the Marleyan navy. Against the Mid-East war ending and freeing up Marleyan forces. Against the world noticing that the island of devils is building railroads and making friends." Okonkwo zoomed the image. "They have maybe six months. A year if they're lucky. They're not lucky."

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] watched the railway creep toward the coast. A million people inside walls that were about to become a battlefield. A fifteen-year-old boy with a weapon no one understood. A defector ship from a mutiny that no one outside Paradis knew had happened.

And CATF — eight thousand kilometres south, on islands that belonged to no one, watching through eyes made of carbon fibre and silicon.

The Marleyan patrols came in a cluster across Days 50–54.

The drone captured the first engagement: a Marleyan cruiser — an iron-hulled steamship with twin gun turrets, eight-inch guns, a crew of perhaps 120 — approaching Paradis from the southwest. It was a reconnaissance vessel. Its mission, [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] later confirmed, would have been to assess the situation after the Warrior Unit's failure — to determine whether Paradis remained containable or whether a full naval expedition was required.

The Paradisian response was not containable.

Survey Corps soldiers attacked from above — figures swinging on cables from the cliff faces overlooking the approach channel, descending on the cruiser's deck like birds of prey. The optical turret captured muzzle flashes from the cruiser's deck guns, then the flash of explosive warheads — Thunder Spears, [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] had told them about those, Marleyan reports of an Eldian anti-Titan explosive that could punch through Armored Titan plating and was undefeatable against steel deck armour.

The cruiser took three Thunder Spear hits in the first sixty seconds. Its forward turret was blown off its mounting. Its bridge was shattered. The surviving crew surrendered. The Survey Corps boarded the vessel and took control.

By the end of the engagement — fourteen minutes from first descent to surrender — Paradis had a captured Marleyan cruiser with intact hull and engine, a complement of naval artillery, and an entire ship's worth of Marleyan signals intelligence. The cruiser was towed into port. The crew was marched off in Survey Corps custody. The flag of Paradis — the Wall crest, the crossed swords, the rose — was raised on the captured mast.

The second patrol suffered a worse fate. It approached from the north and was met by what the drone analysts identified as the same Survey Corps boat that had captured the first ship — a converted Marleyan patrol vessel, presumably from the Volunteer defectors. The engagement was over in minutes. The Marleyan ship was sunk by concentrated Thunder Spear fire directed at its waterline. Its crew abandoned ship in lifeboats. The Survey Corps vessel collected the survivors and sank the lifeboats. There were no prisoners from the second patrol.

The third patrol turned back before it entered Paradisian territorial waters. Its captain had seen the smoke on the horizon. He knew what it meant.

"Paradis has a navy now," Okonkwo said. "Three ships — one Volunteer defector vessel, two captured Marleyan hulls. And that supply train they're building to the coast? It's for naval logistics. They're going to contest their own waters."

"They're preparing for war," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said.

"They're fighting one already. They just don't have a name for it yet."

On Day 55, [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] ordered the GPR sortie.

The drone was a modified KUS-15 with a ground-penetrating radar pod slung under its fuselage — a unit normally used for geological survey in the Antarctic, rigged for stealth at low altitude. The sortie required cloud cover, which the weather satellite from Earth showed moving in over Wall Maria at approximately 02:00 Paradis local time. The drone crossed the AOT ocean at 35,000 feet, descended to 2,000 feet at the edge of Paradis airspace, and began its pass.

The radar activated at 01:47.

The returns came in pulses. The outer face of Wall Maria showed as a uniform crystalline structure — the hardened Titan crystal that composed the Wall surface. The drone followed the Wall crest for seventy kilometres.

At thirty-two kilometres in, the radar return shifted.

The crystalline uniformity gave way to irregular density patterns. Large-scale organic structures. Bodies. Standing vertically, shoulder-to-shoulder, embedded in the Wall matrix. The signatures were unmistakable — humanoid forms, fifty metres in height, suspended in crystal like insects in amber.

The drone continued. More bodies. More returns. An uninterrupted line of Colossal Titans stretching the full diameter of the outermost Wall — 480 kilometres of circumference, an unbroken ring of embedded giants.

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]]'s analysts ran the numbers. A single Colossal Titan occupied approximately fifteen metres of Wall frontage. That yielded roughly one Colossal per fifteen-metre segment. Across 480 kilometres of outer Wall: approximately 32,000 Colossals in Wall Maria alone. The inner Walls — Rose and Sina — were smaller in diameter but no less dense. The total across all three Walls was probably between 300,000 and 600,000 Colossal Titans.

"Are there any gaps?" [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] asked.

"No gaps," the analyst said. "Continuous formation. Maria, Rose, Sina. All three Walls. The outer circumference of Maria alone could produce a line of Colossal Titans stretching from here to Paradis and back five times. If all three Walls activate simultaneously — "

"They cover the planet," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] finished.

The GPR drone completed its pass and climbed back to altitude, its radar pod still pulsing, its data storage filling with the signatures of half a million sleeping weapons. The cloud cover held. The drone crossed back into open ocean undetected.

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] transmitted the finding to the committee with the simplest subject line he could construct:

**"WALL TITANS CONFIRMED. ESTIMATE 300,000–600,000. THE RUMBLING IS REAL."**

### Scene 3: Quad Committee — The Greenlight — Day 55–58

[[Lee Jun-ho]] convened the emergency session at 04:00 Seoul time. The other three principals joined within nine minutes — [[Zheng Weimin]] from Beijing, [[Elizabeth Cross]] from Washington, [[Nakamura Kenji]] from Tokyo. [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka Kenji]] presented the findings from the drone control module aboard the [[R-V Isabu]], his face rendered in 4K on four screens across four capitals. He had not slept in twenty-seven hours, and the resolution of the video feed did him no favours. But his voice was steady. His briefing was terminal. And by the time he finished, none of the four principals needed convincing.

"The Walls are a weapon," he said. "Not a defensive structure. Three hundred to six hundred thousand Colossal Titans — which we now know, from Dr. [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]]'s debriefing, Marleyan theology describes as an apocalyptic mechanism called the Rumbling. The activation mechanism is the Founding Titan, currently held by [[Eren Yeager]] of Paradis. If activated, the Wall Titans will march outward in all directions. The Marleyan Empire will be crushed within days. The continent beyond it — weeks. The entire AOT world surface — months. No known defensive measure exists. No diplomatic channel exists. And the holder of this weapon is a fifteen-year-old boy who watched his mother get eaten by a Titan when he was ten and who has just been told that the entire human race outside his Walls wants his species exterminated."

The silence that followed was the silence of four people accepting that their problems had just become larger than any solution they had the authority to implement.

"We need a live specimen," [[Lee Jun-ho]] said. "We know what the Walls are. We know why the AOT world is afraid of Paradis. But we still do not know what a Titan *is*. Not biologically. The drone imagery shows us behaviour. The GPR data shows us scale. But we cannot make policy toward Paradis without understanding the transformation mechanism. If the spinal fluid — "

"If Dr. [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]]'s account is accurate," [[Zheng Weimin]] said, "the spinal fluid turns Subjects of Ymir into Titans. We have eight Subjects of Ymir under our roof at this moment — Eldian engineers we have employed. If that mechanism is real, our personnel are a biosafety risk we have not accounted for. We need to know. And the only way to know is to capture a live Titan and study the biology."

[[Elizabeth Cross]] leaned forward. "A mission to Paradis. Eight thousand kilometres from Grande Terre. An opposed insertion into territory patrolled by an active military force that has just demonstrated the capability to sink warships. If the extraction fails, CATF personnel are in Eldian hands. They will be interrogated. First contact with Paradis will happen on Paradis's terms, with CATF personnel as prisoners, and our diplomatic position will be defined by an incident we cannot control."

"That is the argument against," [[Lee Jun-ho]] said. "The argument for is that every day we wait, the Survey Corps exterminates more of the local Titan population. The cleanup operation is visible from our drones. At the current rate of clearance — the Executioner from Hell device, the coordinated hunting patrols — Paradis will be Titan-free within six to nine months. The window to capture a live specimen without triggering contact is closing. If we want a Titan, we must move now."

[[Nakamura Kenji]] spoke for the first time. His voice was quiet. The room quieted further to accommodate it.

"The [[USS Tripoli (LHA-7)|USS Tripoli]] arrived at Grande Terre five days ago with a full MEU complement. Her well deck carries two LCACs. Her flight deck can accommodate the CH-53K we need for the airlift. She can make the transit to Paradis in four days at thirty knots with an escort destroyer — the [[JS Suzutsuki (DD-117)]] has the range and the sensor package. If we are going to do this, the window is now. The ship is fuelled. The Marines are briefed — at a classification level that does not yet include the target. The helicopter crews have been running practice lift drills at the [[Alfred Faure Forward Operating Base|Alfred Faure]] runway for a week. The capture rig — industrial chains, cargo straps, four-point anchoring system — was designed and fabricated by the engineering detachment last month for a contingency no one expected to use."

Another silence. Then [[Elizabeth Cross]], the Deputy Secretary of State, the diplomat who had spent her career preventing wars and managing crises, said:

"I move we greenlight the mission. The risk of not knowing what a Titan is outweighs the risk of a failed extraction. We cannot stand in front of our presidents in six months and explain that we knew the Rumbling was real, knew the Eldian population was biologically distinct, knew a global extinction event was embedded in the landscape — and chose not to gather the intelligence that would let us do something about it."

[[Zheng Weimin]] nodded. "Seconded."

The vote was 4-0.

The mission would be codenamed Operation REEVE — a word that meant nothing in any of the four official languages of the coalition, chosen specifically because its meaninglessness was its only security value.

### Scene 4: The Northern Expedition — Day 58–65

The [[USS Tripoli (LHA-7)|Tripoli]] departed [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] at 00:30 local on Day 58, steaming north with her running lights dark and her radar silent. The [[JS Suzutsuki (DD-117)|Suzutsuki]] took station 3,000 metres off her port quarter, her AESA arrays sweeping a passive perimeter that extended 200 kilometres in every direction, cataloguing every surface contact and filing them according to threat status. There were no Marleyan ships on this track. There had never been Marleyan ships on this track. The 8,000-kilometre corridor between the Antarctic Convergence and Paradis Island was empty ocean — too cold for trade, too remote for patrol, too far from any shipping lane to matter. It was why CATF had been undetected for two months. It would be why the Tripoli arrived at Paradis without anyone knowing she had come.

Captain [[Park Sung-ho]] stood in the Tripoli's hangar deck, watching his Marines run the capture drill for the seventh time. He was thirty-eight years old, a Republic of Korea Marine Corps officer with two tours in Special Reconnaissance, and he had been handed the command of Operation REEVE's ground element six hours before the Tripoli sailed. He had read the target assessment twice. The first time, he had thought the classification was a joke. The second time, he had stopped thinking and started planning.

The Titan was a Class A target — six to seven metres in height, bipedal, shambling gait, exhibited typical Pure behaviour. Daylight speed: approximately fifteen kilometres per hour. Night speed: zero (dormant). Combat capability: regeneration from non-nape wounds, crushing force in hands and jaw, no ranged attack. The capture sequence was:

**One.** KUS-15 identifies and tracks the Titan from fifteen thousand feet. Maintains visual until the helicopter deploys.

**Two.** CH-53K launches from the Tripoli with a twelve-Marine capture team and two CATF biologists — Dr. [[Ahn Jae-hyun|Ahn Jae-hyun]] requested to observe personally, and a Chinese biochemist named Dr. Lin Qing who had been seconded from the PLA Medical Research Institute.

**Three.** Marines deploy 500 metres from the target. If the Titan charges, the M2 Browning .50 calibre machine gun engages the creature's knees, ankles, and hip joints. Regeneration is estimated at ten to thirty seconds for limb damage. The fire must be continuous and sustained to maintain immobilisation.

**Four.** Capture team moves in with industrial chains and ten-tonne cargo straps. Four-point anchoring at the creature's shoulders and pelvis. The Titan screams — recorded Titan speech sounds like distorted human language, no known dialect — and the screaming does not stop.

**Five.** CH-53K sling-loads the Titan from the capture site to the Tripoli's flight deck. Transit time: eighteen minutes at combat power. Titan weight: estimated thirty-two tonnes at this size class, within the CH-53K's external lift envelope.

**Six.** Titan is placed on a marked zone of the flight deck. The shroud team deploys a light-blocking fabric cover — modified from the ship's helicopter-wash curtains — over the creature. Titan enters dormancy within three to five minutes of light deprivation.

**Seven.** Transit back to Grande Terre. Shroud maintained. Titan remains dormant. Crew maintains security until the specimen is transferred to the containment facility.

[[Park Sung-ho]] had identified eleven points of failure. The most dangerous was Step Six — the interval between the Titan touching down on the deck and the shroud being fully deployed. During that interval, the creature would be awake, mobile, and unrestrained on a flight deck with eighty-seven crew personnel within immediate reach. The solution was a steel breaker net — borrowed from the CATF construction detachment's debris containment inventory — that would be spread over the landing zone before the CH-53K released its load. The net would not restrain a thirty-two-tonne Titan. It would buy the twelve seconds the shroud team needed to cover the creature before it oriented itself and began killing.

"If the net fails," [[Park Sung-ho|Park]] said to his squad leaders, "we do not fight a Titan on a flight deck. We do not attempt to contain it. We eliminate it — concentrated fire to the nape — and accept the mission failure. A mission failure is acceptable. A casualty list is acceptable. A loose Titan on a ship with a thousand souls is not. Are we clear?"

The squad leaders were clear.

The Tripoli steamed north. The ocean was grey and empty. The Titan, somewhere ahead, did not know it was being hunted.

### Scene 5: The Capture — Day 65 Evening / Day 66 Early Morning

The KUS-15 acquired the target at 19:14 Paradis local time.

It was a six-metre class, wandering in the open territory between Wall Maria's outer boundary and the shoreline at the northern edge of the island — the least patrolled sector of Paradis during the cleanup operation. The Survey Corps concentrated its Titan-clearing efforts in the south and west, where the population centres were denser and the terrain was more navigable. The north was low priority: tundra, forest, and a handful of fishing villages that had already been evacuated. The Titan had been wandering alone for at least twelve hours since the drone began tracking. It had not encountered any human contact. It had not encountered another Titan. It was as isolated as a creature of its kind could be on an island that was systematically exterminating its species.

The Tripoli moved to her launch station — thirty-two kilometres off the coast, beyond visual horizon for shore-based observers, in water deep enough to submerge her hull signature from any surface vessel's sonar. The night was overcast. The moon was thin — a crescent obscured by low cloud cover. Visibility was effectively zero for any lookout without night-vision equipment.

At 01:45 ship time, [[Park Sung-ho]] gave the launch order. The CH-53K lifted off the Tripoli's deck with the capture team aboard, its rotor wash flattening the grey sea for a hundred metres in every direction. The KUS-15 tracked the Titan's position — still wandering, still alone, now four kilometres from the coast. The CH-53K flew at 200 feet AGL, navigation lights off, the pilots wearing ANVIS-9 night-vision goggles that turned the dark Paradisian coast into a green-lit landscape of forest silhouette and shoreline whitecaps.

The Titan noticed the helicopter when it was 800 metres out. It turned. Its mouth opened — the silent working of a jaw designed for consuming, not speaking. It began to move toward the sound. The M2 gunner, a staff sergeant named Okuda who had been a competitive marksman before joining the Marine Corps, sighted on the creature's right knee and waited.

The helicopter touched down. The Marines deployed. The Titan was at 300 metres and closing.

"Engage," [[Park Sung-ho]] said.

The M2 Browning fired. The .50 calibre rounds hit the Titan's right knee with the force of a sledgehammer striking wet clay — the joint buckled, collapsed, and the creature pitched forward onto its face. It screamed. The sound was unearthly — a human voice warped through a throat that was two metres wide, producing frequencies that the Marines' earplugs could not fully suppress. The Titan pushed itself up on its arms. Its knee was already regenerating — steam rising from the wound, flesh knitting back into form at a visible rate, the knee joint reforming from splintered bone and shredded cartilage in less than the ten-second estimate. Okuda fired again. The knee collapsed. The Titan screamed again. Okuda fired a third time, walking the rounds up the thigh to the hip joint, and the Titan's entire right leg went limp.

"Left leg," [[Park Sung-ho|Park]] said. "Same protocol."

The left leg went the same way. The Titan was now immobile — on its chest in the cold Paradisian earth, its legs destroyed and regenerating and destroyed again, its arms still thrashing. Corporal [[Lee Dae-won]] (to be named in the after-action report as Corporal Lee Dae-won, in the Almanac entry that would be classified at the highest level) moved in with a set of anchor chains to secure the creature's left arm.

The Titan's right arm — unexpected, devastating — swept out in a blind arc and caught Corporal Lee across the torso. The impact threw him six metres into the trunk of a pine tree. His left arm was broken in two places. Two ribs were fractured. The armour plate in his vest had absorbed the killing force of the blow, and the tree had absorbed what the vest could not, and Corporal Lee was alive — dazed, winded, in pain — but alive.

[[Park Sung-ho|Park]] did not hesitate. "Infantry. Concentrated fire on the right arm. I want it down now."

Three M4 carbines and the M2 Browning fired simultaneously. The Titan's right arm disintegrated at the elbow. The forearm fell to the earth, steaming, already beginning to regrow from the severed joint. The chains went onto the left arm. The anchors went into the ground. The Titan screamed and screamed and screamed, and the Marines worked through the screaming because they had been trained for everything except this, and training, it turned out, was more durable than fear.

The four-point anchoring was complete in six minutes. The Titan — chained at shoulders and pelvis, its arms and legs shattered and regenerating and shattered again in a cycle the Marines maintained with bursts of automatic fire — could not move. Could not escape. Could not harm anyone else.

"Reeve Actual to Christchurch," [[Park Sung-ho]] said into his radio, using the CH-53K's callsign. "Package is secure. Ready for lift."

The CH-53K descended. The sling-load cables went onto the anchor points. The helicopter's engines screamed at combat power. And the Titan — thirty-two tonnes of impossible biology, a creature that was once a person, a weapon and a victim and the most important scientific specimen in either world — rose into the air, dangling from cables, its face blank with mindless hunger, its voice now silent, its regeneration already repairing the damage that had brought it down.

At 03:12, the Titan touched the flight deck of the Tripoli. The breaker net was already in position. The shroud team deployed. Thirteen seconds later, the light-blocking fabric covered the creature. Five seconds after that, the Titan went dormant.

Corporal Lee was treated in the Tripoli's medical bay. His arm would heal. His ribs would heal. The Almanac entry for Operation REEVE would list one wounded, zero killed, one live Titan captured.

The Tripoli turned south. The KUS-15 continued to orbit, watching the capture site for any sign of Survey Corps response. None came. The night was quiet. Paradis did not know what had been taken from it.

### Scene 6: [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] — The Ex Nihilo Finding — Day 66–71

The containment pen was a converted forty-foot shipping container with the roof removed and a reinforced lid installed — a steel cage with industrial anchoring points, a light-control system, and a remote camera array that fed data to the biological analysis team in the adjacent compound. The Titan was moved into the pen at 05:00 on Day 66, still shrouded, still dormant. When the sun rose over Grande Terre at 05:47, the shroud was remotely retracted.

The Titan awoke.

It was six metres of grey flesh and mindless hunger, chained to the floor of a shipping container on an island it did not recognise, surrounded by scientists and soldiers who watched it through screens and plexiglass. It thrashed against its restraints for eleven minutes. The chains held. The anchors held. The Titan subsided into the passive shambling that was its natural state, its face slack, its eyes vacant, its mouth opening and closing in an endless silent rhythm. It did not eat. It did not drink. Sunlight sustained it. Sunlight was all it had ever needed.

The biological analysis began within the hour.

Dr. [[Ahn Jae-hyun]] led the joint team — Korean, Chinese, American, and Japanese biologists working in a prefab laboratory that had been purpose-built for this investigation. Dr. [[Wang Lijuan]] coordinated the chemical and spectroscopic analysis from the [[Xiang Yang Hong 06]], where her existing equipment was sufficient for the initial assays. The team worked in rotating shifts, sleeping in four-hour blocks, eating meals delivered by CATF personnel, communicating in a pidgin of English and technical vocabulary that had become the unofficial language of the coalition's scientific corps.

The first finding was the blood work.

The Titan's blood was human. Not structurally — the cellular morphology was aberrant, the regenerative factors off-scale, the metabolic rate impossibly high — but genetically. The nuclear DNA extracted from a blood sample matched the baseline sequences that the team had established from the eight Eldian engineers at Grande Terre. The Titan was a Subject of Ymir. Genetically Eldian. The creature in the cage was, before its transformation, a person.

The team ran the comparison twice. Then three times. Then a fourth time, with a different sample, extracted from a different site, under different conditions. The result did not change.

"It is Eldian," Dr. Lin Qing said. She was forty-one, a biochemist who had spent her career studying haemorrhagic fevers, and her hands were steady on the microscope controls. "Every marker. Eldian human DNA. The transformation did not overwrite the genome. It added something."

The second finding was the spinal fluid assay.

Spinal fluid extracted from the Titan — a careful procedure conducted while the creature was dormant, the needle inserted between two cervical vertebrae under real-time ultrasound guidance — was tested in vitro against blood samples from the eight Eldian engineers and from a panel of seven non-Eldian CATF personnel who had volunteered as controls.

The Eldian blood samples reacted. Within fifteen seconds of exposure to the Titan's spinal fluid, a cascade of protein activation occurred — unknown enzymes, unclassifiable reactions, a chain of molecular events that did not correspond to any known biological pathway. The non-Eldian blood samples showed no reaction whatsoever.

"Marleyan Titan science is empirical," Dr. [[Ahn Jae-hyun|Ahn]] said. He was reading the report over Lin's shoulder, his glasses pushed up on his forehead, his face grey with fatigue. "They knew the spinal fluid triggered transformation. They did not know why. We still do not know why. What we know is that it works. It works on Eldians. It does not work on anyone else."

"The transformation is specific to Subjects of Ymir," Lin said. "Which means every Eldian on either Earth is biologically capable of becoming a Titan given exposure to spinal fluid. The eight engineers in our employ. The million people inside the Walls. The millions more in the Liberio Internment Zone and elsewhere in Marleyan territory. All of them. One injection. One Titan."

The third finding — the one that made the physics obsolete — came on Day 68.

The Titan's mass had been measured precisely during the capture. Thirty-two tonnes at rest, accounting for the weight of the chains and anchors. The human body from which it had been transformed would have weighed approximately sixty-five kilograms, based on the genetic markers indicating an adult male Subject of Ymir. The mass increase was approximately 500-fold. Origin of the additional mass: unknown.

The creature consumed nothing. The Titan had not eaten, had not drunk, had not absorbed any detectable material from its environment since its capture. Its mass remained constant — thirty-two tonnes, unchanging, sustained entirely by ambient sunlight. The energy input from solar radiation was a fraction of what would be required to sustain its metabolic rate, let alone to account for the creation of thirty-two tonnes of biological tissue from a sixty-five-kilogram body. The numbers did not add up. The numbers, in fact, violated the first law of thermodynamics at a scale that made a mockery of three centuries of physics.

Dr. [[Ahn Jae-hyun|Ahn]] and Dr. [[Wang Lijuan|Wang]] presented the finding to the Quadripartite Committee on Day 71 via secure video link. Dr. Nathan McCullough, a theoretical physicist seconded from the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology, delivered the technical briefing.

"This is not an energy conversion problem," McCullough said. He was thirty-four, a specialist in quantum field theory, and his voice carried the specific tension of a physicist who had just been asked to explain the impossible. "A sixty-five-kilogram human body converted entirely to energy via E=mc² would release approximately 5.8 exajoules — equivalent to 1,400 megatons of TNT, roughly the entire world's nuclear arsenal. That is not what is happening here. The transformation does not destroy the original body. It does not convert mass to energy. It creates new mass — thirty-two tonnes of cellular tissue, bone, nerve, and muscle — from a source that is not thermal, not chemical, not nuclear, and not observable. The mass appears. It is created. Ex nihilo."

He paused. The committee was silent.

"The implications are not limited to Titan biology. If the mechanism by which this species violates mass-energy conservation can be understood — if even a fraction of it can be replicated — we are looking at a technology that makes fusion power look like a candle. We are looking at unlimited matter synthesis. Unlimited energy. Unlimited everything. And the only species on either Earth that can access this mechanism is the Subjects of Ymir."

The finding was filed under classification EXNIHILO-1. It was accompanied by a cover note from [[Ahn Jae-hyun|Dr. Ahn Jae-hyun]], appended at 23:47 on Day 71:

*"We came here to understand an alternate Earth. We have discovered that our understanding of physics is incomplete. The Eldian population is a biological powder keg that violates the fundamental laws of thermodynamics. The Titans are not monsters. They are a message. And we do not yet know what the message says."*

### Scene 7: The Eldians Are Told — Day 72

The CATF personnel briefing room was a prefab building on the eastern ridge of Grande Terre, looking out over the harbour where four destroyers rode at anchor and the Tripoli sat in the deep-water berth with a flight deck that had recently carried a Titan. [[Frieda]] arrived first, her hands still marked with grease from the motor pool, a crescent wrench in her back pocket that she had forgotten to return to the tool crib. She sat in a folding chair near the front, her posture alert, her expression watchful. She had learned, in Liberio, to read a room before the room read her, and every instinct she had developed across twenty-four years of survival told her that this meeting was not good news.

The seven other Eldian engineers filed in behind her. They sat together, the way they had learned to sit in Liberio — clustered, compact, the unconscious formation of a group that had learned to survive by taking up as little space as possible.

Ensign [[DeSilva]] and [[Tanaka Kenji|Lieutenant Colonel Tanaka]] entered last. [[DeSilva|DeSilva]] looked tired. [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] looked worse. He had not slept since the Ex Nihilo finding was transmitted to the committee, and the past twenty hours had been consumed by a debate about whether the Eldian engineers should be told anything at all.

He had argued yes. [[Lee Jun-ho]] had agreed. [[Zheng Weimin]] had questioned the operational security risk. [[Elizabeth Cross]] had questioned the humanitarian obligation. [[Nakamura Kenji]] had pointed out that the Eldian engineers were already inside the perimeter, already aware of the Gate, and would inevitably learn the truth from rumour if not from briefing — and a truth learned from rumour was a truth that became a weapon. The committee had voted 4-0 to inform them.

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] spoke in Marleyan. His accent was imperfect. His grammar was functional. His tone was the tone of a soldier delivering a situation report, and the detachment in his voice was deliberate. He would not pity them. They had been pitied enough.

"Four days ago, CATF captured a live Titan on Paradis Island," he said. "The creature is in our containment pen. It is alive. It is undergoing biological analysis. We have made findings that concern you directly, and you have a right to know them before anyone else in this facility."

He paused. The room was silent.

"The Titan was once a human being. An Eldian. A Subject of Ymir. The transformation was triggered by the injection of Titan spinal fluid — a process that Marley uses as both a punishment system and a weapon of war. The spinal fluid transforms Eldian biology in a way that non-Eldian biology is not susceptible to. You are not infected. You are not carriers of a disease. But your bodies — through mechanisms we do not understand — possess the latent capacity for Titan transformation. If you were injected with Titan spinal fluid, you would become a Titan. If you are not injected, you remain exactly as you are now: human beings, no more dangerous than any other member of this coalition."

He paused again. The weight of the next sentence was heavier.

"The transformation process violates conservation of mass-energy. A sixty-five-kilogram human becomes a thirty-two-tonne Titan. The additional mass is created from a source that modern physics cannot detect, measure, or explain. You are not devils — the Marleyan language for your people is incorrect. But you are biologically distinct in a way that makes you, collectively, the most significant scientific discovery in human history."

[[Frieda]] had been silent through the entire briefing. She had not moved. She had not looked away. She had watched [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]]'s face with the same focus she once applied to the foreman in the Liberio machine shop — the focus of a person who had learned that missing a single word could cost her everything.

When she spoke, her voice was calm.

"In Liberio," she said, "they told us we were dangerous. Every Eldian child learns this before they can read. You are a devil. Your blood is contaminated. Your existence is a threat. I spent twenty-four years being told that I was a monster, and I spent twenty-four years convincing myself it was a lie. Now you tell me — " she paused, and for the first time since [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] had met her, her composure wavered, " — now you tell me they were correct. About the biology. About the danger. The only thing they were wrong about was the word."

"They were wrong about the word," [[DeSilva|DeSilva]] said, "and the word matters more than the biology. You are not a monster unless someone injects you with spinal fluid. That is not your biology — that is their weapon. You are the victim of a weaponisation programme, not the source of a threat."

[[Frieda]] turned to look at her. Her expression was unreadable. "Ensign [[DeSilva|DeSilva]], you have been kind to me. You were the first person in my life who told me I was free. I believe you meant it. But I also believe — " she looked around the room, at the folding chairs, at the prefab walls, at the view of the harbour where four destroyers sat at anchor, " — that the committee in Seoul and Beijing and Washington and Tokyo is going to read the same report you just gave me, and they are not going to see victims. They are going to see a million triggers walking around inside three Walls, and they are going to wonder what happens if one of those triggers gets pulled."

She stood. She had not been dismissed. She did not care.

"Thank you for telling me," she said. "I would rather know than be lied to. I have been lied to enough."

She walked out. The other seven Eldian engineers followed — not because they had been told to, but because [[Frieda]] had become, in the weeks since the capture of the Pelikan, the leader they had never had in Liberio.

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] watched them go. He did not move. The briefing room was silent save for the hum of the base's generators, and the distant roar of a helicopter lifting off from the [[Alfred Faure Forward Operating Base|Alfred Faure]] runway, and the sound of eight people walking toward a future that had just become something none of them could name.

### Scene 8: The Committee's Burden — Day 73–75

The Quadripartite Committee convened in closed session at 12:00 Seoul time on Day 73. The agenda was a single line, drafted by [[Lee Jun-ho]] and approved by the other three principals: *Strategic implications of findings EXNIHILO-1, RUMBLE-1, and PARADISO-1.*

EXNIHILO-1 was the mass creation discovery. RUMBLE-1 was the Wall Titan mapping. PARADISO-1 was the composite drone intelligence on Paradisian political development — the railway, the Volunteers, the captured Marleyan patrol ships, the accelerating military industrialisation of a nation that had been medieval two years earlier.

[[Lee Jun-ho]] opened.

"Ladies and gentlemen. We now know the following. One: the Eldian population of Paradis is biologically capable of Titan transformation triggered by spinal fluid injection. Two: the Walls of Paradis are made of three hundred to six hundred thousand Colossal Titans that can be activated by the Founding Titan to produce a global extinction event. Three: the transformation process violates conservation of mass-energy through a mechanism unknown to modern physics. Four: Paradis is industrialising for war, making foreign contact with anti-Marleyan forces, and capturing Marleyan naval vessels — demonstrating a capacity for self-defence that renders invasion increasingly costly. Five: we have a live Titan in containment, eight Eldian employees who now know their own biology, and a Marleyan naval observer who has mapped our base and is one unguarded moment away from transmitting what he knows to his government."

He looked around the room — or rather, at the three screens that represented the room, since the committee had not been in the same physical space since Singapore.

"We have all the information required to recommend a policy to our presidents. The question is: what policy?"

[[Zheng Weimin]] spoke first. His Mandarin was deliberate, his translation lag minimal, his expression the careful blankness of a diplomat who had learned that every word would be scrutinised by three other governments.

"Policy requires a threat model. We do not have a threat model. We have a biological specimen that violates physics, a demographic profile that represents an unprecedented biosecurity challenge, and a situational assessment that is changing faster than our drone flights can update it. If we brief our presidents tomorrow, they will ask us what we recommend. We have no recommendation to give. Every available option is unacceptable for a different reason."

He listed them.

"Option one: pre-emptive strike against Paradis. Eliminate the Founding Titan holder before the Rumbling can be triggered. This requires a military operation against a civilian population of one million, with no guarantee of success — we do not know where [[Eren Yeager]] is located at any given moment, and a failed decapitation strike triggers the Rumbling by provocation. Option one is genocide in the guise of prevention, and it may not work."

"Option two: diplomatic engagement. Open contact with Paradis. Offer recognition, aid, and a negotiated solution to the Rumbling threat. This requires treating the Founding Titan as a diplomatic partner rather than a hostile weapon system. It also requires convincing the Marleyan Empire — the dominant military power on the AOT planet — that a multinational coalition from another universe has arrived and intends to protect the island nation that Marley has spent a century trying to exterminate. Option two risks war with Marley and an armed confrontation for which CATF is not postured."

"Option three: containment without contact. Maintain the current posture — covert surveillance, intelligence gathering, no engagement. Wait for the political situation on Paradis to clarify. This buys time but solves nothing. Paradis is industrialising. Marley is mobilising. The Mid-East war will end eventually, freeing Marleyan forces for a Paradisian campaign. The Rumbling remains a live trigger. And our presence on the AOT side is getting harder to hide with every passing week. Option three is not a policy — it is a delay. And delays do not prevent catastrophes."

"Option four — " he paused, and the silence was the silence of a man who did not want to say what he was about to say, " — is biological intervention. Target the Eldian capacity for Titan transformation. If the spinal fluid mechanism can be blocked — if we can find a way to make a Subject of Ymir incapable of transformation — we eliminate the Rumbling threat without eliminating the civilian population. The problem with option four is that it requires biological research on live Eldian subjects at a scale that no medical ethics framework permits. It also requires access to Paradis that we cannot obtain without first choosing option two. It is an elegant solution with an impossible prerequisite."

[[Elizabeth Cross]] was silent for a long moment after [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] finished speaking. Then she said:

"Deputy Director [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] has described four options, all of which are various shades of unacceptable. I agree with his analysis. I also believe — " she chose her words with the precision of a diplomat crafting a statement that would be quoted in history books, " — that the decision to act on any of these options cannot be made by this committee. It must be made by the heads of state of the four signatory nations. We are not a government. We are a task force. Our mandate is to manage the Gate, not to determine the fate of a civilisation."

[[Nakamura Kenji]] nodded. "I concur. The committee's responsibility is to present findings to our principals and to recommend that a policy decision be deferred until the findings are complete and the options are fully assessed. We have the Titan — we can continue biological research. We have the drones — we can continue surveillance. We have the Pelikan crew — we can continue intelligence extraction. What we do not have is a decision framework that any of our governments would authorise based on six weeks of data."

[[Lee Jun-ho]] looked at the screens. The three faces that represented the three other governments in the coalition. Two months earlier, he had been a crisis manager in a windowless office, arguing with a defence minister about whether to call the Americans. Now he was sitting on the most powerful committee in human history, deciding whether to intervene in an alien civilisation that could end the world.

"Then we defer," he said. "The committee votes to maintain the current posture. The Titan remains in containment. The drones continue flying. The Eldian engineers remain employed under existing protocols. The strategic recommendations will be prepared for presidential briefing at a date to be determined by the committee when the biological analysis yields a complete threat model. The decision to act — or not to act — is reserved for the heads of state."

The vote was 4-0.

The meeting adjourned. The screens went dark. [[Lee Jun-ho]] sat alone in the secure communications office at the Blue House, looking at the blank wall where the other three principals had just been, and felt the specific weight of a decision that was not a decision — the decision to not decide, to wait, to gather more intelligence, to hope that the situation would clarify before it became a catastrophe.

It would not. He knew it would not. But he did not know what else to do.

### Scene 9: Grande Terre — Dreher's Notebook — Day 75

The decoding was completed at 14:00.

The CATF linguist — a Japanese signals intelligence specialist named Nakamura Hiroshi, no relation to the Vice Foreign Minister — had spent seventeen days on [[Klaus Dreher]]'s notebook. The Marleyan officer had written in a layered substitution cipher that used vocabulary drawn from naval signals manuals, meteorology codes, and the specific jargon of the Marleyan Titan Security Division. The cipher was not sophisticated by modern standards — a Second World War codebreaking team would have cracked it in three hours — but it was designed to resist a cursory inspection by a non-Marleyan reader, and it had served its purpose. Dreher had written every day since his detainment. The notebook contained:

- A complete ship count of the CATF fleet at Grande Terre: four destroyers, one amphibious assault ship, twelve patrol craft, six logistics vessels. Hull types, armament estimates, patrol schedules.
- A base layout diagram with annotations identifying the command complex, the communications hub, the fuel farm, the drone launch site, and the Eldian personnel quarters (marked with the Marleyan tactical symbol for "hostile biosignature — containable.")
- A personnel estimate: approximately 2,500 CATF troops on Grande Terre and Alfred Faure combined. Nationality distribution: approximately equal across the four coalition partners, with a slight American plurality.
- A capabilities assessment: "Unknown propulsion (no boilers, no stacks, estimated sustained speed 35+ knots). Surface-to-air missile capability confirmed (observed launch exercise, Day 51). Digital encryption beyond Marleyan cryptanalysis capability. Drone reconnaissance range exceeds 2,000+ km. This is not an expedition. This is an invasion force in waiting."
- A final entry, dated Day 74, in handwriting that was noticeably steadier than the earlier pages: *"I have no means of transmission. I have no line of communication. I am constructing a record on the assumption that it will be discovered after my death or after the Empire finds its own path to this island. If you are reading this and you are Marleyan — the Gate must be secured. The Gate is everything."*

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] read the decoded notebook in his office, a prefab unit overlooking the harbour. When he finished, he called [[Marcus Rivera]].

"Oberleutnant Dreher's restrictions need to escalate," he said. "He has mapped the entire base. He knows our capabilities. He has made tactical recommendations for a Marleyan seizure of the Gate. If he ever gets within reach of a radio transmitter, the strategic situation on this side of the Silver Line changes in minutes."

"Under the First Contact Protocol, we need committee authorisation for additional restrictions," [[Marcus Rivera|Rivera]] said.

"Then get it. In the meantime, I want a dedicated guard on his quarters at all hours. No unsupervised movement. No communication with other Pelikan crew. And I want his notebook confiscated — every page. If he starts another one, we confiscate that too. Dreher is no longer a detainee awaiting status determination. He is a hostile intelligence officer who has completed a tactical survey of our base. Treat him accordingly."

The guard was posted within the hour. Dreher's notebook was removed from his quarters. He did not resist. He did not speak. He watched the guard take the notebook with the same cold, methodical attention he had applied to counting ships and mapping fuel depots — the attention of a soldier who had expected this outcome and measured it as validation.

He would wait. He would watch. He would find another notebook. He would write again.

Because he was, after all, a soldier. And soldiers waited.

### Scene 10: The Exclusion Zone — Day 75, 18:00 KST

[[Park Do-hyun]] stood on the bridge wing of [[KCG 506]] with coffee that was hot — the galley's water heater had been repaired again, and the replacement had lasted three full days without failing, which was either a quality improvement or a statistical anomaly. The Gate stood in the evening light — the Silver Line on the blue water, the seam between two worlds, unchanged and unchanging.

[[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]]'s voice came over Channel 16.

"KCG 506. Haijing 2306. Good evening, Lieutenant [[Park Do-hyun|Park]]."

"Good evening, Captain [[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]]."

*"The supply vessel this morning was carrying additional security personnel. I counted forty-two. Armed. With additional ammunition crates."*

"I noticed."

*"Have you also noticed that the cargo manifests have been reclassified? I am no longer permitted to see the contents of the containers that go through the Gate. Only the weight. Only the destination. Nothing else."*

Park looked across the water at the distant silhouette of the Chinese cutter. He had not seen [[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]]'s face in months. He had learned to read him through voice alone — the cadence, the pauses, the things he did not say.

"You think something has changed," Park said.

*"I think something has changed already. Something they have not told us. The mood at Grande Terre — what I hear from the personnel who transit through my perimeter — is different. They do not speak as freely. They do not look at the Gate the same way. Whatever is happening on the far side, it has escalated."*

Park considered this. He thought about the Titan — the creature that the Marines had captured, the secret that the entire base was now protecting, the discovery that had reclassified every cargo manifest and silenced every loose conversation. He did not tell [[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]] any of this. He could not. But he understood what the Chinese captain was sensing.

"Something very large," Park said carefully, "has been learned. Something that the committee is still processing. I cannot say more than that."

A pause. Then:

*"You are being careful with your words. Which means the thing you are not saying is larger than either of us is cleared to discuss."*

"Yes."

*"Then we will not discuss it. We will guard the perimeter. We will watch the Gate. We will wait until someone tells us what we are waiting for."*

"That is what we do," Park said.

*"It is what we have always done."* Another pause. *"Does it bother you? Knowing that we are standing on the shore while the tide is coming in?"*

Park looked at the Gate. The Silver Line. The seam between two worlds. On the far side — eight thousand kilometres north — a fifteen-year-old boy held the power to end the world, and a million people lived inside three cages that could rise and walk, and a coalition of four nations had just decided to do nothing because no one knew what doing something looked like.

"Yes," Park said. "It bothers me."

He paused.

"But I would still rather be here than in a conference room in Seoul. Because when the tide comes in — " he looked at the Gate, " — we will see it first. And that, Captain [[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]], is not nothing."

The Silver Line caught the evening sun. The sea moved beneath it. The Gate was still. And somewhere on the far side, a Titan sat in a shipping container, and a notebook full of ship counts sat in an intelligence officer's safe, and eight Eldian engineers sat in their quarters trying to understand what it meant to be told they were not devils — but that everything in their blood said otherwise.

The quiet war was over. No one had fired a shot across the Silver Line. But the knowledge had crossed. And knowledge, [[Park Do-hyun|Park]] had learned, was the heaviest thing that ever passed through a Gate.
