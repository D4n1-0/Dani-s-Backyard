# The Theology of Waiting

## Summary
A theological framework articulated by [[Bishop Aldric]] upon his return from the modern world, born from his conversation with [[Peter Chisholm|Archbishop Chisholm]] and his immersion in a world where faith survives without visible miracles. The theology argues that the modern world is neither an ark ([[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]]'s position) nor a test ([[Inquisitor Paulus|Paulus]]'s instinct) — it is a *third thing*: a people who have lived through the hiddenness of God (*[[Deus Absconditus]]*) and have emerged bearing witness to a kind of faith the Faithful do not yet understand.

## Core Argument
- The moderns are not instruments of Providence sent to rescue the Faithful — they do not believe themselves to be, and forcing that role upon them would break them on an expectation they never accepted.
- The moderns are not heretics or deceivers to be tested and judged — they are what they claim to be: a people whose survival is a mystery, not a deliverance, and whose meaning is not yet revealed.
- The only faithful response is to **wait** — to accept the alliance without demanding theological conformity, to watch the moderns as they are rather than as the Faithful wish them to be, and to let the meaning of their arrival reveal itself in God's time, not the Synod's.

## Theological Roots
The *[[Deus Absconditus]]* — the hidden God — is the ancient Christian concept that God sometimes conceals Himself deliberately, not as punishment but as a condition for genuine free faith. The modern world, by [[Bishop Aldric|Aldric]]'s account, has lived under this hiddenness for eight centuries. Its faith is quiet and unfortified, surviving without miracles, miracles being absent. This makes the moderns uniquely valuable not as saviors but as *witnesses* — people who can show the Faithful what faith might look like when the war is over.

## Political Implications
- Validates accepting [[Davenport's Reply]] on its own terms — friendship without Providence
- Undercuts [[Inquisitor Paulus|Paulus]]'s instinct to test and categorise
- Reframes [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]]'s ark theology: the ark exists, but the ark does not know it is an ark
- Gives the Faithful command a theological basis for patience where patience has been lacking

## Status
