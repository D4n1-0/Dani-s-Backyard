# Laïcité

## Summary
The French constitutional principle of secularism — the most absolute form of church-state separation in the modern world. Codified in the 1905 law on the separation of Churches and State, *laïcité* is not merely freedom of religion but freedom *from* religion in public life. The state is neutral; no faith may claim public authority.

## Context
[[Étienne Morel|Foreign Minister Morel]] invokes *laïcité* as the non-negotiable foundation of France's response to the Anomaly. If the [[Crown of England|Faithful Crown]]'s [[The Ark|ark theology]] implies that modern secularism is blindness, then French secularism will push back — hard. This positions France as the most doctrinally opposed to Faithful theological claims, even more than the UK's [[Cautious Secular Engagement]] policy. The collision between a French republic that treats religion as a private matter and a theocratic monarchy that treats everything as a matter of God is coming, and it will not end in compromise.
