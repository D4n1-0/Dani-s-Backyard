# London (Modern)

## Overview

The capital of the [[United Kingdom (Modern)|United Kingdom]] on modern Earth, circa 2026. A city of nine million that has never known war on its own soil — no siege, no bombardment, no trench lines — and whose relationship with the divine is private, optional, and largely forgotten. Glass towers and stone monuments, bridges arching across the Thames, a skyline that hums with electric light and the noise of eight million vehicles. St. Paul's Cathedral is a functioning church and a heritage site — people pay an entry fee to photograph it. The Palace of Westminster hosts a Parliament that begins its sessions without prayer. Oxford Street is a secular cathedral of consumerism: people worship at an altar of things they can hold, mistaking the holding for the meaning.

## Features

- **Palace of Westminster** — seat of Parliament; sessions begin without invocation, debate proceeds without prayer
- **St. Paul's Cathedral** — partly a museum, partly a church; a monument to a faith that is no longer the organising principle of public life
- **Oxford Street** — the high street of consumerism; shops, advertisements, the endless liturgy of buying and selling
- **The City** — financial district; towers of glass and steel that represent an economy built on consumption
- **Whitehall** — seat of government; the Prime Minister's office at 10 Downing Street

## Inhabitants

Modern Londoners — a population that has forgotten God not through rejection but through distraction. They are not evil. They are not damned. They are asleep.

## Current Events

[[Bishop Aldric]] visited the city — the first Faithful cleric to enter the modern world. He found it more terrifying than any Heretic trench. He is staying to understand more.
