# Cautious Secular Engagement

## Summary
The policy doctrine established by PM [[Stephen Davenport]] at the COBR meeting: treat the Faithful Crown as a foreign power with unusual characteristics, refuse theological entanglement, maintain secular neutrality — while preparing for the possibility that the Faithful will not accept secularism as a valid framework for engagement.

## Details

Announced at the conclusion of the COBR meeting (Chapter 04), the doctrine has four pillars:

1. **Diplomatic, not theological.** Accept King Robert's embassy invitation, but the mandate is confined to boundaries, protocols, threat assessment, and peaceful coexistence. No discussion of the ark, divine purpose, or any cosmic narrative.
2. **Defensive military posture.** Full contingency planning for Heretic incursion, but rules of engagement remain defensive. No first strike. No provocation.
3. **Information control.** A prepared public communications strategy that strips the encounter of theological framing — "contact with a parallel human civilisation," full stop.
4. **No entanglement.** The UK is a secular democracy, not an instrument of Providence. Faithful beliefs about the nature of this world are their affair and will not shape UK policy.

## Context

Davenport's course is correct procedure and likely politically necessary — his National Unity Government is fragile, the domestic crisis that created it still unresolved, and any hint of theocratic entanglement could shatter the coalition. But the doctrine contains a built-in contradiction: [[Peter Chisholm|Archbishop Chisholm]] points out that the Faithful *cannot* accept secular engagement as a neutral position — to them, it is blindness. The ark theology will not be ignored. The doctrine is thus a holding action, destined for collision with Faithful expectations.
