# Germany

## Overview
The Federal Republic of Germany in the modern timeline — Europe's largest economy, a NATO member, and a parliamentary democracy whose constitution was designed, in the ashes of the Third Reich, to prevent precisely the kind of unchecked executive military action that the portal crisis demands.

## Position on the Anomaly
Germany's position is defined by constitutional constraint, not political will. [[Anna Strauss|Chancellor Strauss]] has made clear to [[Stephen Davenport|Davenport]] that Germany cannot commit troops to any portal-related military operation without a lengthy and uncertain parliamentary process:

- [[Grundgesetz|Article 87a]] (Parlamentsheer): Every Bundeswehr deployment requires a Bundestag mandate specifying scope, duration, rules of engagement, and troop cap. No existing legal framework covers "interdimensional theocratic contact" or "non-territorial supernatural threat." A new mandate would require committee hearings, floor debate, and a majority vote — weeks at best, months if contested.

- **Article 4(3) (Conscientious objection):** Absolute and non-waivable. Even if the Bundestag authorises deployment, individual soldiers can refuse to participate in "holy war" on grounds of conscience, and the constitution protects that refusal absolutely.

The Grundgesetz was written to prevent militarism — every safeguard that makes German democracy resilient also makes it slow. In a crisis that demands speed, Germany's constitution is an obstacle no government can override.

## What Germany Offers
Science. Diplomacy. Engineering. Strauss has proposed an [[International Civilian Agency for Portal Affairs]] — a non-military body staffed by scientists and diplomats, with a mandate to understand the portal before anyone decides what to do about it. Germany will contribute every resource it has except troops.
