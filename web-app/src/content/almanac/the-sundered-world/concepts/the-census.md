# The Census

## Summary

The Census is the mechanism by which the [[Kaelen Mandate]]'s population cap — no continuously built urban area may exceed one million souls — is monitored, enforced, and ritualised across the Scattered Realms. It is simultaneously a bureaucratic procedure and an instrument of state terror.

## Details

### Mechanics

Census-taking varies by nation but follows a common pattern:

- Annual or biennial counts conducted by dedicated census bureaus — often with secret-police authority.
- Population is measured within "continuously built urban areas," a definition that has generated centuries of legal scholarship. Green belts, artificial rivers, and dedicated "gap districts" are maintained to keep adjacent precincts legally separate.
- Exceeding the cap is a capital crime for the responsible officials — and potentially an extinction event for the city, as Kaelen's response to a violation would be a ballistic missile strike erasing the excess.

### Transmigration Lottery

When a city approaches the million-soul cap, the census bureau conducts a **transmigration lottery** — a random drawing of families designated for relocation to border towns, coastal garrisons, and expansion territories. The lottery is deliberately, meticulously indifferent:

- **Random by design.** Every household within the precinct is assigned a lottery number. The draw is conducted publicly, witnessed by census officials and observed by [[Silent Chorus|Kaelen's surveillance]] — or at least, conducted under the assumption that it is being observed.
- **The Beneficial Exception.** A family whose lottery number is drawn may be spared if the bureau determines that the household's head performs labour deemed *too beneficial to transmigrate* — a surgeon, a power-plant engineer, a census mathematician. The exception applies to the entire immediate family; the bureau does not split households. When a family is spared, the lottery is spun again for a replacement.
- **Never based on identity.** The bureau does not transmigrate — or spare — anyone based on looks, attitudes, personal beliefs, social standing, generational tenure, or political connections. A five-generation precinct family is no more or less likely to be selected than a couple who moved in last month. The lottery is the one institution in the Scattered Realms that is genuinely, terrifyingly fair.

### Compliance Strategies

- **[[Federated Crowns of Valdris|Valdran]] Model:** Cities organised into precincts of 999,000 precisely, separated by mandated green belts. Census bureau has secret-police powers to ensure no precinct tips over.
- **[[Technate of Vorr|Vorran]] Model:** Cities built vertically downward into mountains; population counted in catacomb districts whose legal status as "continuously built" is deliberately ambiguous.
- **[[Theocratic Union of the Last Flame|Theocratic]] Model:** Population-control edicts enforced through religious mandate. Infanticide is a grimly accepted policy tool to keep villages under limit.
- **Elite Evasion:** Wealthy citizens maintain multiple "country estates" that are practically small towns, connected by dedicated tram lines — a permanent, murmured debate as to when an estate becomes a city.

### Cultural Impact

Every child in the Scattered Realms learns that population is sacred arithmetic. "Count your children, count your neighbours, count your dead" is a nursery rhyme with a lethal subtext. Birth registries are kept with obsessive precision; stillbirths and infant mortality are reported in triplicate. The phrase "one too many" carries a weight no outsider can comprehend.

## Context

The Census is arguably the Mandate's cruelest article. Military caps constrain states; technology caps constrain progress. The population cap constrains *families*. It makes the most intimate human decisions — whether to have a child, whether to report a birth — matters of state security.
