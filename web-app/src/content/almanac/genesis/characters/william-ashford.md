# William Ashford

## Description

Composed, immaculate, mid-sixties. A man who spent thirty years in the diplomatic corps before politics claimed him. Speaks in the measured cadences of someone who believes every problem can be solved with the right communiqué and the correct channel of communication. Dovish by instinct, institutionalist by training.

## Background

Career diplomat before entering government — served as Ambassador to Washington, Permanent Under-Secretary at the Foreign Office. Appointed Foreign Secretary under the caretaker government that preceded the National Unity Government and retained the post through the unity settlement, a testament to his competence and the fact that no one else wanted the job. Believes in process, dialogue, and established channels. Treats the Faithful Crown as a foreign power with unusual characteristics, nothing more.

## Stance on the Anomaly

Ashford argues for cautious diplomatic engagement: accept the embassy, establish a communication protocol, treat the Faithful as a sovereign state. He wants to strip the theological dimension from the contact entirely and treat it as a matter of international relations. He is correct in procedure but, as [[Michael Grantham|Grantham]] points out, may be wrong about the nature of the entity he is negotiating with.

## Relationships

- [[Stephen Davenport]] — Supports Davenport's cautious course but wants the theology kept further from policy.
- [[Michael Grantham]] — Professional adversary. Ashford's dovish diplomacy and Grantham's hawkish defence instincts clash regularly, but both are professionals who keep the argument substantive.
