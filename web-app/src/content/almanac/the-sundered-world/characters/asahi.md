# Asahi

## Description

Seventeen years old. Dark hair worn short for practicality. Her hands move before she thinks — a childhood instinct her family called "the riverbank habit," catching things before they fall. She attends the Kedros Garrison upper school on the north side of town and works part-time at a small supermarket near the harbour, stocking shelves and counting ration cards for an elderly couple whose children were taken by the same lottery that brought her here. She doesn't learn customers' names because she doesn't plan to stay long enough to need them — but she knows the old woman's breathing pattern, the rattle in her chest on damp mornings, the way she leans on the counter when the fog rolls in.

## Background

Came from a family rooted in a Valdran precinct-city for five generations — ancestors buried in the same cemetery, streets named for relatives she'd never met. Selected for transmigration at age ten, her family relocated from [[Occidens]] to the contested border town of [[Kedros Garrison]]. The move severed her connection to every grave, every street, every story that made her who she was. She carries her Valdran identity like armour: proper pronunciation, precise paperwork, a small enamel pin of the precinct crest she wears under her collar where no one can see.

The transmigration taught her that the [[The Census|system]] is indifferent. She keeps her papers in order, her rent paid, her plans tentative. She does not wait for anyone. She does not plant roots.

## Relationships

- [[Akahito]] — childhood best friend from the precinct. They were separated when her family was selected for transmigration. He was the gentle one, the one who noticed when she hurt. She hasn't spoken to him in six years.
- Family — mother works at the garrison laundry; father is a railway signalman. A younger brother who was *born* in Kedros, who has never seen the precinct and doesn't understand why his sister still mourns a city she left at ten.

## Current Status

Working at a supermarket in Kedros Garrison, serving ration cards to the same garrison soldiers whose transport trains rumble past the window every evening. Her family's name is flagged for secondary relocation — the Five-Year Tension is overdue and the census bureau is clearing the transmigrant district as a precaution. The harbour is crowded with warships. The garrison is at double complement. She has a choice to make, and she's been making it alone.
