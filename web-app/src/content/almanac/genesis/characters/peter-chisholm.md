# Peter Chisholm

## Description

Short, broad, northern — looks like a man who did physical work before the Church claimed him. Grey hair, reading glasses constantly polished on his sleeve. Wears a simple black clerical shirt under a grey jacket; no mitre, no crozier, no ceremony. Speaks plainly, with the authority of someone who has thought deeply about things most people prefer to avoid. Evangelical-tradition Anglican, but wears it lightly. The first Archbishop of Canterbury from a genuinely working-class background in a generation.

## Background

Appointed Archbishop of Canterbury in 2026, succeeding Justin Welby. Rose through the Church as a parish priest and later a bishop in the North of England, where he acquired a reputation for plain speaking, theological depth, and an indifference to the niceties of ecclesiastical politics. His appointment was seen as a signal that the Church intended to reconnect with ordinary people after a period of institutional crisis. He was not the establishment's first choice, but he was the choice the moment demanded.

## Personality

- Direct, unpretentious, allergic to cant.
- Thinks before he speaks, but speaks fully when he does.
- Holds his faith with the intensity of someone who has wrestled with doubt and come out the other side.
- Has no interest in being the government's chaplain. Sees his role as the Church's conscience, not the state's legitimiser.

## Theological Position

Chisholm is an evangelical Anglican, but his encounter with the Faithful delegation is forcing him into territory no Anglican theologian has ever had to map. He is drawn to the "Deus Absconditus" — the hidden God — and sees modern Earth's spiritual silence not as absence but as design. His private question to Davenport after the COBR meeting — "what if the Faithful are not wrong, only mistaken about what it means?" — is arguably the most philosophically dangerous idea in the story: that the Faithful have correctly identified the ark, but have the direction of salvation reversed.

## Relationships

- [[Stephen Davenport]] — Prime Minister. Chisholm respects Davenport's secular integrity more than he expected to. Their private exchange is the beginning of an alliance neither man quite understands yet.
- [[Daniel Foster]] — Has read the transcript of Foster's conversation with Beauchamp. Recognises Foster as a kindred theological spirit — a man of the hidden God.
- [[Father Beauchamp]] — Has not met Beauchamp, but has read his report and transcript. Regards Beauchamp's "ark" theology with wary respect — more threatened by its plausibility than by any error in it.

## Current Status

At Lambeth Palace, reading everything the Church's theological advisors can compile on the Faithful, the Anomaly, and the physics of two worlds. Preparing for the day when the question he raised with Davenport becomes public — because he knows it will.
