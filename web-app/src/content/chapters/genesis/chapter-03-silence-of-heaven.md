# Chapter 03 – Silence of Heaven

## Summary

Aboard *[[HMS Dragon]]*, [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] requests a man of the cloth — and meets [[Daniel Foster|Reverend Daniel Foster]], the ship's Anglican chaplain. Their conversation becomes the first theological exchange between the two worlds: [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] describes the tangible, daily reality of divine intervention — miracles, the power of [[Orichalcum]], the presence of saints — while [[Daniel Foster|Foster]] speaks of a hidden God whose silence is an act of grace. Horrified and fascinated, [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] arrives at a dangerous conclusion: perhaps God hid this world from the Adversary to preserve a remnant of uncorrupted faith. Perhaps they are [[The Ark|the ark]]. At the [[Fortress of the White Cliffs]], [[Caius Blackwood|Inquisitor-Admiral Blackwood]] receives a royal decree ordering him to treat the strangers as brothers under God and extend an invitation to London — planting the official seed of Britannia's future worshipful stance. The chapter ends at [[The Portal Threshold]], where [[Rebecca Osei|Osei]], preparing to cross, receives [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]]'s trembling blessing: she goes to a world that has seen the face of the Enemy, and he does not know if God will be with her — but perhaps she will bring Him back.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: *[[HMS Dragon]]* — The Request

**[TIME]: Day 2 — 2026-06-19, 17:45 Zulu**
**[POV]: [[Father Beauchamp]]*

The modern warship was a temple of silence.

[[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] had been aboard *[[HMS Dragon|HMS Dragon]]* for nine hours. He had been swabbed for pathogens, scanned by machines that read his bones without touching them, interviewed by intelligence officers in civilian clothes who asked gentle, relentless questions about the Adversary, the war, the nature of his world. He had answered with the patience of a man who had testified before the Synods. He had watched [[Edmund Lassiter|Doctor-Abbot Lassiter]] grow almost giddy over the data streaming from his portable galvanometers — the old man chattering about "aetheric null signatures" and "baseline sanctity readings" with the zeal of a child who had found a new star. He had eaten food that tasted of nothing — not because it was bad, but because it had no blessing on it, no prayer spoken over the galley, no chrism in the salt. He had felt, for the first time in his ordained life, utterly unmoored.

The ship hummed around him. Not the creak of timber and the throb of coal-fired engines, but a low electric drone, a constant presence like the breath of a sleeping beast. The lights did not flicker with the motion of the sea. The air was sterile and cool. There were no crucifixes, no reliquaries, no watch-chaplains pacing the decks with aspergillum and censer. The crew were polite, efficient, and spiritually naked.

He found [[James Hawthorne|Captain Hawthorne]] on the bridge — a glass-walled chamber filled with glowing panels and screens that showed the sea, the sky, the portal, the positions of every ship in the cordon, all at once, in colours that did not exist in nature.

"Captain," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] said. "I wish to speak to a man of the cloth. A chaplain. A priest. Whatever you call them here."

[[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]] turned from the tactical display. He was a younger captain than [[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] — sharper in bearing, less burdened by the weight of holy war — but the same careful intelligence showed in his face. "We have a chaplain aboard. Reverend [[Daniel Foster|Foster]]. Anglican. He's…" [[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]] paused, searching for the right word. "He's a good man. Thoughtful. Not what you're used to, I suspect."

"I suspect nothing," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] said. "I only wish to understand."

[[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]] nodded and spoke into a device on his wrist — a tiny radio, [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] realised, small enough to fit in a locket. "Foster to the bridge, please. You've got a visitor."

While they waited, [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] looked out through the great windows at the other sea — his sea — shimmering through [[The Anomaly|the portal]]. *[[HMS Resolute|HMS Resolute]]* was visible as a dark silhouette against the bruised sky of the TC dawn. She looked, from this distance, impossibly ancient: a ship of the line from a bygone century, her funnels pouring black smoke into a sky that had never known satellites. And yet she was his. She was home.

The door opened. The man who entered was not what [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] expected — though he had not known what to expect. [[Daniel Foster|Foster]] was compact, balding, somewhere in his middle forties. He wore a simple blue uniform with a cross on the lapel, no larger than a thumb. No vestments. No armour. No [[Orichalcum]]. He carried a small black book in one hand and a mug of tea in the other. He looked like a man who had been called away from paperwork, not like a priest preparing to face the unknown.

"You're the one from the other side," [[Daniel Foster|Foster]] said. It was not a question.

"I am [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]], chaplain of His Majesty's Ship *[[HMS Resolute|Resolute]]*, ordained priest of the [[Sacred Order of the Dragon]]. I have crossed the threshold to learn what I can of your world. And I find I have questions that your scientists and your intelligence officers cannot answer. They are questions of God."

[[Daniel Foster|Foster]] took a slow sip of his tea. His expression was unreadable — not hostile, not afraid, but deeply, professionally alert. "Then we should probably find somewhere quieter than the bridge."

### Scene 2: *[[HMS Dragon]]* — The Dialogue

**[TIME]: Day 2 — 2026-06-19, 18:10 Zulu / 1914-09-15 TC, 12:10 hours**
**[POV]: [[Father Beauchamp]]*

They sat in the wardroom, a functional space with metal tables and bolted-down chairs, a photograph of the King on the wall — a different King, a different Crown, but the same Windsor bloodline, if [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] understood the genealogy correctly. The porthole looked out onto the grey Atlantic and the shimmering wall of [[The Anomaly|the portal]] beyond. The tea was hot and flavourless.

"Do you have saints in your world?" [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] began.

[[Daniel Foster|Foster]] considered the question with the care of a man who had been asked many things by many sailors but never this. "We have saints in the sense of historical figures. People who lived lives of exceptional holiness. We venerate them. We name churches after them. But they don't…" He paused. "They don't intervene. They don't appear. We don't expect them to."

"Ours do." [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] leaned forward, his armoured vestments creaking softly. "I have seen Saint George manifest above the battlefield at Cordoba. I have felt the presence of Saint Michael in the moment before a broadside. I have witnessed a martyr's relic stop a Heretic shell in mid-flight and shatter it into salt. These are not metaphors, Reverend. They are facts of our existence. The divine is not hidden in our world. It is *present*. Tangible. Daily."

[[Daniel Foster|Foster]] set down his mug. His face was thoughtful but not disbelieving — a man listening to a patient describe symptoms that fell outside his medical textbooks. "And the Adversary? Hell? You say those are tangible too."

"Every soul in our world bears the mark of one or the other," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] said. "The Rite of Discernment can read it. I have cast the Rite a hundred times. I know the feel of infernal taint — a pressure in the mind, a cold that has nothing to do with temperature. I know the feel of divine grace — warmth, order, the sense of being known. Every Faithful soldier carries that grace. Every Heretic carries the stain. There is no neutral ground. Creation itself is contested."

"And when you cast this Rite on Lieutenant Commander [[Rebecca Osei|Osei]]?" [[Daniel Foster|Foster]] asked quietly.

[[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] was silent for a long moment. "Nothing. No taint. No grace. Her soul is a blank page. And so, I believe, are all of yours."

The word hung in the sterile air: *blank*. It was the most frightening word [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] had ever spoken.

[[Daniel Foster|Foster]] nodded slowly, as if this confirmed something he had been trying to formulate. "We have a concept in our theology. The *Deus Absconditus*. The hidden God. It goes back to the prophets — 'Truly, you are a God who hides himself.' The idea that God's silence, His absence from visible affairs, is not a failure of His power but an expression of His nature. That faith requires no proof. That the absence of miracles is not the absence of God, but the space God gives us to choose freely."

[[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] stared at him. "You *choose* to believe in a God who does nothing?"

"We choose to believe in a God who has already done everything that matters," [[Daniel Foster|Foster]] said. "The Incarnation. The Cross. The Resurrection. After that, what more proof do we need? Everything else is just… special effects."

"Special effects," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] repeated, the words tasting strange in his mouth. "You would call the intervention of the Almighty in the hour of our greatest need a *special effect*?"

"I would call it a sign and a wonder, if I saw it," [[Daniel Foster|Foster]] said evenly. "But I have never seen it. No one I know has ever seen it. And we have been taught that the absence of such signs is not a curse — it is a test. Blessed are those who have not seen, and yet have believed."

[[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] had read that verse a thousand times. He had preached it to doubting ratings in the dark hours before battle. But he had never encountered a whole world that lived it. A world of eight billion souls who believed, or did not believe, with no flame above their heads to guide them. A world where faith was a leap into the dark, not a hand clasped in the light.

"Your faith is…" [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] began. He stopped. He could think of many words. *Foolish.* *Brave.* *Impossible.* None of them were right. "Yours is purer than mine," he said at last. "You have faith without evidence. We have faith *because* of evidence. We do not choose to believe in God's power — we have seen it. But we did not choose to believe in the Adversary either; we have seen him too. Your world has never been forced to believe in anything. Your souls are free."

He fell silent. The ship hummed around them. On the other side of [[The Anomaly|the portal]], the TC dawn was darkening into the bruised grey of an Atlantic afternoon. Somewhere on *[[HMS Resolute|Resolute]]*, the ship's bell would be ringing the next watch.

Then the thought came to him — not as a deduction, not as a theological argument, but as a sudden, terrifying clarity, like a candle lit in a catacomb.

"Reverend [[Daniel Foster|Foster]]," he said, and his voice had changed — lower, more urgent. "What if God hid you?"

[[Daniel Foster|Foster]] blinked. "I'm sorry?"

"The Adversary. The Tyrants of Hell. They entered our world at Jerusalem in 1099. They found the faith of Christendom and set themselves against it. They have contested every soul, every nation, every battlefield for eight hundred years. But your world — your world diverged from ours at that same moment. The portal opened. Hell took our world… and it never touched yours. Why?"

[[Daniel Foster|Foster]]'s face had gone very still. "You're asking me to speculate about the will of God."

"I am asking you to consider a possibility," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] said. "What if the Creator, in His infinite foresight, saw what would happen in Jerusalem in 1099? What if He knew that the Adversary would pour through the breach and consume creation — and so He made a second creation. A hidden world. A world sealed away from the war. A remnant. An ark."

"An ark," [[Daniel Foster|Foster]] repeated. He did not sound convinced. He sounded like a man who had just been handed a theological bomb and was trying very hard not to drop it.

"An ark," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] said. "A world preserved from the flood of fire. A world where faith could grow without the pressure of Hell's visible hand — where souls could choose God freely, without the coercion of miracles or the terror of the Tyrants. A world that has never been tested, so that its faith could be… pure. Uncannily, impossibly pure."

"Father," [[Daniel Foster|Foster]] said carefully, "you are describing my world as a kind of museum. A garden kept behind glass. And I have to tell you — we don't feel preserved. We feel… ordinary. We have wars. We have atrocities. We have invented horrors that require no demons at all. If we are God's remnant, He has a strange way of showing it."

"All remnants suffer," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] said. "Suffering is not proof of damnation. The question is not whether your world suffers. The question is whether your suffering has a *design* behind it — or whether it is simply the chaos of a creation that has been left to itself. And I am telling you, Reverend, that the absence of the Adversary in your world is not an accident. It is a sign. Perhaps the most important sign in eight centuries."

[[Daniel Foster|Foster]] was quiet for a long time. He looked out the porthole at the portal, that shimmering scar between worlds. When he spoke again, his voice was softer, stripped of its professional calm.

"You're telling me that my world — my ordinary, secular, doubt-filled world — might be the only thing God has managed to keep safe. The only place the Devil never reached."

"Yes," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] said.

"And you're telling me that you — a priest from a world of miracles and saints and visible divine power — are jealous of us."

[[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] met his eyes. "Yes."

The word hung between them like a bell struck once.

"I have wielded [[Orichalcum]] that was forged in prayer," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] said quietly. "I have blessed battleships that bled holy oil when struck. I have felt the hand of the Almighty on my shoulder in the instant before a shell passed through the space where my head had been. And every night of my life, I go to sleep knowing that the Adversary is real, that Hell is real, that the war is eternal, and that I may wake to find a [[Grail-spore]] blooming in my chest because some Heretic's prayer found its mark while I was not watching. Your world has none of that. Your world is quiet. And I am only now beginning to understand what a terrible, wonderful gift that is."

[[Daniel Foster|Foster]] said nothing. He reached for his mug, found it empty, and set it down again.

"Father [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]]," he said at last. "If what you're saying is true — if my world is an ark, a preserved remnant — then you are also saying something else. You're saying that God has not abandoned your world. He simply… divided it. Put the war in one place and the seed in another. And if the ark is ever opened…"

"Then the seed must be planted," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] finished. "Your world may be the ark. But my world may be the flood. And the ark was not built to float forever."

Neither man spoke for a long time after that. The ship hummed. The portal shimmered. And somewhere, in the deeps between the worlds, a theology was being born that neither Synod nor Parliament would know how to contain.

### Scene 3: [[Fortress of the White Cliffs]] — The Crown's Decree

**[TIME]: Day 3 — 1914-09-16 TC, 07:30 hours**
**[POV]: [[Caius Blackwood]]*

The King's seal was crimson wax the size of a fist, stamped with the Crown and Cross of England, threaded with a scarlet ribbon. The courier — a young officer of the Royal Household, still breathing hard from the climb up the fortress stairs — had delivered it at first light, riding through the night from London on a special train that had displaced all other traffic on the Dover line. The Crown did not wait for ordinary despatch.

[[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] broke the seal with his thumb, the wax shattering cleanly across the oak of his map table. The parchment was heavy, the script the formal pointed hand of the Palace scribes, but the signature at the bottom — *Robert Rex* — was unmistakably the King's own, a bold, slashing autograph that had signed declarations of war and peace treaties alike.

He read:

*To Our Trusted and Well-Beloved Inquisitor-Admiral Caius Blackwood, Grand Commander of the Faithful Fleet, Guardian of the White Cliffs, greetings in the name of Almighty God and of His Most Holy Church.*

*We have received and studied with great care your despatches concerning the apparition in the Celtic Sea and the world which lies beyond it. We have consulted the Synod of the Unified Church, the Lords of the Admiralty, and the Privy Council in extraordinary session. The judgement of the Crown is this:*

*That the strangers who have appeared beyond the portal are our lost kin, separated from us by the mysterious providence of God at the hour of the First Fall, and preserved through centuries unknown to us. That they are not servants of the Adversary. That they are not enemies of the Faithful Crown. That they are, by every sign and discernment available to us, men and women of the same blood, the same language, and the same England — an England that has never known the holy war, and whose ignorance is not a sin but a mercy.*

*Therefore we command you to treat these strangers as brothers under God. Extend to them every courtesy of the Faithful Crown. Invite them to send a formal embassy to our court in London, that we may receive them in person and learn the full measure of what Providence has delivered to us. Furnish them with such knowledge of our world as they may require, and receive from them such knowledge as they may offer. Let no sword be raised against them, nor any Rite of banishment cast, unless they first move with hostile intent — which, by the grace of God, we do not expect.*

*These strangers may be His instrument. We do not yet know the shape of that instrument. But we will not meet Providence with a closed fist.*

*Given at the Palace of Whitehall, this fifteenth day of September, in the Year of Our Lord nineteen hundred and fourteen, by Our own hand.*

*Robert Rex*

[[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] read the letter twice. Then he set it down on the map table with the care of a man handling a live shell.

"'His instrument,'" he murmured. "He thinks they are a weapon."

[[Morgan Pell|Pell]], who had been standing silently at the window watching the dawn bleed across the Channel, turned. "My Lord Inquisitor-Admiral?"

"The King's language." [[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] tapped the parchment. "'These strangers may be His instrument.' He does not say 'our brothers.' He does not say 'our friends.' He says 'His instrument.' The King is not welcoming them. He is *claiming* them."

[[Morgan Pell|Pell]] crossed the chamber and read the decree over [[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]]'s shoulder. His thin face tightened. "The Synod concurred with this language?"

"The Synod wrote half of it, I'd wager." [[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] straightened, his gaunt frame rigid against the grey light. "They were always going to see this as providence. Eight hundred years of holy war. Eight hundred years of sacrifice and martyrdom and prayer. And now, suddenly, an untouched world appears — a world full of souls who have never known Hell, who have never signed a pact or knelt to a Tyrant. Of course the Synod sees them as an instrument. What else would they be? Uncorrupted humanity, delivered to the Faithful's doorstep at the very hour the war enters its ninth century?"

He turned to the window. The Channel was grey and restless below, whitecaps catching the early light. On the horizon, the seaward batteries of the fortress sat silent, their Orichalcum-alloy barrels aimed south toward Heretic Gibraltar.

"[[Morgan Pell|Pell]], do you know what 'an instrument of Providence' means in the language of the Synod?"

[[Morgan Pell|Pell]] hesitated. "It means a vessel through which God accomplishes His will."

"It means a thing that is used," [[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] said. "A tool. A weapon. A sacrifice. The Synod reads Scripture the way an engineer reads a schematic: it sees parts that fit together to achieve an outcome. It does not ask what the parts want. It asks what the parts *do*."

"And the other world? What does it want?"

[[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] looked at the parchment again. The King's signature glowed in the thin morning light. "They want to understand. That is all they have asked for — understanding. And we are about to hand them an invitation to London wrapped in the language of divine purpose. They will not see providence. They will see presumption."

He walked to his writing desk — a heavy piece of oak scarred by decades of use, the surface marked with rings from coffee cups and chrism vials. He drew a fresh sheet of paper and dipped his pen.

"I will obey the King," he said. "I will extend every courtesy. I will invite their embassy to London. But I will also write a second letter — a private one, for [[Alistair Pembroke|Captain Pembroke]]'s eyes only. He has met these people face to face. He knows their tenor. I will ask him to watch for the moment when our reverence becomes their cage."

[[Morgan Pell|Pell]] said nothing. The silence in the chamber was the silence of a man learning that even the Crown could not be trusted with the implications of its own theology.

[[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] wrote for a long time. Outside, the fortress stirred to life — the clang of bells, the tramp of boots on chalk-cut stone, the distant crackle of a wireless set picking up the morning's signal traffic from the picket line. The world was waking to a truth it had not yet learned to name.

And in the heart of the white cliffs, the Inquisitor-Admiral wrote his warning.

### Scene 4: [[The Portal Threshold]] — The Blessing

**[TIME]: Day 3 — 1914-09-16 TC, 10:00 hours / 2026-06-20, 16:00 Zulu**
**[POV]: Lieutenant Commander [[Rebecca Osei]]*

The order had come from [[Northwood Headquarters|Northwood]] at 08:00 Zulu, routed through [[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]] on *[[HMS Dragon|Dragon]]*. It was extraordinary, unprecedented, and very likely illegal under half a dozen treaties and standing orders. It had been approved by the Prime Minister personally, after a late-night call with the Palace and a heated exchange with the Foreign Secretary and the Archbishop of Canterbury in a conference chamber that smelled of stale coffee and institutional panic.

[[Rebecca Osei|Osei]] was to cross the threshold.

She was to become the first human being from modern Earth to step into the Trench Crusade timeline. No weapons. No escort beyond a single boat crew who would remain at the boundary. Her mission: to present formal greetings from His Majesty's Government to the authorities of the Faithful Crown, to establish a protocol for regular communication, and — unspoken but understood by everyone in the chain of command — to see the other side with her own eyes and come back sane enough to report on it.

She had volunteered. Of course she had volunteered.

Now she stood on the deck of the RIB, the same rigid-hulled craft that had carried her to the first parley two days earlier, the same crew around her, the same cold Atlantic spray on her face. The portal loomed ahead — a mile-wide curtain of silvered light, humming at the edge of hearing. On the far side, *[[HMS Resolute|Resolute]]* waited. And on this side, a figure in armoured vestments had come down to the water's edge — or rather, to the *[[HMS Dragon|Dragon]]*'s boat deck — to see her off.

[[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] looked older than he had two days ago. The crossing had aged him. The conversation with [[Daniel Foster|Foster]] had aged him more. But his eyes were clear, and when he raised his [[Orichalcum]] crucifix, the light of the portal caught it and ran along the blade-like edges of the metal like water.

"Lieutenant Commander [[Rebecca Osei|Osei]]," he said. His archaic English carried easily across the noise of the sea and the hum of the anomaly. "You are about to do what no soul of your world has ever done. You are about to walk into the war."

"I know," she said.

"You do not know." His voice was not harsh, but it was absolute. "You have heard me describe it. You have read the despatches. But you have not smelled the brimstone on the wind off Gibraltar. You have not heard the sound a man makes when a [[Grail-spore]] blooms in his chest and the chaplain has to choose between prayer and the mercy blade. You have not felt the pressure of a Tyrant's attention on your mind — the sense that something vast and hungry has noticed you and is deciding whether you are worth the trouble."

[[Rebecca Osei|Osei]] said nothing. She had learned, in the two days since first contact, that [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] was not a man who spoke for effect. He spoke because the weight of what he had seen demanded words, and silence would have been a lie.

"I do not know if God exists in your world," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] said. "Your chaplain — [[Daniel Foster|Foster]] — has a faith I do not understand. It is a faith that expects nothing. It is a faith that has been so long without proof that it has forgotten proof was ever possible. And yet it endures. This is a mystery I am not priest enough to unravel."

He lowered the crucifix and pressed it into her hands. The metal was warm — warmer than it should have been, as if it carried some residual heat from its forging, or from the prayers that had been spoken over it.

"This is [[Orichalcum]]," he said. "It is the metal of our world. It was blessed by the Patriarch of New Antioch and forged in the furnaces of the [[Moving Fortress of Britannia]]. It has turned aside shells and spells. It has witnessed the deaths of martyrs and the births of miracles. I am giving it to you. Not as a gift. As a test. If God is absent from your world, then this crucifix will do nothing on the far side — it will be cold metal, and you will know that your world's silence is final. But if it warms in your hand when you cross… if it does what it has always done, even in the absence of any Rite to sustain it… then perhaps He has not abandoned you after all. Perhaps He has simply been waiting."

[[Rebecca Osei|Osei]] closed her fingers around the crucifix. It was heavy. The silvered surface was etched with prayers in Latin she could not read, and the edges were sharp enough to cut. She had never held a religious object that felt like a weapon before. She was not sure she wanted to. But she was also not sure she could let it go.

"Father," she said, and her voice was steady even though her heart was not, "are you saying I might bring God back with me?"

[[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] looked at her for a long moment. His eyes — grey, tired, ancient in a face that was not yet old — held something she had not seen in any of the priests or chaplains of her own world. It was not certainty. It was not doubt. It was the raw, bleeding edge between the two.

"I am saying that you go to a world that has seen the face of the Enemy," he said. "I do not know if God will be with you there, for He has been absent from yours. But perhaps…" He paused. His voice cracked, just slightly — the first time she had heard it falter. "Perhaps you will bring Him back."

He raised his hand — trembling, she saw, the hand of a man who had cast too many Rites, who had blessed too many dying, who had asked God for too much and received answers that were not answers. He made the sign of the cross over her, the gesture slow and deliberate, as if he were tracing it not in air but in stone.

"*In nomine Patris, et Filii, et Spiritus Sancti.* Go. And may the God who hides Himself in your world choose this day to be found."

[[Rebecca Osei|Osei]] nodded. She tucked the crucifix into the pocket of her life vest, where it sat heavy against her ribs. Then she turned to her boat crew and gave the order to cast off.

The RIB's engine muttered to life — they would use power to the threshold and kill it at the boundary, the same ritual of neutrality that both sides had improvised in the first hours of contact. Behind her, *[[HMS Dragon|Dragon]]*'s grey superstructure dwindled. Ahead, the shimmering wall of [[The Anomaly|the portal]] grew until it filled the horizon, a curtain of light so vast that she could no longer see the edges.

As the boat nosed toward the boundary, [[Rebecca Osei|Osei]] put her hand in her pocket and wrapped her fingers around the [[Orichalcum]] crucifix. The metal was still warm. Warmer than it should have been.

She did not know yet whether that warmth was a property of the metal, or a property of the prayer that had been spoken over it, or something else entirely. She only knew that she was about to cross into a world where God was visible and Hell was real, and she was carrying with her the first piece of proof that perhaps — just perhaps — her world was not as empty as it seemed.

The curtain rippled. The boat slid through.

And on the far side, the sky was the colour of old blood.

## New Concepts Introduced

- [[The Ark]] — the theological concept that modern Earth was deliberately hidden by God from the Adversary as a preserved remnant of uncorrupted faith, an "ark" meant to survive the flood of holy war
- [[Daniel Foster]] — Royal Navy chaplain aboard *[[HMS Dragon|HMS Dragon]]*, representing the modern "hidden God" theology of faith without visible proof
- [[Deus Absconditus]] — the modern theological concept of the hidden God, whose silence is an act of grace rather than abandonment
- [[King Robert the Longsword]] — sovereign of the Faithful Crown of England, who issues the decree of hospitality
