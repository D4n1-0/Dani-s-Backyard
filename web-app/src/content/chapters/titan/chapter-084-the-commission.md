# Chapter 08.4 – The Commission

## Summary

Three weeks have passed. The south bank compound has its pier. It has its helipad. It has its first barracks and its first clinic and its first radio tower — a skeleton of steel and aluminium rising from the flat grassland, flanked by the grey shapes of stacked containers and the yellow lattice of the mobile crane. And the military faction, having lost the frigate and the paper war, has found a new weapon: procedure. [[Hoffmann|General [[Hoffmann]]]], the Chairman of the General Staff, arrives at Odiha by train to conduct a formal compliance review of the MOU implementation. He is accompanied by a commissioner's brief written by [[Strobl|Admiral Strobl]], who has argued — correctly, if disingenuously — that the presence of a foreign carrier strike group in Marleyan-adjacent waters exceeds the authority granted to a single field commander acting on a three-paragraph endorsement. The review is not a trial. It is a performance. [[Hoffmann]] needs to be seen doing something so that he can continue doing nothing. And Magath needs to make sure the performance ends with the MOU intact. Across three days, he will walk the compound with the chairman, answer questions about Paradis, concede a military faction observer to the south bank, and watch the first forty CATF personnel arrive by Black Hawk to take possession of the base they have been building for six weeks.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The Telegram — Day ~190

The telegraph key clattered in the Port Authority's communications room at 09:00, and the message that came through was longer than any message the clerk had processed since the day the capital endorsed Magath.

It was addressed to Commander Magath, personal and immediate. It was signed by [[Hoffmann|General [[Hoffmann]]]], Chairman of the General Staff. It ran to five paragraphs, and each paragraph was a door opening into a room that Magath did not want to enter.

*Paragraph one:* The General Staff had reviewed the recent events at the Odiha river approaches, including the presence of foreign naval assets in waters adjacent to Marleyan territory. The review was preliminary. The findings were not yet final. A formal commission would be convened.

*Paragraph two:* [[Hoffmann|General [[Hoffmann]]]] would travel to Odiha personally to conduct the commission. The commission's scope included, but was not limited to: the terms of the MOU, the operational boundaries of Commander Magath's negotiating authority, the presence of foreign military vessels in Marleyan-adjacent waters, and the undisclosed Paradisian engagement that Dr. Ishihara had acknowledged during the second negotiation session. Witnesses would be called. Testimony would be taken. A report would be filed.

*Paragraph three:* The commission was not a disciplinary proceeding. Commander Magath's authority remained in effect pending the commission's findings. The construction of the south bank compound was not suspended. The commission's purpose was to clarify, not to obstruct.

*Paragraph four:* [[Strobl|Admiral Strobl]] of the Home Fleet had requested that a representative of the naval command be included in the commission's proceedings. The request was under review. Commander Magath would be notified of the commission's composition before the chairman's arrival.

*Paragraph five:* [[Hoffmann|General [[Hoffmann]]]] would arrive by express military train within five days. Commander Magath was instructed to prepare a full briefing on the status of the MOU implementation and to make the south bank compound available for inspection.

Magath read the message three times. The language was [[Hoffmann]]'s — formal, precise, constructed to give the military faction the appearance of a victory without granting them the substance. The commission was real. The review would happen. And [[Strobl]], having failed with paper and with frigates, was now using the General Staff's own procedures as his weapon.

[[Konrad Verner|Verner]] was at his desk when Magath walked in. The admiral had been reading the same message — the telegraph room distributed copies to the port command as a matter of routine, and Verner had been on the distribution list for thirty years. He looked up when Magath entered, and the look on his face was the look of a man who had seen this kind of message before and knew exactly what it meant.

"The military faction has found a new lever," Verner said. "They could not stop the construction with permits. They could not stop it with a frigate. They are now trying to stop it with a committee."

"They are not trying to stop it. They are trying to control it. The commission is a mechanism to restrict my authority — to redefine my negotiating brief in terms that [[Strobl]] can influence. [[Hoffmann]] will arrive. He will tour the compound. He will ask questions. He will file a report. And the report will include conditions that the military faction has demanded as the price of continued General Staff support."

"Conditions such as."

"An observer. A military faction liaison stationed at the south bank — a pair of eyes that reports to [[Strobl]], not to me. Access to the CATF communications relay so that the naval command can monitor coalition traffic. A seat at the negotiation table for the Titan Security Division, because [[Richter]] has been arguing for months that the MOU bypasses the empire's primary strategic asset."

Verner leaned back. "[[Hoffmann]] will not grant all of those. [[Hoffmann]] is not [[Strobl]]'s ally. He is a survivor. He will give [[Strobl]] enough to claim a victory and you enough to keep the MOU intact. The question is where he draws the line."

"The line is the observer. An observer is a concession I can sell to Ishihara. A naval representative monitoring coalition communications — she will not accept that. A seat for the Titan Security Division at the negotiation table — that contradicts the MOU's explicit exclusion of Titan-related technology from the military advisory cell. [[Strobl]] will demand all three. [[Hoffmann]] will grant one. The observer." Magath looked out the window. The south bank was visible from Verner's office now — not the details, but the shape of it, the radio tower rising against the mountains, the containers stacked on the bank, the yellow crane moving against the grey sky. "The commission is not a threat to the MOU. It is a test of whether I can manage the politics while the construction continues. The construction is the answer to every question the commission will ask. The pier is real. The helipad is real. The compound is real. [[Hoffmann]] will see it, and he will file a report that says the MOU is producing results, and the military faction will take their observer and claim they won."

"And if the report says otherwise."

"Then the MOU is reopened on the faction's terms, and I am recalled to the capital, and the coalition learns that Marley cannot honour an agreement for longer than six weeks." Magath turned from the window. "That is not going to happen."

"How do you know."

"Because I am going to give [[Hoffmann]] a tour that leaves him no choice but to endorse the MOU. Every question he asks — the compound has an answer. Every concern [[Strobl]] has raised — the compound is the rebuttal. The faction's case is built on paper. My case is built on concrete. And concrete, Admiral, is harder to argue with than paper."

### Scene 2: The Tour — Day ~195

[[Hoffmann|General [[Hoffmann]]]]'s train pulled into Odiha station at 10:00 on a cold clear morning, and the chairman of the Marleyan General Staff stepped onto the platform looking exactly like what he was: a seventy-year-old survivor of forty years of imperial politics, silver-haired, straight-backed, his uniform pressed to the crease, his eyes taking in the station and the harbour and the mountains to the north with the rapid, cataloguing assessment of a man who had been reading situations for longer than most of the officers under his command had been alive.

Magath met him at the platform. Verner stood beside him — the admiral's cane planted on the stone, his dress uniform worn for the first time in weeks, the Southern Command's insignia catching the winter sun. The station was quiet. The harbour was quiet. The only sound was the steam venting from the locomotive and the distant thrum of the dredge, still working the river mouth, still deepening the berth for the next logistics vessel.

"Commander Magath," [[Hoffmann]] said. The greeting was formal. The tone was not. The tone was the tone of a man who had been sent to do something he did not want to do and was going to do it anyway because the alternative was worse. "Your report from the *[[Steinadler]]* incident was thorough. [[Strobl|Admiral Strobl]]'s report was less thorough and considerably longer. I have read both. I am here to determine which one is accurate."

"The south bank compound is twenty minutes from here, General. You can determine that for yourself."

"I intend to." [[Hoffmann]] looked at Verner. "Admiral. You have been managing this situation since the Pelikan returned. I should have visited sooner."

"General," Verner said, "you are here now. That is what matters."

They walked. The route from the station to the south bank had changed in the six weeks since Magath had first walked it. The dirt path along the river was now a graded service road — gravel-surfaced, cambered for drainage, wide enough for two vehicles to pass. The CATF engineering detachment had laid it in three weeks using a grader that ran on diesel fuel and moved earth at a rate that Marleyan road crews would have considered mythical. The road was the first piece of permanent infrastructure the coalition had built on the AOT mainland — not a building, not a pier, not a radio tower, but the thing that connected them all, the artery that would carry supplies from the port to the compound for the next ninety-nine years.

[[Hoffmann]] walked the road without speaking. He was seventy years old, and the walk was twenty minutes, and he did not slow. He looked at the grader — parked at the roadside, its blade raised, its engine silent, the CATF operator drinking coffee from a metal cup and watching the general pass with the incurious gaze of a contractor who had been told to keep working and not engage. He looked at the river — the grey water moving south toward the sea, the dredge still churning, the channel markers bobbing at hundred-metre intervals. He looked at the mountains — the same mountains that Verner had looked at for four years, the same grey peaks, the same snow on the summits, the same permanence.

And then the road curved, and the compound opened before him, and [[Hoffmann]] stopped walking.

The pier was complete. Thirty metres of reinforced concrete extending into the river channel, supported on piles that had been driven forty metres into the riverbed by a pneumatic hammer that Marleyan engineers had heard from the port and could not identify. The deck was wide enough for two vehicles to pass — a logistics ramp at the shore end, a deep-water berth at the river end, a fuel line running beneath the deck to a bladder on the bank.

Tied up alongside the pier, her grey hull low against the concrete, her ensign — the rising sun of the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force flying beneath the four-ring CATF pennant — snapping in the harbour wind, was a warship.

She was not large. Not by the standards of the *Ford* or the *Tripoli* or the AEGIS destroyers that had accompanied the Maersk. She was a frigate — 133 metres, sleek, modern, her lines designed by computers and tested in wind tunnels, her weapons concealed in angular housings that Magath had learned were called "stealth panels" and that made the ship look like a blade folded into a box. She was the JS *Kumano* — a Mogami-class frigate of the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force, the first permanent CATF warship stationed on Marleyan soil. She had arrived three days earlier, relieving the *Thomas Hudner* of local escort duty, and her commanding officer — a Japanese captain named Nakamura, forty-five years old, his English fluent and his Marleyan functional — had presented his credentials to Verner with the quiet formality of a professional who understood that his presence was a statement.

[[Hoffmann]] looked at the frigate for a long time. The ship was real. The pier was real. The compound was real. Every argument that [[Strobl]] had made in the capital — that the construction was behind schedule, that the coalition was stalling, that the MOU was a diplomatic fiction with no physical manifestation — was refuted by the grey hull of a modern frigate tied to a concrete pier on the south bank of the Odiha river mouth.

"That ship," [[Hoffmann]] said, "was not mentioned in [[Strobl|Admiral Strobl]]'s report."

"[[Strobl|Admiral Strobl]]'s report was written before the ship arrived," Magath said. "The ship arrived three days ago. She is the JS *Kumano* — Japanese frigate, permanent forward deployment. Her mission is presence. She will patrol the southern approaches, coordinate with Marleyan naval command on search and rescue, and serve as the command platform for the CATF military advisory cell. She is not a combat deployment. She is a statement of permanence."

"A statement of permanence that [[Strobl|Admiral Strobl]] will interpret as a provocation."

"[[Strobl|Admiral Strobl]] interprets every statement as a provocation. The ship is here. The pier is here. The compound is here. The question for the commission is not whether these things exist — they exist. The question is whether the empire benefits from their existence. I believe the answer is yes. The commission will determine whether the General Staff agrees."

[[Hoffmann]] looked at the frigate a moment longer. Then he turned toward the compound.

"Show me the rest."

The helipad was a square of asphalt, freshly painted, the landing circle marked in white, a windsock streaming from a pole at the northern edge. The machine on the pad was not the small civilian helicopter that Ishihara had demonstrated for Magath six weeks earlier. It was a UH-60 Black Hawk — olive drab, twin engines, its rotors folded for maintenance, its crew chief walking the pre-flight inspection with a checklist on a tablet. The Black Hawk was a military aircraft — troop transport, cargo lift, medical evacuation, the workhorse of the coalition's rotary-wing fleet. Its presence on the helipad meant the compound was no longer a construction site with a landing zone. It was a military base with an air asset.

"The first permanent rotary-wing detachment," Magath said. "One Black Hawk, with a second scheduled within the month. Primary mission is logistics — cargo transfer from the port to the compound, medical evacuation, personnel transport. Secondary mission is search and rescue. The pilots are US Army, on CATF secondment. The crew chief is Korean. The aircraft itself is American. The multinational integration is deliberate."

[[Hoffmann]] walked around the helipad. He examined the fuel bladder — a collapsible container, the same design that Marleyan engineers had first seen on the deck of the *Southern Venture* six weeks earlier, now connected to the helipad by a permanent fuel line. He examined the maintenance shelter — a prefab structure, its walls lined with tools, its floor marked with the positions for each piece of ground support equipment. He examined the Black Hawk itself — the rotor hub, the engine intakes, the tail assembly, the cockpit instruments visible through the open crew door. He asked no questions. He did not need to. The answers were visible in every component of the machine — the precision, the power, the implicit threat of a technology that had been designed for war and was being used for construction.

They walked the barracks next. Four prefab buildings, each one capable of housing twenty personnel — eighty bunks, currently occupied by the advance engineering team, the medical detachment, and the first wave of the security complement. The buildings were heated by electric radiators. The water ran from a desalination unit on the riverbank. The lights were electric — the same clean white glow that had first appeared on the deck of the Envoy, now illuminating every room in every building on the compound. The canteen served food that had been shipped from Korea — rice, vegetables, fish, the kind of food that the CATF personnel had been eating for six months on Grande Terre and that the Marleyan engineers who had visited the compound for joint briefings had eaten without comment and then described to their colleagues with the specific awe of people who had never tasted food that had not been salted for preservation.

The clinic was the last stop. It was a single prefab building, smaller than the barracks, its interior divided into a waiting room, two examination bays, and a pharmacy. The pharmacy shelves were half-empty — the medical supplies that CATF had promised under the technology transfer agreement were still on the logistics vessels, still in transit, still weeks from arrival. But the examination bays were functional, and the waiting room was clean, and the Marleyan civilian who had arrived two days earlier with a broken arm — a fisherman from the Odiha waterfront, injured in a winch accident, brought to the compound by a CATF patrol boat because the port clinic was closed for the evening — had been treated by a Korean medic with a portable X-ray machine and a plaster cast and a bottle of antibiotics that would prevent the infection that would have killed him in any Marleyan hospital.

[[Hoffmann]] stopped in the clinic doorway. The fisherman was still there — sitting in the waiting room, his arm in its cast, his wife beside him, their children playing on the floor with a plastic toy that the medic had given them from a supply cupboard.

"The clinic treats Marleyan civilians," Magath said.

"I see that."

"When the technology transfer agreement is fully implemented — the medical supplies, the radio equipment, the advisory cell — the compound will be treating Marleyan patients, communicating with Marleyan ships, and training Marleyan officers on a daily basis. The coalition's presence is not a foreign occupation. It is an integration. The compound will become, over time, a facility that serves the empire as much as it serves the coalition. That is the vision behind the MOU. That is the vision that the military faction wants to kill."

[[Hoffmann]] looked at the fisherman. At the cast on his arm. At the children playing with the plastic toy. At the Korean medic who had treated a Marleyan civilian without asking for payment or identification or proof of loyalty.

"The military faction," [[Hoffmann]] said, "does not want to kill the vision. They want to control it. The difference is important. If they kill it, they lose the technology. If they control it, they gain the technology and the credit. The observer they have demanded — the liaison officer from [[Richter]]'s division — is their mechanism of control. Not a veto. Not a vote. A pair of eyes that reports to them."

Magath looked at the chairman. The old general's face was unreadable — the face of a man who had been balancing factions for forty years and was still alive because he had never committed to a position that could be proven wrong.

"General. What does the commission actually want."

"The commission wants to file a report that keeps the General Staff united. The military faction wants the report to include an observer. The cautious faction — Franke, Werner, the men who argued for restraint during the first session — wants the report to include a commitment to the technology transfer timeline. I want the report to include both, because both are necessary to maintain the coalition that signed the MOU and the coalition that will implement it." [[Hoffmann]] paused. The pause was the length of a calculation — a political calculation, the kind of calculation that had kept him alive for forty years. "You are going to accept the observer, Commander. You are going to give [[Anton Schreiber|Colonel Schreiber]] — [[Richter]]'s man, a Titan Security Division officer with no naval experience and no diplomatic training — an office in the Port Authority, a desk, a telegrapher, and daily access to the compound's perimeter. You are going to let him watch. You are going to let him report. And in exchange, the commission is going to endorse the MOU, recommend the acceleration of the technology transfer, and authorise the next phase of construction. That is the deal. That is the only deal that keeps the General Staff together."

Magath looked out the clinic window. The frigate was still at the pier. The Black Hawk was still on the helipad. The compound was still rising from the grassland. And somewhere in the capital, a Titan Security Division colonel was packing his bags for a train south.

"[[Anton Schreiber|Colonel Schreiber]]," Magath said. "Tell me about him."

"He is [[Richter]]'s deputy. Forty-five years old. Career Titan Security Division. He has never commanded a field unit. He has never negotiated a treaty. He has spent his career writing reports about Eldian population management and Titan deployment doctrine. He is intelligent. He is loyal to [[Richter]]. He will arrive with a list of questions that [[Richter]] has prepared for him. He will send daily reports to the capital. And he will be, for the duration of his assignment, the military faction's eyes in the room." [[Hoffmann]] looked at Magath. "You will manage him the way you managed the harbormaster and the frigate captain and the paper war. You will give him access and you will answer his questions and you will let him file his reports. And in twelve months, when the compound is fully operational and the technology transfer is delivering results, [[Anton Schreiber|Schreiber]] will be recalled to the capital and assigned to a desk job that keeps him far from the south bank."

"And if I refuse."

"Then the commission files a divided report. The military faction dissents. The General Staff fractures. The MOU becomes a political liability that no chairman can endorse without risking his position. And the coalition — " [[Hoffmann]] looked at the frigate, the helipad, the barracks, the clinic, the fisherman with the cast on his arm, " — the coalition learns that Marley cannot sustain an agreement without fracturing itself."

Magath was silent. The clinic hummed with the sound of the electric radiators. The children were still playing with the plastic toy. The fisherman's wife was still sitting beside her husband, her hand on his good arm, her eyes on the foreign medic who had fixed what was broken.

"I will accept the observer," Magath said. "On two conditions. One: [[Anton Schreiber|Schreiber]] reports through my office — his dispatches go to the capital, but I see them first. Two: his access to the compound is escorted — he does not walk the perimeter alone, he does not enter the communications room, he does not interview CATF personnel without my authorisation. If [[Richter]] objects, tell him the conditions are the price of my endorsement. He has spent his career managing Eldians. He can manage a colonel with a telegrapher."

[[Hoffmann]] nodded. "The conditions are acceptable. [[Anton Schreiber|Schreiber]] will be informed. The commission's report will be filed by the end of the week. Your authority will be confirmed — with the observer, with the conditions, with the full backing of the General Staff." He looked out the window — at the frigate, the helipad, the compound, the city of Odiha climbing the lower slopes of the mountains in the distance. "Commander. You have built a foreign military base on Marleyan soil in six weeks. The military faction sent a frigate to stop you, and a carrier strike group appeared on the horizon. They sent paper to stop you, and you answered with concrete. They are sending a colonel. I expect you will answer with something equally persuasive."

"The compound," Magath said. "The answer is always the compound."

### Scene 3: The Draft — Day ~196

[[Kiyomi Azumabito]] returned to Odiha on the evening of the commission's first full day, and this time she did not walk through the city.

The Hizuran steamer anchored at the outer pier at 18:00 — the same vessel, the same Azumabito crest on the funnel, the same flag of [[Hizuru]] snapping in the harbour wind. Kiyomi descended the gangway alone — no aides, no documents case, nothing visible except the small white card that she had left on Ishihara's table four weeks earlier and that Ishihara had carried in her coat pocket ever since.

She did not walk to the Port Authority. She walked directly to the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]]'s gangway at Pier 3, where [[Takeda Kaito|Takeda]] was waiting with the quiet, unsurprised expression of a diplomat who had been expecting this visit since the day Kiyomi first arrived.

"Ambassador Kiyomi. Dr. Ishihara is in the conference room. She has been waiting."

"I am sure she has," Kiyomi said. "The commission is in town. The military faction is demanding an observer. The general is touring the compound. Dr. Ishihara has been watching all of it from behind the diplomatic barrier, waiting for me to reappear with an offer that she can use as leverage." She smiled — the same not-warm, not-cold smile that Ishihara had seen four weeks earlier. "I have not come to provide leverage. I have come to provide an alternative."

The conference room on the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] was a converted cabin on the lower deck — a table, four chairs, a pot of Japanese green tea that the steward had brewed at Ishihara's request. The porthole looked onto the harbour — the signal tower, the Port Authority building, the lights of Odiha climbing the mountain slopes. The frigate at the south bank was invisible from this angle, but its presence was in the room — the knowledge that the compound was now a military installation, that the commission was deciding its future, that the coalition's investment in Marley was reaching its moment of maximum political risk.

Kiyomi sat without being offered a seat. She opened her coat and withdrew a single document — not the document case she had left four weeks earlier, but a new document, a fresh draft, its pages still crisp from the Hizuran embassy's printing press. She placed it on the table.

"This is a draft compact between the Nation of Hizuru and the Combined Anomaly Task Force," she said. "It is not a counter-offer to the MOU. It is not a replacement. It is a supplement. Hizuru does not need to compete with Marley for the coalition's attention. Hizuru needs to establish that it exists — that it is a trading nation with technology, access, and diplomatic reach that the empire cannot match."

[[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] picked up the document. It was six pages — shorter than the MOU, simpler, structured around trade rather than territory. The provisions were clear:

Hizuru would grant CATF access to its aviation technology — airframe designs, propulsion systems, the engineering knowledge that had produced the Azumabito clan's fleet of seaplanes and dirigibles. Hizuru would offer iceburst stone refinement technology — the precision optics and chemical processing that Paradis was already using to fuel its ODM gear, and that CATF had been trying to understand since the first Survey Corps rendezvous. Hizuru would provide diplomatic mediation — a neutral channel through which CATF could communicate with any AOT-world power without navigating the empire's political machinery.

In return, CATF would recognise Hizuru as a neutral trading partner with full access to the Gate trade lanes. CATF would grant Hizuran merchants the same commercial terms as Marleyan merchants under the trade compact. CATF would establish a liaison office at the Azumabito clan's Odiha hangar — separate from the south bank compound, independent of Marleyan oversight, a direct Hizuru-CATF channel that bypassed the empire entirely.

"The iceburst stone clause," Ishihara said. "CATF has been trying to understand iceburst chemistry for months. You are offering us the technology."

"I am offering you the refinement process — the optics, the pressure regulation, the purity control. I am not offering you the stone itself. The stone is on Paradis. You already have access to Paradis. Giving you the refinement technology simply means your access becomes useful." Kiyomi leaned forward. "Dr. Ishihara. The commission is deciding whether Commander Magath's authority survives the week. The military faction is demanding an observer. The general is negotiating conditions. The MOU that you signed six weeks ago is being re-litigated by men who were not in the room when it was drafted. Hizuru does not do this. Hizuru signs a compact, and the compact stands. We do not send observers to watch our partners. We do not anchor frigates in their channels. We do not file complaints about their dredge permits. We trade. And our trading relationships have lasted two thousand years."

"The Quadripartite Committee will need to review this. The decision is not mine alone."

"I know. I am not asking for a signature today. I am asking you to recommend acceptance to your committee — and to tell them, when you do, that Hizuru's offer will not remain open indefinitely. The military faction is not the only force in Marleyan politics. There are elements in the capital — in the Tybur estate, in the intelligence corps, in the trade ministry — who are watching the commission as closely as I am. If the commission weakens Magath, they will see an opportunity. They will move. And when they do, the coalition will need a partner that does not fracture under political pressure."

Ishihara looked at the document. Six pages. Iceburst refinement. Aviation technology. Diplomatic mediation. A trade lane through the Gate. A liaison office at the Azumabito hangar, separate from the compound, outside Marleyan jurisdiction. The offer was generous — more generous than Kiyomi had hinted at four weeks earlier, and more specific. The specificity meant Kiyomi had been working on this since the day she left Odiha. The specificity meant she was serious.

"Ambassador. I have one question. Why are you bringing this to me during the commission — at the moment when Marley is most divided, when the MOU is most vulnerable, when every signal you send will be read by the military faction as an attempt to undermine their position?"

"Because I want the military faction to read it that way." Kiyomi smiled — the same smile, the same calculation, the same merchant's precision. "The military faction needs to understand that the coalition has alternatives. If they believe CATF is trapped in the MOU — that you have no choice but to accept whatever conditions they impose — they will never stop imposing conditions. An observer today becomes a veto tomorrow becomes a renegotiation the day after. The only thing that stops them is the knowledge that you can walk away. Hizuru is the proof that you can walk away."

Ishihara was silent for a moment. The tea steamed between them. The porthole showed the harbour lights — the signal tower, the Port Authority, the city climbing the mountain. She thought about the commission. About [[Hoffmann]]'s tour. About the observer that Magath was almost certainly being asked to accept. About the military faction's next move and the move after that and the move after that.

"I will recommend acceptance," she said. "The committee will review the draft. The process will take weeks — possibly months. I cannot promise a timeline. I can promise that the draft will be read."

"That is all I ask." Kiyomi stood. She did not take the document. She left it on the table. "The Azumabito hangar is being reactivated. My staff will arrive within the week. You will have a direct line to me — not through the port, not through the admiral, not through the general. Direct. Use it when you are ready."

She walked to the door. At the threshold, she paused. "Dr. Ishihara. You have built a base on Marleyan soil. You have negotiated a compact with an empire that has never negotiated with an equal. You have watched a carrier strike group appear on the horizon and a frigate withdraw from a channel. You are winning. But you are winning against men who do not know how to lose. When they realise they cannot beat you — when the observer fails, when the conditions fail, when the compound continues to rise despite every lever they have pulled — they will not concede. They will try something else. Something that is not paper. Something that is not procedure. Hizuru has been watching Marley for two thousand years. We know what the empire does when it is losing. It will do the same thing to you."

"Which is."

Kiyomi's expression did not change. Her voice did not change. But the words landed in the room like a stone dropped into still water.

"It will find a way to make you the enemy. And it will tell itself it was defending its sovereignty all along."

She left. The door closed. The document sat on the table — six pages, iceburst refinement, aviation technology, a liaison office at the Azumabito hangar. The tea was cold. The harbour lights were bright. And [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] sat alone in the conference room, thinking about an observer who had not yet arrived, a military faction that would not stop, and an ambassador who had just told her exactly what was coming.

### Scene 4: The Report — Day ~197

The commission's report was filed on the third day of [[Hoffmann]]'s visit, in a document that ran to twelve pages and contained exactly the compromise that Magath had predicted.

The report confirmed Commander Magath's authority to negotiate on behalf of the Marleyan Empire. It endorsed the MOU as a valid instrument of Marleyan foreign policy. It noted the presence of the CSG in Marleyan-adjacent waters and recommended — in language that was carefully designed to satisfy the military faction without binding the General Staff — that future deployments of foreign military assets in waters adjacent to Marleyan territory be subject to prior notification through the diplomatic channel. It acknowledged the Paradisian engagement and recorded the same concern that the MOU's verbal annex had recorded: the empire noted CATF's contact with Paradis, reserved the right to raise concerns, and did not consider the matter resolved.

And it authorised the immediate assignment of a Marleyan liaison officer to the south bank compound — a colonel from the Titan Security Division, to be appointed by [[Richter|General [[Richter]]]], to serve as an observer with access to the compound perimeter and the Port Authority's communications office, reporting to Commander Magath with copies to the capital.

The observer was named Colonel [[Anton Schreiber|Schreiber]] — forty-five years old, twenty years in the Titan Security Division, a career administrator whose most significant field command had been a three-month rotation supervising Eldian labour battalions during the Mid-East mobilisation. [[Anton Schreiber|Schreiber]] was [[Richter]]'s deputy — loyal to the man who had advocated for the Titan Security Division's inclusion in the MOU negotiations, competent in the administrative sense, utterly unqualified to assess a multinational military operation. He would arrive at Odiha within the week.

Magath read the report in Verner's office while the general waited. [[Hoffmann]] had not left Odiha — his train was scheduled for the evening, and he had asked for a final meeting before his departure. The meeting was a formality. The report had already been filed. The observer had already been appointed. The commission's purpose — to give the military faction a visible concession while preserving the MOU — had been achieved.

"Commander," [[Hoffmann]] said. "You are not pleased."

"I am not displeased. I am calculating. [[Anton Schreiber|Schreiber]] is a Titan Security Division officer with no diplomatic training and no experience with foreign military operations. He will arrive at the south bank with a list of questions prepared by [[Richter]]. He will file daily reports to the capital. He will attempt to find violations — security lapses, procedural irregularities, anything that can be used to argue that the MOU is being implemented improperly. He will not find violations, because there are no violations. But he will look. And the looking will consume time that I would prefer to spend on other things."

"That is the price of keeping the General Staff united. You pay it, or the military faction writes the next MOU on its own terms."

"I accept the price. I do not accept the premise. The premise is that the military faction has earned a seat at the table. They have earned nothing. They obstructed construction, and the construction continued. They sent a frigate, and the frigate withdrew. They demanded a commission, and the commission endorsed me. They are three for three in tactical defeats, and their prize for losing every engagement is a colonel with a telegrapher." Magath looked at the report on Verner's desk. "[[Anton Schreiber|Schreiber]] is not a threat. He is a symptom. The disease is the faction's belief that the MOU can be controlled. They have not yet accepted that the MOU is a bilateral agreement between sovereign powers — not a Marleyan domestic policy that can be adjusted by committee. Until they accept that, they will keep sending observers and filing reports and believing that the next procedural move will win them what the last three moves lost."

"And when they finally accept it?"

"They will have a choice. Accept the MOU as it is — a partnership between equals. Or reject it entirely and try to destroy the coalition's presence by force. The observer is a test of which path they choose." Magath stood. "General. The commission has done what the commission was designed to do. The MOU stands. My authority stands. The compound stands. I will receive [[Anton Schreiber|Colonel Schreiber]]. I will give him his office and his telegrapher. And when he files his first report — when he tells [[Richter]] that the pier is complete, the frigate is at anchor, the Black Hawk is flying, and the compound is treating Marleyan civilians in a clinic that the empire did not build — I will have answered the only question that matters."

"Which question."

"The question the military faction has been asking since the day the Pelikan returned. The question the frigate was trying to answer. The question the paper war was testing. The question the commission was convened to resolve." Magath looked at the window — the harbour, the frigate, the compound, the mountains. "The question of whether the coalition is here to stay."

### Scene 5: First Arrivals — Day ~210

The first forty CATF personnel arrived by Black Hawk on a morning of low cloud and cold wind, and the south bank compound stopped working to watch them land.

They were not the first CATF personnel to set foot on the compound — the engineering team had been there for weeks, the construction crews for longer, the surveyors and the dredge operators and the logistics officers who had been running the supply chain from Grande Terre since the first construction vessel arrived. But they were the first *permanent* personnel — the advance detachment of the two hundred who would occupy the compound when it reached full operational capacity. They were the first who would sleep in the barracks, eat in the canteen, work in the clinic, patrol the perimeter, and call the south bank home. They were the face of the coalition's permanent presence on Marleyan soil.

The Black Hawk made three flights from the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] to the helipad — twelve personnel per lift, plus cargo pallets on the fourth rotation. The aircraft was loud — the distinctive *whump-whump-whump* of the twin rotors echoing across the river mouth, carrying to the port and the city and the signal tower where Lieutenant Brandt, the watch officer who had first seen the Pelikan's smoke on the horizon, was still on duty, still drinking the same black tea, still processing the impossible.

The personnel stepped off the Black Hawk one at a time — duffel bags over their shoulders, their uniforms a mix of national patterns and CATF insignia, their faces the faces of people who had volunteered for a posting that did not exist on any personnel roster six months ago. Logistics officers from Korea. Engineers from Japan. Medics from the United States. Communications technicians from China. A security detachment drawn from the CATF joint force — twelve soldiers with small arms and the quiet, professional stillness of troops who had been told they were not at war and had been trained to treat every situation as though war was one mistake away.

The commanding officer of the advance detachment was a Japanese captain named [[Ito|Ito]] — forty-one years old, JGSDF, a logistics officer who had spent the last three months at Grande Terre running the supply chain for the Paradis aid shipments and had volunteered for the south bank posting because he wanted to see the mainland. He stepped off the final Black Hawk flight, his duffel bag on his shoulder, his eyes taking in the compound — the pier, the frigate, the helipad, the barracks, the clinic, the radio tower, the perimeter fence with its posts and its wire and its CATF flag snapping in the wind — with the expression of a man who had seen the site plans and the satellite imagery and had not imagined that the reality would be this substantial.

[[Theo Magath|Magath]] was waiting at the helipad. He had walked the service road from the port at dawn — the same walk, the same river, the same mountains, the same road that the CATF grader had laid three weeks earlier and that was now being extended south toward the foothills where the next phase of construction would begin. He had come alone — no escort, no adjutant, no guard. The walk was a habit now, the twenty minutes between the Port Authority and the compound, the time he used to think about what the military faction would try next and what he would do when they tried it.

He walked to the edge of the helipad as the last Black Hawk lifted off — returning to the Envoy for the final cargo pallet — and stood beside [[Ito|Captain [[Ito|Ito]]]]. The Japanese captain was shorter than him, younger by a decade, his uniform pressed and his posture straight and his expression the expression of a professional meeting a counterpart he had only read about in briefing documents.

"Commander Magath. [[Ito|Captain [[Ito|Ito]]]], CATF advance detachment. We are here to take possession of the compound."

"The compound is ready," Magath said. "The pier has been tested to ten metres. The frigate is at anchor. The clinic is operational. The barracks are heated. The canteen is serving food that does not come from a military ration pack. The perimeter is fenced — the gate needs a guard, and the guard rotation will be your responsibility. The radio tower is linked to the Envoy's communications suite, and the Envoy is linked to Grande Terre, and Grande Terre is linked to the Quadripartite Committee. If you need something — anything — you have a supply chain that stretches eight thousand kilometres south." He paused. "Welcome to Marley."

[[Ito|Ito]] looked at the compound. The barracks. The clinic. The frigate at the pier — JS *Kumano*, grey and sleek and silent, her crew on deck, her ensign flying. The Black Hawk returning with the final pallet. The service road leading north toward the port, and the river beyond it, and the mountains beyond the river, and the empire beyond the mountains.

"Commander. I have read the briefings on the military faction's obstruction. The frigate. The commission. The observer. My orders are to maintain operational readiness and avoid provocation. I am authorised to use force only in self-defence. If the faction tries something — "

"The faction sent a frigate into the construction channel and a carrier strike group appeared on the horizon. The faction demanded a commission, and the commission endorsed the MOU. The faction demanded an observer, and the observer is a colonel with a telegrapher who will file daily reports that no one in the capital will read." Magath looked at the Japanese captain. "Captain. The faction has tried, and the faction has failed. The compound is here. The frigate is here. Your personnel are here. The question is not whether the faction will try again. It is whether you will be ready when they do."

"We will be ready."

"Good." Magath extended his hand. [[Ito|Ito]] took it. The handshake was brief — professional, not warm, the handshake of two officers from different worlds who had not yet decided whether they were allies but had both decided they were not enemies. The Black Hawk landed on the helipad behind them — the final cargo pallet, loaded with the last of the medical supplies for the clinic, the last of the communications equipment for the radio tower, the last of the thousand components that would turn the compound from a construction site into a base.

Magath released [[Ito|Ito]]'s hand and turned toward the service road. The walk back to the port was twenty minutes. He would make it in fifteen — the road was good, the weather was cold, and he had a report to write for the capital. The report would describe the arrival of the first CATF personnel. It would describe the frigate at the pier and the Black Hawk on the helipad and the clinic that was treating Marleyan civilians. It would describe a compound that was no longer a construction site and no longer a diplomatic fiction and no longer a point of contention in a General Staff debate. It would describe a fact.

And the military faction, sitting in the capital with their observer and their telegrapher and their daily reports, would read it and understand that the fact was not going away.

---

He walked the service road alone. The river was grey. The mountains were grey. The sky was grey. And behind him, on the south bank of the Odiha river mouth, forty personnel from four nations were unpacking their duffel bags in barracks that had not existed six weeks earlier. The frigate was at the pier. The Black Hawk was on the pad. The clinic had an X-ray machine and a pharmacy and a Marleyan fisherman with a cast on his arm who would tell his neighbours that the foreigners had fixed what was broken.

The compound was real. The compound was permanent. And whatever the military faction tried next — the observer, the procedure, the next lever and the lever after that — would have to reckon with the fact that there was a foreign base on Marleyan soil, and it was not leaving, and the man who had built it was walking back to his office to write a report that would begin with the same three words he had been writing since the day the Pelikan returned.

*The situation is stable.*
