# The Ark Strategy

## Summary
The Earth coalition's calculated decision — reached in the days after the *[[USS Stout (DDG-55)|USS Stout]]* collision with *[[HMS Resolute]]* — to stop correcting the [[Crown of England|Faithful Crown]]'s theology of Providence. Instead of insisting on secular honesty (as [[Stephen Davenport|Davenport]] had done in [[Davenport's Reply|his letter]]), the coalition would let the Faithful believe that Earth's arrival was divine intervention — not because the coalition shared the belief, but because the belief was strategically useful.

## Origin
Proposed by CIA analyst [[Marcus Webb]] during a post-collision coalition council. Webb's observation: every Faithful signal after the collision framed the *[[USS Stout (DDG-55)|Stout]]*'s performance — detecting the [[The Heretic Submarine|Heretic submarine]], destroying it in thirty-one seconds — as confirmation of divine favour, not as evidence of danger. The Faithful *wanted* to believe. Webb argued that the coalition did not need to claim the title of Providence — it merely needed to stop refusing it. The silence in the room after he finished was the strategy's first cost.

## Objectives
1. **Stabilise the Trench Crusade world** — Let the Faithful's own theology do the work of keeping their society cohesive, avoiding the fragmentation that would follow a theological crisis.
2. **Secure Earth's strategic interests** — Leverage the Crown's devotion to establish friendly military and diplomatic access, intelligence cooperation, and a united front against the [[Heretic Legion]].
3. **Arrange a grand Faithful summit in London** — Frame it as a "great council" that the Crown could not refuse, while bringing the [[Iron Sultanate]] to the table as a trilateral partner — the first time the Crown and Sultanate would meet in peace.

## Moral Calculus
The Ark Strategy is not a lie — the coalition does not claim to be Providence, and [[Stephen Davenport|Davenport]]'s reply to [[King Robert the Longsword|King Robert]] remains technically honest. But it is a manipulation: the coalition chooses silence where it could offer correction. The Faithful believe the moderns are the answer to eight centuries of prayer. The moderns let them believe it — not because it is true, but because it is useful. The moral cost is borne by those who know what they are doing: [[Stephen Davenport|Davenport]], [[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]], [[Terrence Kohler|Kohler]], and the members of the council who did not object.

## Tension with CSE Doctrine
The [[Cautious Secular Engagement|CSE doctrine]], established at the outset of first contact, explicitly prohibited participation in the theological frameworks of the Trench Crusade world. The Ark Strategy does not violate the letter of CSE — the coalition does not participate in Faithful theology — but it violates its spirit. [[France]], through [[Étienne Morel|Foreign Minister Morel]] and [[Étienne Roussel|Capitaine de vaisseau Roussel]], objected on these grounds. The fracture between Britain's pragmatic acceptance and France's principled resistance is one of the strategy's unresolved costs.

## Future Implications
The Ark Strategy sets the stage for [[The Summit of London]] and opens the door to deeper entanglement with Faithful theology. Once the coalition has let the Providence framing stand, it becomes progressively harder to correct — and progressively easier to extend. What begins as strategic silence may become institutional habit.
