# Five-Year Tension

## Summary

The Five-Year Tension is the cyclical border dispute between the [[Federated Crowns of Valdris]] and the [[Iron Concordat]] — a ritual of diplomatic letters, troop rotations, and requisitioned warehouses that flares up approximately every five years. It is not quite a war, but it mimics one closely enough to evacuate border towns and reshape lives. Adults who have lived through multiple Tensions treat them as weather; children born into the cycle know no other rhythm.

## Details

The Tension originates from the border drawn after the [[War of Convergence]], a line that both powers dispute but neither can alter — redrawing borders would require a treaty, and a treaty would require a negotiation, and a negotiation might attract [[Kaelen]]'s attention. So instead, every five years, the dispute re-emerges through a predictable sequence:

- Concordat patrols push closer to the border than the previous year.
- Valdran diplomats lodge formal protests.
- The Concordat masses troops at the frontier — never exceeding the 250,000 cap, but always suggesting it might.
- Valdris reinforces its garrison towns, doubling complements, crowding harbours with warships.
- Both sides posture for six to eighteen months.
- Nothing happens. The Tension subsides. Everyone goes home.

The last Tension was six years ago, making the current cycle overdue. This is, somehow, more alarming than the Tension itself — Valdrans are unsettled by things that do not adhere to schedule.

## Context

The Tension is experienced differently depending on where you live. In [[Valdris Prime]], it is a news item. In [[Kedros Garrison]], it is the reason the troop trains run day and night, the harbour is crowded with pre-dreadnoughts, and the census clerk drafts secondary relocation orders for the transmigrant district. For people like [[Asahi]] and [[Akahito]], the Tension is simply the rhythm of life — something their parents talk about in hushed tones, something that rearranges their town every few years, something that has never yet become a war but has never quite stopped threatening to.
