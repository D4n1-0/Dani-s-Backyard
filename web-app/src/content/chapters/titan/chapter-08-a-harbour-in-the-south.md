# Chapter 08 – A Harbour in the South

## Summary

The fourteen Marleyan crew who volunteered sail the [[Pelikan]] home — seventy-eight days after the expedition was declared lost, thirty-one souls reduced to the fourteen who would return. No Eldians. No [[Klaus Dreher]]. No [[Elias Vogler|Dr. Vogler]]. Just the sailors who had spent the longest months of their lives as detainees on a subantarctic island and had chosen, when CATF offered them a role, to ride their own ship back to their own port and serve as the human bridge between their captors and their country. [[Satomi Ishihara]] boards the [[CATF Envoy]] at Grande Terre with a small diplomatic delegation and a specific instruction: open a channel, demonstrate capability without threat, and leave before the empire could mobilise. What follows is a ten-day voyage through waters no Marleyan chart had ever recorded, and then a harbour in the south — Odiha, where Admiral [[Konrad Verner]] processes the impossible sight of his lost ship returning with two vessels that should not exist following in her wake. The contact is brief. Controlled. Each side measures the other across a pier gate and a wood-panelled office and a night spent in a borrowed bunk. When the envoy departs, no treaty has been signed. No weapons have been fired. A radio frequency has been left behind, and the first Marleyan flag officer to meet a power his empire has no framework for understanding is writing a report to a capital that will not believe him.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The Pier at Grande Terre — Day 108

The fourteen Marleyan sailors boarded the [[Pelikan]] at dawn.

They had been on [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] for seventy-two days. They had learned the layout of the base — the prefab modules, the fuel farms, the grey warships at anchor — and they had learned to stop cataloguing it because the catalogue was too large and too strange and every new item made the old items feel less real. The fourteen who were sailing home were not the fourteen who had been captured. Three of the original twenty-two Marleyan crew had declined to return — one had married into the civilian support staff, two had requested asylum, their reasons private and their paperwork pending. Five remained in detention pending security review, their allegiances still being assessed. The fourteen who walked the gravel path to Pier 4 were the ones who had answered yes when the CATF liaison officer asked: *Do you want to go home and help us talk to your government?*

The pier was cold. Subantarctic autumn. The wind off the harbour carried the smell of diesel and salt and the specific metallic tang of prefab construction that had become the smell of captivity for every detainee on the base. The [[Pelikan]] was tied at the outer berth — her icebreaker bow still scarred from the drift that had carried her into CATF waters, her funnel cold, her hull small against the grey shapes of the destroyers at the next pier. She had been maintained during her impoundment: hull scraped, engine serviced, provisions loaded. CATF had returned her in better condition than they had found her. The gesture was deliberate.

[[Jürgen Haas]] was the last to board. He was thirty-nine years old, nineteen years in the Imperial Marleyan Navy, a career able seaman who had joined to escape a farming village and stayed because the sea made more sense than the land. He had spent seventy-two days at Grande Terre — first as a detainee, then as a consultant, then as the man who had drilled a Japanese diplomat on lower-deck Marleyan slang and engine-room vocabulary because she had asked and because he had nothing else to do and because, somewhere in the third week, he had stopped thinking of the CATF personnel as captors and started thinking of them as — he did not have a word for it. Not friends. Not allies. Something that occupied the space between.

He stood at the top of the gangway and looked back at the base. The red cross on the medical facility. The drone hangar. The fuel farm. The flag of the [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)]] — four interlocked rings on a blue field — snapping in the cold wind on the same pole where the Marleyan naval ensign had never flown. Seventy-two days. He had counted them. He had written his observations in a notebook that CATF had let him keep because his observations were not strategic and his notebook was full of sketches of seabirds and notes on the weather and a single sentence in his own handwriting that he had written on Day 51: *They are not what I was told they would be. Nothing is what I was told it would be.*

He walked onto the [[Pelikan]]'s deck. The wood was cold. The fittings were the same. The ship smelled of coal and salt and the specific must of a steam vessel that had been dormant for too long. The other thirteen were already aboard — stowing gear, checking lines, lighting the boiler. They moved with the practiced economy of sailors who had done this a thousand times and were now doing it for the last time in a place they would never see again.

On the next pier, the [[CATF Envoy]] was preparing to get underway. She was a white-hulled motor yacht, sixty metres, gas-turbine propulsion, the four-ring insignia on her bow. She had been commissioned for this mission — the first diplomatic vessel in the history of either Earth to carry an envoy from one world to another. Her crew was six: Captain [[Fujii Masahiro]], a Japanese civilian skipper with thirty years of blue-water experience, a chief engineer, two deckhands, a steward, and a communications technician. Her diplomatic complement was seven: [[Satomi Ishihara]] — Chief of Mission; [[Takeda Kaito]] — deputy protocol officer, a thirty-four-year-old Japanese career diplomat who had been seconded to CATF from the MOFA European Affairs desk and was still adjusting to the fact that there was no European Affairs desk for the world he was now serving; [[Seo Min-ah]] — translator and linguist, a Korean civilian who had spent four months studying Marleyan under [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]]'s tutelage and could now form sentences in a language that had never been spoken on her Earth; Dr. Yoshida Rina — medical officer, a Japanese physician whose principal qualification for the mission was that she had volunteered when no one else would; and two security personnel whose names were not recorded on the manifest and whose presence was not visible to anyone watching from the pier.

[[Satomi Ishihara]] stood on the [[CATF Envoy]]'s bridge wing, watching the [[Pelikan]]'s funnel begin to vent smoke. She was forty-one years old, a career officer of the Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and she had been selected for this mission over more senior candidates because she was a civilian, a woman, and a scholar — a profile that Marleyan military culture would read as non-hostile before any conscious calculation about threat assessment occurred. She had fifteen years of diplomatic experience. She had written a doctoral thesis on first-contact protocols between nations of radically asymmetric capability. She had never, in any of the scenarios she had modelled, imagined that the asymmetric capability would belong to her side.

The third ship — the [[Southern Venture]] — was already at anchor outside the harbour, waiting. A medium-size commercial cargo vessel chartered out of Singapore, civilian-crewed, no visible armament. On her deck: five armoured Toyota Land Cruisers lashed to tie-downs, a containerised helipad with an Airbus H125 folded for transit, fuel bladders, generators, water purification units, medical supplies. A capability-demonstration platform that would never be used. The proof was in the ship itself — the fact that such a ship existed, that it carried machines that existed, that the civilisation that had built it could build a hundred more.

[[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] was on the pier. He had come to see the [[Pelikan]] off — his ship, or what had been his ship for forty-seven days before it had been captured and he had ceased to be its head of expedition and become, instead, a scientific consultant to the power that had captured it. He was not sailing home. The decision had been his, and CATF's, and mutual: his knowledge of the Gate was too deep, his scientific value too high, and his presence on the returning ship would complicate the message CATF wanted to send. The [[Pelikan]] was returning her crew. Not her mission. Not her secrets. The distinction was the kind of diplomatic calculus that [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] had been trained to make and [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] had learned to accept.

He walked to the foot of the [[CATF Envoy]]'s gangway. [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] descended to meet him. She was dressed in a dark blue civilian coat — no uniform, no insignia, no visible marker of rank. She looked like a university professor or a government functionary. That was the point.

"The language materials are aboard," [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] said. "The grammar supplement. The cultural notes — what I could recall of Odiha protocol. It's not complete. I was never stationed in the southern districts. But it should be enough."

"It will be enough," [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] said.

[[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] looked at her. He had spent ten days teaching her Marleyan — the phonemes, the syntax, the specific cadence of a language that had evolved across a thousand years of continental politics and racial hierarchy. She had learned faster than any student he had ever taught. She had asked questions about register and formality that had forced him to think about his own language in ways he never had.

"You speak like a diplomat from the northern provinces," he said. "Your accent is formal. Educated. It will mark you as an outsider to a Marleyan ear, but a respectable outsider. An ambassador from a country that does not exist, speaking Marleyan like a scholar who learned it from a book. They will not know what to make of you."

"Good," she said. "That is the goal."

"The admiral at Odiha — [[Konrad Verner|Verner]] — I met him once. Before the expedition. He signed our departure orders. He is not a politician. He is a sailor who was promoted past his ambition. He will listen. Whether he believes — " [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] paused. "He will listen. That is more than most Marleyan flag officers would offer."

[[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] nodded. She had read the intelligence summaries — the profiles of Admiral Verner, of Odiha, of the Marleyan naval district command structure, of the political factions in the capital that would receive Verner's report. She had read everything there was to read. The intelligence was incomplete. The intelligence was always incomplete. The gap between what you knew before you arrived and what you learned after you stepped onto the dock was the gap that diplomacy existed to cross.

"Dr. Vogler," she said. "Thank you. For the language. For the culture. For trusting us enough to help us talk to your people."

[[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] looked at the [[Pelikan]]. The funnel was smoking steadily now. The crew were at their stations. The ship that had carried him into captivity was about to carry fourteen of his countrymen home without him. He had been on Grande Terre for seventy-two days. He had not seen his family. He had not seen his country. He had helped the people who captured him prepare to talk to the government he had served. The calculation was too complex for any single emotion.

"I am not trusting you," he said. "I am trusting that talking is better than shooting. That is a calculation, not a belief."

"Most diplomacy begins as a calculation," [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] said. "And becomes a belief if it works. We have not worked yet. I will let you know when we have."

She climbed the gangway. The [[CATF Envoy]]'s engines were warming — the low hum of the gas turbine, almost inaudible against the harbour wind, the kind of sound that a Marleyan naval engineer would spend the rest of his career trying to replicate. The lines were cast off. The [[Pelikan]] was already moving through the harbour mouth, her hull small against the grey water, her funnel smoke trailing flat across the dawn. The [[Southern Venture]] fell into formation behind the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]]. The three ships cleared the breakwater and turned north, toward a continent that no modern navigational chart had ever recorded, toward a port called [[Odiha]] where an admiral was about to receive the most important visitor in the history of his command.

On the harbour wall, [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] watched until the ships were hull-down on the horizon. Then he turned and walked back toward the base, toward his laboratory, toward the Titan in its cage that he still did not fully understand and the world that had captured him and the language he had given to the diplomat who was now sailing toward his home.

The ships disappeared. The horizon was empty. The subantarctic wind kept blowing, and somewhere in the grey water, a periscope that had been watching the harbour since midnight retracted and sank beneath the thermocline, and the boat that had been assigned to follow the envoy began to move north at twelve knots, a shadow beneath the surface that no one aboard the three ships could see.

### Scene 2: The Crossing — Days 109–118

The voyage took ten days.

The [[Pelikan]] led. Her steam engine — a single reciprocating piston, the same one that had carried the expedition forty-seven days north and then eleven days south across an ocean that had changed beneath their feet — drove her at a steady eight knots through water that grew warmer as the latitude fell. The [[CATF Envoy]] followed at a distance of five hundred metres, her gas turbine pushing a clean twenty-two knots when she wanted it and throttled back to match the [[Pelikan]]'s pace when she did not. The [[Southern Venture]] brought up the rear, a grey box on the grey water, the vehicles on her deck catching the morning light.

The formation was deliberate. The [[Pelikan]] in front — the familiar silhouette, the ship that Marleyan harbour lookouts would recognise first. A lost expedition returning home. Behind her: the proof that the expedition had not been lost in the usual way. The diplomatic yacht and the cargo vessel were the second paragraph of a message whose first paragraph was the [[Pelikan]] herself. *Your ship is back. We are the reason. We want to talk.*

The crew of the [[Pelikan]] rotated through the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] in shifts — two or three at a time, ferried across the open water in a small launch that the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] carried on her stern davits. They were not prisoners. They were not guests. They were something in between — consultants, the CATF liaison officer had called them, a word that had no exact equivalent in Marleyan and that [[Seo Min-ah|Seo]], the translator, had rendered as *advisors-who-were-once-captives*. The translation was imperfect. The relationship was imperfect. But the sailors came aboard, and they ate in the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]]'s small mess, and they answered [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]]'s questions about Marleyan naval protocol, and they taught [[Seo Min-ah|Seo]] the difference between the formal language of the admiralty and the lower-deck language of the sailors who would be watching from the pier, and they sat on the afterdeck in the evening and watched the sun set over an ocean that was leading them home and did not know, yet, what to feel about it.

[[Jürgen Haas|Haas]] spent more time on the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] than any of the others. He had become, without anyone formally designating it, the senior Marleyan liaison — the man who had spent enough hours with [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] that the strangeness of her presence had worn into something approaching familiarity. He drilled her on the admiral's name — Konrad Verner, pronounced with a soft *e*, the stress on the first syllable. He taught her the proper form of address for a flag officer of the Imperial Marleyan Navy: *Herr Admiral*, the *Herr* carrying a weight of formal deference that *Sir* did not capture. He explained the layout of Odiha — the deep-water piers, the signal tower, the port authority building with the admiral's corner office overlooking the harbour, the mountains that rose to the north and made the city feel walled even though no wall had ever been built there.

On the fifth day of the crossing, [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] stood on the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]]'s bridge wing and watched the [[Pelikan]] ahead and thought about the submarine.

She had been told about it on the night before departure — a brief meeting in a windowless room at Grande Terre, an American naval officer she had never met before and would never meet again, a single sentence: *The USS Annapolis will be shadowing your group at periscope depth for the duration of the contact. You will not see her. You will not hear her. If the contact turns hostile, she will surface and extract your delegation. If you need her, transmit on frequency 243.0. Otherwise, she does not exist, you do not know about her, and this conversation never happened.*

She had not transmitted on frequency 243.0. She had not told anyone on the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] — not Captain [[Fujii Masahiro|Fujii]], not [[Takeda Kaito|Takeda]], not [[Seo Min-ah|Seo]]. The knowledge was hers alone, a cold weight at the base of her consciousness, a backup plan that she hoped she would never need and was grateful existed. The submarine was somewhere behind them, below the thermal layer, a silent shape of American steel and nuclear fire that could erase the harbour of Odiha from the map if the order were given and the authority existed. The order would not be given. The authority did not exist. But the boat was there, and the boat was watching, and [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] carried the knowledge of it the way a diplomat carried a letter of credence — a document you did not show unless the alternative was worse.

On the seventh day, the ocean changed.

The latitude was approximately thirty-five degrees south. The water temperature had risen from subantarctic cold to temperate. The seabirds had changed — albatross replaced by gulls, the species that followed fishing fleets and coastal shipping. The sky was clearer. The horizon was closer. And on the navigation table of the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]], Captain [[Fujii Masahiro|Fujii]] made a note in the log: *Crossing 35S. Expected Marleyan patrol zone begins at 34S based on detainee intelligence. All vessels maintaining visual and radar watch. No contacts.*

On the eighth day, the [[Pelikan]]'s crew identified a landmark — a headland on the starboard bow, low and grey, its shape matching a sketch in a Marleyan coastal pilot that had been aboard the expedition ship through all seventy-eight days of her disappearance. The headland meant they were inside Marleyan-patrolled waters. It meant the next ship they saw would be Marleyan. It meant the contact was no longer a plan and was now an event — something that was about to happen, in real time, to real people, on a stretch of ocean that belonged to a power whose first instinct toward the unknown was to shoot it.

The tenth day was calm. The sea was flat. The sky was overcast. The [[Pelikan]]'s smoke rose straight from her funnel and hung in the still air like a signal that had been placed there by someone who wanted to be seen. Ahead, the coastline of the Marleyan southern continent began to resolve — mountains first, grey peaks above a haze of coastal mist, then the river mouth, then the harbour walls, then the signal tower, then the city.

Odiha.

[[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] stood on the bridge wing for the final approach. [[Takeda Kaito|Takeda]] was beside her, tablet in hand, the contact protocol checklist open and complete. [[Seo Min-ah|Seo]] was in the communications room, running a final check on the translation equipment. The two security personnel were belowdecks, out of sight, where they would remain for the duration of the contact. Captain [[Fujii Masahiro|Fujii]] was on the bridge, his hands on the throttles, his eyes on the [[Pelikan]] ahead and the harbour ahead of the [[Pelikan]] and the mountains ahead of the harbour and the empire ahead of the mountains.

"There is a signal from the tower," [[Fujii Masahiro|Fujii]] said. His voice was calm — the voice of a man who had navigated a hundred harbours and was treating this one as just another port call. "Flag sequence. They are asking the lead vessel to identify herself."

"The Pelikan will reply," [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] said. "She is flying the Marleyan naval ensign. She is a Marleyan ship. She will request permission to enter. And then they will see us, and they will not have a flag sequence for what we are."

She lifted a pair of binoculars — standard CATF-issue, lighter than anything produced on either Earth, the glass clearer than any Marleyan lens could grind. Through them, she could see the signal tower. The flags were moving. The harbour master was processing the arrival of a ship that his records showed as seventy-eight days overdue. She lowered the binoculars.

The submarine was somewhere beneath the water. She did not look. She did not need to. The knowledge was a pressure against her sternum, a second heartbeat, a thing that had been there for ten days and would not leave until the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] was back in the subantarctic ocean with nothing behind her but open water.

"Captain Fujii," she said. "Proceed into the harbour. Follow the Pelikan. Maintain the five-hundred-metre gap. And please — make sure the CATF insignia is visible."

"It's visible," [[Fujii Masahiro|Fujii]] said. "It's the only white ship in the harbour. Everyone can see it."

Good, [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] thought. That was the point.

### Scene 3: The Harbour — Day 118

The signal tower at Odiha was a square stone structure, three storeys, perched on the headland at the harbour's northern entrance. It had been built sixty years earlier, when Marleyan naval expansion had required permanent watch stations along the southern coast, and it had not changed since. The flag locker held forty-seven signal flags. The watch officer's logbook was leather-bound, hand-ruled, the pages filled with the careful script of a hundred years of harbour entries — ship names, cargo manifests, arrival times, departure times, the quiet record of a port that had never seen anything worth recording.

The watch officer on the morning of Day 118 was a lieutenant named Brandt — twenty-four years old, three years into a naval career that had been spent almost entirely at Odiha, a posting he had once resented for its distance from the Mid-East front and had since learned to appreciate for its distance from the Mid-East front. He was drinking tea — the same black tea that every watch officer drank, brewed in the same iron pot that had been brewing tea in the signal tower for sixty years — when he saw the smoke.

Three columns. Low on the horizon, approaching from the south — a direction from which no ships were expected. The southern trade routes ran east-west. The fishing fleet was in harbour. The naval patrols were concentrated on the Mid-East approaches. No one came from the south.

He raised his glass. The first ship was small — a steamer, single funnel, roughly forty metres. Her lines were familiar. The icebreaker bow. The reinforced hull. The silhouette that he had seen in the harbour logbook every week for the past two months under a single notation: *Pelikan — Expedition Vessel — Status: OVERDUE — Presumed Lost.* The notation had been written by Admiral Verner himself. The admiral had signed the departure order. The admiral had written the overdue notice. The admiral had closed the file.

And now the file was steaming toward the harbour, trailing smoke against the grey sky, with two ships behind her that did not belong to any naval register Brandt had ever studied.

One was white. Clean, modern, the kind of hull that looked like it had been drawn rather than built, the kind of lines that said *this was designed* rather than *this was constructed*. She was sixty metres, maybe longer, with no funnel and no visible propulsion and no smoke and the specific wrongness of a ship that moved without making a sound. The second ship was grey, box-shaped, commercial — but on her deck, visible even through the glass, were shapes that did not belong on a cargo vessel. Vehicles. Machines. A structure that Brandt would later describe to the admiral as *a platform with a machine on top, folded, like a bird with its wings closed.*

Brandt put down his tea. He opened the harbour log. He wrote, in his careful officer's hand: *0847 — Three vessels approaching from south. Lead vessel appears to be Pelikan (expedition steamer — OVERDUE). Two unknown vessels in company. No signals received. Notifying harbour master.*

He walked to the flag locker. The flags were wool, heavy with sea damp, the colours faded by sixty years of coastal sun. He pulled the interrogation sequence — *Identify Yourself* — and gave the order to hoist. The flags rose. The wind caught them. The three ships approached. And Lieutenant Brandt, twenty-four years old, stood in the signal tower of the Odiha Naval District and watched the impossible sail into his harbour.

Admiral [[Konrad Verner]] was in his office when the runner came.

The runner was a boy — sixteen, maybe seventeen, a junior orderly who had been assigned to the signal tower because his father was a dock foreman and the navy needed boys who knew the harbour. He was out of breath. His uniform was damp with sweat. His hand was shaking when he handed the admiral the signal tower's written note.

Verner read it. He read it twice. He was sixty-one years old, a Konteradmiral of the Imperial Marleyan Navy, and he had been processing bad news for forty years. He knew the texture of bad news. The texture of this news was not bad. It was impossible.

The [[Pelikan]] had been overdue for seventy-eight days. The search had been called off at day sixty. The file had been closed at day sixty-five. The letters to the crew's families — Verner had signed them himself, thirty-one letters, each one a variation on the same template: *The Imperial Navy regrets to inform you that the expedition vessel Pelikan has been declared lost with all hands.* He had signed them with the same pen he had used to sign the departure orders. The pen was still on his desk.

He stood. His knee — the left one, shattered in a storm off Lakua three decades ago — protested with the same dull ache it always produced when he moved too quickly. He ignored it. He walked to the window of his corner office, the one that overlooked the harbour, and raised his own glass.

The [[Pelikan]] was in the harbour mouth, her funnel smoke drifting flat across the water, her hull smaller than he remembered, her crew visible on deck — a handful of figures, fourteen maybe, standing at the rail with the stillness of people who were not sure if they were arriving or returning. Behind her, at a carefully maintained distance, two ships that had no business being in any harbour on any continent of the AOT world.

The white ship was the problem. Not because it was armed — his lookouts were already signalling, and the signals coming back were a stream of negatives: no gun ports, no torpedo tubes, no visible weapon mounts. It was a problem because of what it was, not what it carried. It was a machine that moved without smoke, a hull that looked like it had been poured rather than plated, a flag — he squinted through the glass — a flag he had never seen before. Four rings. Blue field. No nation on any Marleyan intelligence summary flew that flag.

The grey ship behind it was a cargo vessel. Vehicles on deck. A helicopter — he did not know the word, could not have formed the concept, but his eyes processed the folded rotor blades and gave his brain a report that his brain filed under *unknown machine — flight-capable — not Hizuran.* The deck cargo was not cargo. It was a message. The message was: *We brought more than what you can see. We chose not to bring it here.*

He lowered his glass. His adjutant — a lieutenant commander named Drexler, forty years old, competent, unimaginative — was waiting at the door.

"Admiral. The harbour master reports the lead vessel has identified herself as the [[Pelikan]]. She is requesting permission to enter. Her crew are — " Drexler paused. "Her crew are Marleyan. Fourteen souls. They say they are returning after being assisted by — " another pause, " — a foreign power. The translation is approximate. The harbour master is not sure what they mean."

Verner looked at the harbour. The [[Pelikan]]. The white ship. The grey ship. The fourteen Marleyans on deck. The foreign power. The request to enter.

He made the decision in the time it took his knee to ache.

"Pier 3," he said. "All three vessels to Pier 3. Deep-water only. No shore leave. No one leaves the pier. I want a full squad at the pier gate — entrance and exit only, no passage into the city. The vessels stay at Pier 3 until I receive a response from the capital. Understood?"

"Yes, Admiral."

"And Drexler — the [[Pelikan]] crew. They are to remain with the foreign vessels. They do not disembark for shore until I have spoken to them personally. If they are injured or sick, the medical officer attends them on the pier. Not in the base hospital. The pier."

"Yes, Admiral."

Verner walked to the door. His limp was more pronounced than usual. He did not care. He had just given an order that would either prevent a diplomatic incident or create one, and he would not know which until he was standing on the pier facing the people who had brought his lost ship home.

"Have the harbour master clear Berths 1 through 4," he said. "I want an empty pier and clear sightlines and no one on the waterfront who does not have a reason to be there. And get me a runner to the Azumabito hangar. I want to know if the Hizurans have ever seen a ship that looks like that."

"Admiral. The Hizuran representative has been notified. She is — she says she has never seen a ship with a flag of four rings. She asks if this is a new Marleyan classification."

Verner almost laughed. He did not laugh. He had been a flag officer for too long to laugh at things that should have been laughable. "Tell her no. Tell her it is a new classification for everyone." He walked through the door, his adjutant following, his knee protesting, his mind already assembling the report he would have to write to the capital — the report that would describe, in the most clinical language he could construct, the arrival of a civilisation that had not existed seventy-eight days ago.

### Scene 4: The Pier — Day 118

The [[Pelikan]] tied up at Pier 3 at 0924.

The pier was a wide stone platform extending into the deep-water harbour, reinforced with iron bollards and wooden fenders and the accumulated wear of a century of cargo and passengers and naval resupply. It was empty. The harbour master had cleared Berths 1 through 4, but Pier 3 was the only pier that mattered — the end berth, the one with the deepest water and the clearest sightlines and the fewest places for a person to hide. The gate at the pier's landward end was an iron grill, waist-high, flanked by two lamp-posts and a guardhouse. A squad of eight Marleyan naval infantry had taken position at the gate — four on each side, rifles at rest, their expressions the expressions of soldiers who had been told to expect something and had not been told what.

Verner was at the gate. He had walked the length of the pier from the Port Authority Building, his limp slow and deliberate, his adjutant two paces behind him with a notebook and the expression of a man who had stopped trying to predict what his admiral would do next. The wind off the harbour was cold. The sky was grey. The mountains to the north were capped with snow, and the river mouth to the south was broad and grey and empty of traffic, and the smell of the city — coal smoke, fish, wet stone — was the same smell that had been coming off the harbour for the sixty years that Verner had known it.

The [[Pelikan]]'s crew did not disembark. They remained on deck — visible, standing at the rail, fourteen Marleyan sailors in Marleyan naval work uniforms that had been laundered and pressed and returned to them by their CATF hosts before departure. They were not prisoners. They were not guests. They were the human bridge that [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] had been building for ten days, and now the bridge was about to be crossed from the other side.

A gangway was lowered from the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] — not the [[Pelikan]]. The [[Pelikan]] crew transferred to the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] first, crossing the gap between the two ships as they had done a dozen times during the voyage, and then [[Jürgen Haas|Haas]] descended the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]]'s gangway alone.

He had not been home for seventy-eight days. He had been captured, detained, debriefed, interrogated, employed, and then handed a role he had not asked for and had no template for performing. He had been taught to see his captors as a threat and then had stopped seeing them as a threat and had not known, for many days, what to see them as instead. Not friends. Not allies. Something that occupied the space between. Now he was walking down a gangway from a ship that did not belong to his navy, toward an admiral he had served under for three years, carrying a message that his admiral would not believe until he heard it from Marleyan lips.

He stopped at the foot of the gangway. Verner was at the pier gate — twenty metres away, the iron grill between them, the eight naval infantry at his back. The distance was deliberate. Verner had not crossed the gate. He was waiting.

[[Jürgen Haas|Haas]] walked forward. He stopped at the gate. He stood on the pier side, the foreign ships at his back, the admiral of his navy on the other side of an iron grill that had been installed before he was born and had never been used to separate a Marleyan sailor from a Marleyan admiral until today.

"Admiral," he said. His voice was steady. He had practiced the words for ten days. "Able Seaman Jürgen Haas. Imperial Marleyan Navy. Nineteen years of service. I was assigned to the [[Pelikan]] expedition. We were lost. We were — " he paused. "We were found. By a power that does not belong to this world. They are not Marley's enemy. They want to talk. They have sent their diplomat."

Verner looked at him. The sailor was thinner than he had been seventy-eight days ago. His uniform was clean. His eyes were the eyes of a man who had seen things that did not fit inside the language he had been given to describe them. Behind him, the white ship and the grey ship sat at Pier 3, their insignia visible, their crews watching from the rail.

"Seaman Haas," Verner said. His voice was low, level, the voice of a flag officer who was processing information that his training had not prepared him to process. "You are telling me that the [[Pelikan]] was lost, and was found, and is now returning to port after seventy-eight days, escorted by a foreign power from another world, carrying a diplomat who wishes to speak to the [[Marleyan Empire]]. Is that correct."

"Yes, Admiral. That is correct."

Verner was silent for a moment. The harbour sounds filled the gap — gulls, water against stone, the distant clank of a crane at the outer cargo wharf. His adjutant was writing in his notebook. The naval infantry were still. The pier was still. The moment was the opening paragraph of a report that would change the strategic calculus of the empire, and Verner was standing at a gate in the cold morning air hearing it from the mouth of a sailor who had once cleaned the bilges on a frigate he had commanded.

"Who is the diplomat," Verner said.

"Dr. [[Satomi Ishihara]]. Ministry of Foreign Affairs — " [[Jürgen Haas|Haas]] hesitated, " — of a coalition called the [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|Combined Anomaly Task Force]]. Four nations. Civilian. She speaks Marleyan. She wants to meet you. She is — " he searched for the word he had been taught, " — she is the person they sent to prove they want to talk before they want to fight."

Verner nodded. He turned to his adjutant. "Open the gate."

The adjutant hesitated. "Admiral — "

"Open the gate. The diplomat crosses alone. No guards. No escort. She walks from her ship to me, and no one stops her, and no one touches her, and if I am wrong about this, I will be wrong in a way that can be observed and recorded and judged. If I am right — open the gate."

The gate opened. The iron grill swung inward. The eight naval infantry parted. [[Jürgen Haas|Haas]] stepped through — the first of the fourteen to set foot on Marleyan soil — and stood aside, waiting.

The [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]]'s gangway remained lowered. Verner looked past [[Jürgen Haas|Haas]] at the white ship, at the four-ring flag, at the deck where a woman in a dark blue coat was beginning to descend the gangway alone.

### Scene 5: Ishihara Ashore — Day 118

[[Satomi Ishihara]] walked the length of Pier 3 alone.

She had insisted on it. [[Takeda Kaito|Takeda]] had argued — a deputy should accompany, a translator should be present, a security detail should follow at a distance. She had said no to all of it. She had walked into a dozen first meetings across fifteen years of diplomatic service — trade negotiations, hostage handovers, the quiet back-channel conversations that happened in hotel lobbies and airport lounges and the back rooms of restaurants that did not exist on any public register — and she had learned that the first impression was made in the first thirty seconds, and the first impression was about vulnerability. A diplomat who arrived alone was a diplomat who trusted the host to behave. A diplomat who arrived with guards was a diplomat who did not. She wanted Verner to see a woman in a blue coat walking toward him without protection. She wanted him to understand, before she spoke a single word, that she had placed herself in his hands.

The pier was empty. The stone was wet with harbour spray. The wind off the water was cold against her face. She could feel the submarine beneath her feet — not literally, not through the stone and the water and the hull, but as a knowledge, a pressure, the awareness that somewhere two hundred metres below the surface of the harbour a boat was listening through its sonar array and could, if the order came, surface through the harbour floor and end the conversation before it started. The order would not come. The boat was a backup, not a plan. But the knowledge of it was a spine, and she walked straighter because of it.

At the gate, she stopped. [[Konrad Verner|Verner]] was on the other side. She had studied his photograph — a file photo, three years old, from a Marleyan naval registry that the drone intelligence section had captured during their survey of the southern ports. He was older than the photograph. His hair was greyer. The lines on his face were deeper. The limp was new — or not new, but the photograph had been taken from the waist up, and the limp was a thing you saw only when a man walked, and he was standing still now, waiting, watching her with the expression of a flag officer who had spent forty years learning to listen before shooting.

She spoke first. In Marleyan — the formal register, the northern province accent that [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] had taught her, the vowels slightly flattened on her Japanese tongue but the grammar intact.

"Herr Admiral. My name is Satomi Ishihara. I am an officer of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan, serving as Chief of Mission for the Combined Anomaly Task Force. We have returned your ship and fourteen of your crew. We have come to talk. May I enter?"

[[Konrad Verner|Verner]] looked at her. The woman in the dark blue coat. The accent that belonged to a province no Marleyan had heard from in a century. The language that was too formal and too correct and too deliberate — the language of someone who had been taught by a scholar and then drilled by a sailor and had emerged with a speech pattern that belonged to neither. She had named her country. She had named her coalition. She had said she wanted to talk. She had not brought guards. She was, in every measurable respect, exactly what she claimed to be.

"You speak Marleyan," he said.

"I learned. For this conversation. I have been practising for ten days."

"Ten days."

"Yes."

"Who taught you."

"A Marleyan scientist named [[Elias Vogler]]. He is not with the expedition. He remained at our base. He sent his regards."

[[Konrad Verner|Verner]] absorbed this. [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] was alive. The expedition's chief scientist was alive and had taught a foreign diplomat his language and had sent his regards to the admiral who had signed his departure order. The information accumulated in the admiral's mind the way it accumulated in the harbour logbook — one entry at a time, each entry stranger than the last.

"Dr. Ishihara," he said. "You are standing on the soil of the [[Marleyan Empire]]. You are here without invitation and without precedent. Your ships are not registered in any Marleyan port directory. Your flag is not recognised by any Marleyan authority. You are asking me to treat you as a diplomat when I have no framework, no protocol, and no instruction that covers your existence. What do you expect me to do."

[[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] held his gaze. The wind moved her coat. The gate was open. The naval infantry were watching. The question was a test — she recognised the shape of it, the way a flag officer tested a subordinate, the way a diplomat tested a counterpart.

"I expect you to do what you are doing," she said. "You have contained us to a single pier. You have posted guards at the gate. You have cleared the waterfront. You have established control of the situation before beginning any exchange. Those are the actions of a competent commander processing an unprecedented event. I respect the containment. I am asking only for the exchange."

[[Konrad Verner|Verner]] looked at her for a long moment. Then he nodded — a single motion, the kind of nod a man gave when a decision had been made and he was now committed to its consequences.

"Dr. Ishihara. Welcome to Odiha. I will hear what you have to say." He gestured toward the Port Authority Building — the two-storey stone structure at the landward end of the pier, the corner office on the second floor with the view of the harbour and the mountains and the river and the three ships that belonged to no navy he had ever commanded. "My office is this way. Your crew — " he paused. "Your crew and the [[Pelikan]] crew will remain at Pier 3. The pier is confined. The gate is guarded. No one leaves without my authorisation. Those conditions remain in effect until the [[Marleyan Empire|Marleyan]] government issues a formal response. Do you accept."

"Yes."

"Then let us talk."

They walked together — the admiral and the diplomat, the limping man and the woman in the blue coat, past the gate, past the guards, toward the stone building at the edge of the harbour where the first conversation between Marley and another world was about to begin. Behind them, the fourteen Marleyan crew stood on the deck of the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] and watched their admiral walk toward his office with a stranger, and the gate closed, and the guards resumed their positions, and the pier was quiet except for the sound of the water and the gulls and the distant hum of a gas turbine that no Marleyan engineer had ever built.

### Scene 6: The Conversation — Day 118

[[Konrad Verner|Verner]]'s office was a corner room on the second floor of the Port Authority Building — wood-panelled, narrow-windowed, furnished with a desk that had belonged to three admirals before him and a chair that had been reupholstered twice and a bookshelf that held navigational charts and coastal pilots and a single framed photograph of a woman who had been dead for twelve years. The window faced the harbour. From his desk, [[Konrad Verner|Verner]] could see the three ships at Pier 3 — the [[Pelikan]], small and dark and familiar; the [[CATF Envoy]], white and clean and silent; the [[Southern Venture]], grey and laden and patient. The ships were not moving. The pier gate was closed. The guards were at their posts. The situation was contained, as contained as an unprecedented event could be.

[[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] sat across the desk from him. She had brought one person — [[Seo Min-ah|Seo]], the translator, who sat slightly behind her and to the left, a notebook open on her lap, her expression the calm, attentive blankness of a linguist who was prepared to intervene if the diplomat's functional Marleyan failed. [[Konrad Verner|Verner]] had his adjutant — Drexler, standing by the door, notebook also open, also writing, also trying to capture a conversation that would need to be reported to a capital that would not believe it.

The tea was Marleyan — black, bitter, brewed in the same iron pot that the harbour master shared with the signal tower. [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] drank it without comment. She had been offered a seat and had taken it. She had been offered tea and had accepted it. The rituals were the same rituals that began every diplomatic meeting in every culture she had ever studied. The strangeness was not the ritual. The strangeness was the context.

[[Konrad Verner|Verner]] began. "Dr. Ishihara. You said you represent a coalition of four nations. Which four."

"Japan. The Republic of Korea. The People's Republic of China. The United States of America."

The names meant nothing to him. She could see it on his face — the moment of processing, the search through a mental catalogue of nations that did not include any of the four. Marley knew Eldia. Marley knew the Mid-East Allied Forces. Marley knew Hizuru. These nations — Japan, Korea, China, America — did not correspond to any power on any map in any archive in any Marleyan intelligence division.

"These nations," [[Konrad Verner|Verner]] said. "Where are they."

"On another Earth," [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] said. She said it the way she had practiced it — level, uninflected, the tone of a person delivering a factual statement rather than a metaphysical proposition. "Approximately one hundred and twelve days ago, a spatial anomaly opened in the ocean south of your world's equator — and south of ours. The anomaly is a gate. It connects two versions of the same planet — identical geography, diverging histories. We call it the Gate. Your expedition vessel, the [[Pelikan]], drifted into our exclusion zone approximately seventy-eight days ago. We intercepted her. We detained her crew — under humane conditions, which your sailors can confirm. We have since learned your language, studied your world, and identified your political geography. We are now making contact with the [[Marleyan Empire]] — the first AOT world power we have approached directly — to offer a communication channel between our coalition and your government. We are not here to threaten. We are not here to conquer. We are here to establish a relationship."

The words sat in the room. The fire in the grate crackled. The adjutant's pen had stopped moving. [[Konrad Verner|Verner]] was looking at her with an expression that she had seen twice before in her career — once during a negotiation with a North Korean delegation that had just been told about the existence of a hydrophone array they had not detected, once during a hostage handover in Singapore when the hostage-taker had realised the building was surrounded. It was the expression of a person who had just been told something that was not fiction and not fact and not a lie and not a truth — something that occupied the category of *information the mind has not yet learned to process*.

"Another Earth," he said.

"Yes."

"A gate."

"Yes."

"One hundred and twelve days ago."

"Approximately."

"And you have been studying us. Since the [[Pelikan]] was captured."

"Since before the [[Pelikan]] was captured. We identified your ports from the air approximately seventy days ago. We have been surveilling the [[Marleyan Empire]], the [[Mid-East Allied Forces]], and the island of [[Paradis Island|Paradis]] for — " she paused, calculating, " — approximately six weeks. We have not been detected. We will not be detected unless we choose to be. We are choosing, now, to be detected. Because we want to talk rather than watch, and because your expedition's return provided an opportunity to begin the conversation with a familiar face rather than a stranger."

[[Konrad Verner|Verner]] leaned back in his chair. The leather creaked. The fire crackled. Outside the window, the three ships at Pier 3 had not moved. The [[Pelikan]] was real. The white ship was real. The vehicles on the grey ship's deck were real. The woman in his office was real. The story she was telling was impossible. The evidence she had brought was in his harbour.

"If what you are saying is true," [[Konrad Verner|Verner]] said, "then you represent a power whose capabilities exceed anything in the [[Marleyan Empire|Marleyan]] arsenal. You have machines that fly without Titans. You have ships that move without steam. You have weapons — " he paused, " — you have not shown me weapons. But you have implied them. And you are asking me to believe that you have come to talk rather than to fight. History does not offer many examples of powerful strangers who arrived to talk rather than to fight."

"History on this Earth has one example now," [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] said. "We are it."

She opened the folder she had carried from the ship — a slim binder, navy blue, the CATF insignia embossed on the cover. Inside: a single-page document in Marleyan, typed on the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]]'s printer during the voyage, reviewed by [[Seo Min-ah|Seo]] and [[Jürgen Haas|Haas]] and three other Marleyan crew for accuracy of language and cultural tone. She placed it on the desk.

"This is our initial communication," she said. "It contains a radio frequency — a standing channel that your government can use to reach us. It contains a statement of intent — non-aggression, non-interference, respecting Marleyan sovereignty and territorial integrity. It contains no demands. No threats. No deadlines. It is an open door. You may walk through it when you are ready, or you may not. The door will remain open."

[[Konrad Verner|Verner]] looked at the document. He did not touch it. The adjutant stepped forward and picked it up — carefully, the way a man picked up a device he suspected of being a weapon. He scanned it. Looked at [[Konrad Verner|Verner]]. Nodded — the language was diplomatic, the terms were neutral, the document was what it claimed to be.

"I must report this to the capital," [[Konrad Verner|Verner]] said. "The High Command. The general staff. Possibly the war council. The response will not be immediate. The response may be hostile. You understand this."

"I understand it. We are prepared for it. We will remain at Pier 3 until the capital responds. We will not leave the pier. We will not attempt to enter the city. The [[Pelikan]] crew will remain with us — they have been invaluable as advisors, and I would like to thank them personally before we depart. They will sleep on board the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] tonight. That is — " she paused, " — a trust signal. They are your sailors. They could leave our ship at any time and walk through the gate to you. They are choosing to stay with us. I hope you read that as a statement about the conditions of their captivity and the nature of our intentions."

[[Konrad Verner|Verner]] looked at the document. Looked at the ships in the harbour. Looked at the woman in the blue coat who had walked the length of a stone pier to meet him alone. He had been a naval officer for forty years. He had commanded ships and squadrons and districts. He had fought in two wars. He had signed thirty-one condolence letters for a lost expedition that was now tied to his pier. He had never, in forty years, faced a decision that had no precedent, no protocol, and no path of retreat.

"The [[Pelikan]] crew will remain on your ship tonight," he said. "Until the capital responds — the same conditions apply to everyone on this pier. Marleyan or foreign, you are all confined to Pier 3. The gate stays closed. I will not bar my own people from their country indefinitely, but I will not relax the containment until I have word from above. You understand."

"Accepted."

"I will transmit your document to the capital by telegraph. The response time — I cannot predict. The capital is eight hundred kilometres north. The general staff will convene when they convene. The war council will review when they review. You may be waiting for days."

"We are prepared to wait."

[[Konrad Verner|Verner]] nodded. He stood — the chair scraping against the wooden floor, his knee protesting, his hand pressing against the desk for balance. [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] stood as well. The meeting was over. The conversation had happened. The channel was open.

"Dr. Ishihara," [[Konrad Verner|Verner]] said. "I do not know what you are. I do not know if your story is true. But you have returned my ship and my sailors. You have spoken my language. You have walked into my office without a weapon or a guard. And you have given me a document that asks for nothing and threatens nothing and leaves me free to report what I have seen. If you are lying, you are the most careful liar I have ever met. If you are telling the truth — " he paused. "Then the world I have served for forty years is not the world I thought it was. I will need time to adjust to that."

"Take the time," [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] said. "The channel will be open when you are ready."

She walked to the door. [[Seo Min-ah|Seo]] followed, her notebook closed, her job for the day unexpectedly light. At the threshold, [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] paused.

"Admiral. There is one more thing. The crew of the [[Pelikan]] — the fourteen who returned. They have been in our custody for seventy-eight days. They have been treated with dignity. They volunteered for this mission. They are not collaborators. They are not traitors. They are sailors who did their duty and then did the harder thing — they helped two worlds talk to each other. When you write your report, I ask that you remember that."

[[Konrad Verner|Verner]] looked at her. Then he looked out the window, at the fourteen figures on the deck of the white ship, at the gate that separated them from their country, at the harbour that had not changed and the world that had.

"I will remember," he said.

### Scene 7: The Pier at Dusk — Day 118

[[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] walked back through the gate alone.

The sun was setting behind the mountains — a grey sky dissolving into grey water, the peaks to the north catching the last light in a band of orange that looked like fire on stone. The pier was quiet. The [[Pelikan]] was dark, her boiler banked, her crew aboard the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]]. The [[Southern Venture]] was dark as well. The [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] was lit — deck lights casting warm white glow across the stone, electric light that no Marleyan harbour had ever seen.

At the top of the gangway, [[Takeda Kaito|Takeda]] was waiting. "Chief. The admiral's office signalled ten minutes ago. The telegraph to the capital has been sent. No response expected before tomorrow. The harbour master has entered the three ships in the port log. [[Pelikan]]: returned expedition vessel. [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]]: foreign diplomatic vessel, nation unknown. [[Southern Venture]]: foreign cargo vessel, nation unknown. The notation says *occupants confined to Pier 3 pending capital instruction*."

"That is accurate," Ishihara said. "That is what we are."

Takeda lowered his tablet. "Chief. How did it go."

She looked at the harbour. The lights of the city. The mountains. The signal tower. The gate at the end of the pier with its eight guards and its iron grill and its lamp-posts that had never illuminated anything stranger than a cargo shipment. She thought about the admiral's face when she had told him about the other Earth — the specific stillness of a man absorbing a truth that did not fit any drawer in the filing cabinet of his mind. She thought about the submarine, somewhere beyond the breakwater, listening through its sonar array to the harbour sounds and waiting for a signal that would not come.

"It went," she said. "We opened a door. We did not walk through it. We left it standing, and we are waiting to see if the person on the other side will open it from their side."

"That is diplomacy."

"That is the beginning of diplomacy. Diplomacy itself is what happens after someone walks through the door. No one has walked through yet."

She turned from the rail. The deck lights were bright. The [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]]'s mess was serving dinner — the steward had prepared a meal from the ship's stores, and the fourteen Marleyan crew were eating at the long table, their voices low, their faces tired, their movements carrying the specific weight of people who had been at sea for ten days and were now at port and were not allowed to disembark and were not sure if they wanted to.

She walked into the mess. The fourteen Marleyan crew were eating at the long table. The conversation paused when she entered.

"I have spoken to Admiral Verner," she said in Marleyan. "He has your report. The capital has been notified. For now — we remain at Pier 3. All of us. The gate stays closed until the capital responds. I am sorry. You are home, and you cannot step past that gate."

No one stood. No one spoke.

[[Jürgen Haas|Haas]] was at the end of the table. He looked at her, then at the plate of food in front of him. "I will stay tonight. Tomorrow — I don't know. The gate is closed. Maybe that is easier. I do not have to decide whether to walk through it. The decision is made for me."

One by one, the other thirteen nodded.

Ishihara walked to her cabin. The cabin was small — a berth, a desk, a porthole that looked onto the pier. She sat at the desk. She opened her notebook — the same olive-green notebook that [[Park Sung-ho|Captain Park]] had carried across Paradis, the kind that CATF issued to all liaison officers. She wrote:

*Day 118. Odiha. First contact achieved. Channel open. Admiral Verner is professional, sceptical, and listening. The containment to Pier 3 is absolute — no one leaves, not even the returning Pelikan crew, until the capital responds. I do not know if we have made a friend, an ally, or an enemy. I know we have made contact, and contact is the prerequisite for all three.*

*The submarine is still out there. I have not needed it. I do not believe I will need it. But knowing it is there has let me speak without fear. That is the function of a backup — not to be used, but to make its non-use possible.*

She closed the notebook. Somewhere in the city, a telegraph was clicking. Somewhere in the capital — eight hundred kilometres north — a document was being read by men who would decide whether the door she had opened would stay open.

The hardest part of diplomacy was not the opening. It was the waiting.

### Scene 8: Haas — Day 118, Night

The bunk was on the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]]'s lower deck — a crew cabin, four berths, currently occupied by [[Jürgen Haas|Haas]] alone. The cabin was small and clean and smelled of the ship's ventilation system rather than the coal and salt of a Marleyan naval berth.

He lay on his back and stared at the ceiling.

He had been on the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] for ten days now — ten days of meals in the mess and conversations in broken tongues and evening watches on the afterdeck with sailors who were not his countrymen and were not his enemies and had become, across the slow arc of the voyage, something he did not have a word for. He had told his admiral the truth this morning. He had stood at the pier gate and faced Verner across the iron grill. He had looked past the admiral at the city — the stone buildings, the narrow streets, the mountains beyond — and he had smelled, through the gap in the gate, the coal smoke and the fish and the wet stone of the home he had not seen in seventy-eight days.

And then he had walked back down the pier. He had not been ordered. He had not been coerced. The gate had been closed by the time he turned around. But the question remained: if it had been open, would he have walked through?

He did not know. He thought about it, staring at the ceiling, and the reasons were a list that did not add up to a conclusion.

One: the other thirteen had stayed. He was the senior sailor. If he crossed the gate, they would follow. If he stayed, they would stay. He was not ready to make the decision for all of them. He was not sure any of them were ready.

Two: the city beyond the waterfront was his home, but it was also a city that had declared him dead seventy-eight days ago. His father had received a condolence letter. His name had been read at a memorial service he had not attended because he had been, at that moment, eating reheated rice in a CATF mess hall on a subantarctic island. The city had moved on. He had not. He was not ready to walk through streets that had already buried him.

Three: the woman in the blue coat had walked the length of the pier alone. She had stood at the gate and spoken Marleyan with an accent that belonged nowhere and a composure that belonged in a court. She had not asked him to stay. She had not told him he was free to go — because neither of them was free to go, not until the capital answered. But the fact that she had not asked — that she had treated him as someone who could choose, even when there was no choice to make — was the reason he was still here. A person who demanded loyalty deserved suspicion. A person who offered dignity, even in confinement, deserved — he did not know what. But it was something.

He closed his eyes. The ship's ventilation hummed. The water lapped against the hull. Through the porthole, the harbour lights were dim — the signal tower, the Port Authority, the scatter of windows on the mountain. The gate was closed. The guards were at their posts. The world had changed, and the city had not noticed yet.

He thought about the morning. The gate would still be closed. The capital had not responded. He would stand on the pier again and look through the iron grill at the city that had buried him. And he would wait — the same waiting that the white ship was doing, the same waiting that the admiral was doing, the same waiting that the entire harbour was suspended in. The waiting was not a choice. It was the only thing any of them could do.

He pulled the blanket up to his chin. The blanket was synthetic. The ship hummed around him — a machine built by a civilisation that had not existed for him three months ago. He closed his eyes. He slept. And in the water beyond the breakwater, the submarine that had been listening since dawn maintained its vigil, its captain drinking coffee and watching contacts and waiting for a signal that would not come.

## Key Decisions

- Fourteen Marleyan crew of the [[Pelikan]] volunteer to sail home and serve as diplomatic advisors — no Eldians, no [[Klaus Dreher]], no [[Elias Vogler]]
- The three-ship group — [[Pelikan]] leading, [[CATF Envoy]] and [[Southern Venture]] following at 500 metres — crosses 3,000 km of ocean over ten days
- [[Jürgen Haas]] serves as the senior Marleyan liaison, drilling Ishihara on Marleyan naval protocol
- A US nuclear attack submarine — USS *Annapolis* — shadows the group at periscope depth throughout; Ishihara is the only person aboard the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] who knows it is there
- Verner's first order: all three ships to Pier 3 — isolated berth, gate guarded, no one leaves until the capital responds
- [[Jürgen Haas|Haas]] descends the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]]'s gangway and meets Verner at the pier gate — a familiar face first
- [[Satomi Ishihara|Ishihara]] walks the length of Pier 3 alone — a deliberate vulnerability signal
- In Verner's office, Ishihara lays out the Gate, the coalition, and the offer of a communication channel — a radio frequency only, no reciprocal envoy or facility access
- Verner transmits the CATF document to the capital by telegraph; no response expected before the following day
- The [[Pelikan]] crew sleep on the [[CATF Envoy|Envoy]] — fourteen Marleyan nationals confined alongside their foreign hosts, the gate closed at their backs
- [[Jürgen Haas|Haas]] lies awake in a borrowed bunk — his city on the other side of the gate, a condolence letter waiting in his father's house, tomorrow uncertain
