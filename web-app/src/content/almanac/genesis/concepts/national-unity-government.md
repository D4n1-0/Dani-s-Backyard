# National Unity Government

## Summary
The cross-party coalition government led by [[Stephen Davenport]], formed after a constitutional crisis that is never detailed but remains an unresolved pressure on every decision. Davenport governs with one hand tied — his coalition is fragile, his opposition watches from the wings, and the Anomaly has landed on top of a domestic crisis that never paused for interdimensional events.

## Details

The NUG was stitched together after a domestic rupture — likely a constitutional crisis (the specific nature is left deliberately unspecified in Chapter 04) — that made "business as usual" unviable. Old enemies agreed to share power because the alternative was worse. The economy is stabilising but brittle. The Chancellor is under constant pressure to fund domestic recovery. The Home Secretary is overwhelmed by public order concerns that predate the Anomaly.

The NUG's fragility constrains Davenport across every decision: he cannot afford the political capital that a full, transparent engagement with the Faithful would require. His "cautious secular engagement" doctrine is as much a product of domestic necessity as philosophical conviction.

## Context

The NUG shapes every scene in Chapter 04 without ever being the subject of one. It is:
- The reason Davenport cannot call a snap election or pursue a bold alternative
- The reason the Chancellor is watching every pound
- The reason the Home Secretary is anxious about public order
- The subtext beneath Davenport's insistence on secular neutrality — he literally cannot afford a religious dimension to the crisis
