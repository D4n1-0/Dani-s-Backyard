# Chapter 4 – Across the Silver Line

## Summary

Five weeks. The Seoul-Beijing framework expands into a four-nation coalition under the [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|Combined Anomaly Task Force]], formalised by the Four-Power Gate Protocol signed in Singapore. Over the next four weeks, [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] executes a meticulously planned covert build-up on the AOT side — a forward operating base on the [[Alfred Faure Forward Operating Base|Alfred Faure]] Islands, a full-spectrum command-and-logistics hub at [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]], and a network of undersea hydrophone cables running a 100-kilometre arc. Long-range surveillance drones map thousands of kilometres of coastline, identify the major AOT factions from flags, ship movements, infrastructure patterns, and the unmistakeable Walls, and pinpoint key geopolitical boundaries. By the end of the chapter, [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] has a secure foothold, a comprehensive strategic picture, and its first native informants — the crew of a Marleyan polar expedition ship that drifted into the perimeter and was intercepted. The world on the far side of the Silver Line is not merely a geographical mirror. It is a political powder keg. And [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] has just been seen.

## Scenes

### Week 1 — The Treaty — Day 8–14

Singapore was chosen because it belonged to no one at the table.

The city had hosted summits between rivals and allies, trade negotiations and armistice signings, the quiet diplomatic labour that kept the machinery of the world from seizing. It was neutral ground of a particular kind — not ideologically neutral, but practically so, a place where power could talk to power without the baggage of territory. On the morning of Day 8, four cars departed from four separate arrival points at Changi Airport and converged on a conference facility in the Tanglin district. The facility was a low-rise colonial-era building that had been repurposed for secure diplomatic work — soundproofed, electronically shielded, its windows replaced with blast-resistant glass. It was the kind of building that existed to be forgotten.

[[Lee Jun-ho]] arrived first. He had flown from Seoul on a military transport that had been diverted from a routine logistics run, the fiction of normalcy maintained by the simple expedient of not explaining why a Blue House crisis manager was travelling on a Sunday. He was accompanied by a single aide and a briefcase containing the joint finding that [[Ahn Jae-hyun|Dr. Ahn Jae-hyun]] and [[Wang Lijuan|Dr. Wang Lijuan]] had co-authored three days earlier — the document that proved the AOT world was an inverted Earth. He had read it four times on the flight. It did not become less impossible with repetition.

[[Zheng Weimin]] arrived second. He had flown from Beijing on a civilian Air China flight, travelling under a diplomatic passport and a false itinerary, the layers of misdirection a habit of a lifetime in Chinese statecraft. He was accompanied by a single aide and a tablet containing the same joint finding, forwarded to him by [[Wang Lijuan|Wang]]'s office with a cover note that read only: *"They know."* [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] had read the note three times. It was the most unsettling communication he had received since the Gate opened.

[[Elizabeth Cross]] arrived third. She was the Deputy Secretary of State of the United States, a career Foreign Service officer who had served as Ambassador to Seoul before her elevation to Washington, and she was one of perhaps five people in the American government who spoke fluent Korean. The President had selected her for the delegation personally — not for her Korean, which was useful, but for her temperament, which was steady. She had been briefed on the Gate at 03:00 Washington time, four hours before her flight, and she had spent the intervening hours reading every intercept, every satellite image, every fragment of analysis the intelligence community had produced. By the time she landed in Singapore, she understood the situation better than most of the people who had been managing it for a week. She was accompanied by a military aide — a Navy captain who said nothing and noted everything.

[[Nakamura Kenji]] arrived last. He was the Vice Foreign Minister of Japan, a career diplomat who had served postings in Beijing, Washington, and Seoul, and he spoke all three languages with the quiet precision of a man who had learned that words were weapons and deployed them accordingly. He carried a file that his own government's signals intelligence division had compiled — intercepts of encrypted traffic between Seoul and Beijing that JMSDF listening posts in the East China Sea had been collecting for a week. The intercepts were fragmented, incomplete, but sufficient. Japan had known about the Gate within thirty-six hours of its opening. [[Nakamura Kenji|Nakamura]] had been waiting for the invitation. When it came, he was not surprised. He was prepared.

The four principals sat at a conference table that had been chosen for its lack of symbolic weight — plain wood, no national insignia, no flags. The aides sat against the walls. The doors were sealed. The negotiation began.

---

[[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] opened. It was his framework, his negotiation, and he had earned the right to set the agenda.

"Eight days ago," he said, "a South Korean coast guard patrol vessel discovered a stable portal connecting the Yellow Sea to an alternate Earth. The portal is 500 metres across, 200 metres high, half-submerged. It has not closed. It has not degraded. It has not, to the best of our combined scientific knowledge, demonstrated any sign of instability or impermanence. It is, for all operational purposes, a permanent geographical feature."

He paused. The room was silent.

"The alternate Earth is an inverted version of our own — identical landmasses, identical geology, identical celestial positions, but north-to-south inverted and inhabited by a human civilisation with approximately 1920s-equivalent technology. This civilisation is divided into at least four major political entities, and at least two of them are currently at war. We do not yet know the full extent of their capabilities, their ideologies, or their intentions. What we do know is that the Gate is open, it is not closing, and the status quo — a two-nation standoff on the Yellow Sea — is not sustainable."

He looked around the table. "I am not here to ask for permission to continue managing the Gate. I am here to ask for help building a framework that can survive what is coming."

[[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] spoke next. His Mandarin was translated in real time through the facility's interpretation system, but the transcript — the permanent record of what was said — would be in English, the neutral language of the treaty that did not yet exist.

"Senior Secretary [[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] and I have managed the Gate for eight days under a framework of mutual restraint. It has held. But it was designed for two parties and one problem. The problem is now larger than either of our governments can manage alone. The Americans are already present — the [[USS Milius (DDG-69)]] has been on station for two days. The Japanese have been listening — " he glanced at [[Nakamura Kenji|Nakamura]], who did not react, " — and they know as much as we do. A two-party framework is no longer adequate. We need a four-party framework. We need it before the situation on the AOT side produces a crisis that the framework was never designed to handle."

[[Elizabeth Cross|Cross]] leaned forward. "Deputy Director [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]]. Senior Secretary [[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]]. The United States is prepared to participate in a multilateral framework. But I need to be clear about what that means. We will not accept a structure in which American assets operate under foreign command. We will not accept a structure in which our access to the Gate is conditioned on the consent of any single party. And we will not accept a structure that excludes the right of independent action if American national security is threatened."

"That is three things you will not accept," [[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] said. "What will you accept?"

"A standing combined command with rotational authority. Each nation retains operational control of its own forces. Strategic decisions — including rules of engagement, first-contact protocols, and the deployment of assets through the Gate — require consensus of all four parties. No unilateral action within the AOT exclusion zone or the Earth-side perimeter. Full intelligence sharing. Joint scientific oversight. And a public face that gives the international community something to look at while the real work happens in the classified annex."

[[Nakamura Kenji|Nakamura]] spoke for the first time. His voice was soft, unhurried, the voice of a man who had been waiting to say something for a very long time.

"Japan will join this framework on three conditions. First — the Gate is in the Yellow Sea, which is Japan's strategic backyard. We require a permanent seat on the executive body, not observer status. Second — the scientific committee must include Japanese oceanographers and geologists. We have the world's leading deep-sea research capacity. If you exclude us from the science, you exclude the expertise you need. Third — " he paused, " — I would like to know who has been transmitting encrypted traffic on the 225.7 megahertz band for the last three days."

The room went still.

[[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] looked at [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]]. [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] looked at [[Nakamura Kenji|Nakamura]]. [[Elizabeth Cross|Cross]] looked at her military aide, who shook his head almost imperceptibly.

"Explain," [[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] said.

"The frequency is used by a signals intelligence satellite in geostationary orbit over the western Pacific. The satellite is Japanese. The traffic it is intercepting originates from the AOT side of the Gate — specifically, from a source approximately 800 kilometres north of the Gate threshold, in the AOT world's equivalent of the northern hemisphere. The traffic is in the clear. It is not encrypted. It is a voice transmission. The language is not any terrestrial language, but the phonetic structure is consistent with Germanic roots."

[[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]]'s expression did not change, but his hands, resting on the table, tightened. "You are telling us the AOT side has radio."

"I am telling you," [[Nakamura Kenji|Nakamura]] said, "that the AOT side has radio. Marley — if the geography matches — has radio. And if Marley has radio, it has a military command structure. And if it has a military command structure, it will eventually notice that something has changed in its southern ocean."

The silence that followed was the silence of four people recalibrating their threat assessments in real time.

"We need to accelerate," [[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] said. "The treaty. The command structure. The deployment authorisation. All of it. Can we have language by tonight?"

---

The Four-Power Gate Protocol was drafted across the next fifty-two hours. The negotiating teams worked in shifts, the principals rotating through the conference room and the secure communications suites, the draft evolving through seventeen iterations before the final version was approved at 04:17 on Day 12.

The public face was the Joint Gate Research Initiative — a multilateral scientific cooperation agreement that extended the Seoul-Beijing exclusion zone to include American and Japanese research vessels under a coordinated protocol. The language was deliberately vague, a masterpiece of diplomatic opacity: *"recognising the unprecedented nature of the Yellow Sea anomaly"* and *"committing to the peaceful and transparent investigation of its properties."* It named no threats. It claimed no territory. It was the treaty the world would read in the newspapers and promptly forget.

The classified annex was the [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|Combined Anomaly Task Force]].

[[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] was a standing combined military command with rotational authority. Each nation retained operational control of its own forces. Strategic decisions — rules of engagement, first-contact protocols, deployment of assets through the Gate — required consensus of all four parties via a quadripartite executive committee. The committee members: [[Lee Jun-ho]] for the Republic of Korea, [[Zheng Weimin]] for the People's Republic of China, [[Elizabeth Cross]] for the United States, [[Nakamura Kenji]] for Japan.

The annex authorised the following operations, to commence immediately upon ratification under the [[Construction Progress]] timeline:

- [[Alfred Faure Forward Operating Base|Construction of a forward operating base on the AOT-side Alfred Faure Islands]]
- [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Construction of a deep-water logistics hub and fleet anchorage at Grande Terre (AOT-side Kerguelen)]]
- [[Undersea Sensor Array|Deployment of undersea hydrophone arrays for acoustic surveillance and perimeter security]]
- Long-range UAV reconnaissance of the AOT world, with all data shared in real time
- Deployment of a standing CATF naval force on the AOT side of the Gate
- [[First Contact Protocol]]: intercept, isolate, inform, integrate

No provision was made for returning the AOT world to its previous isolation. No provision was made for closing the Gate. The treaty did not address these questions because no one at the table knew the answers, and the treaty was not a document of answers. It was a document of preparation.

The four principals signed at 06:00 on Day 13, in a ceremony witnessed by no one but their aides. There were no photographs. There was no press release. The world, waking to its Monday morning, did not know that four nations had just agreed to occupy an alternate Earth.

[[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] flew back to Seoul at 09:00. On the plane, he opened his briefcase and removed the signed protocol. He read it once — the whole thing, the public clauses and the classified annex, the careful architecture of a framework that had been designed to manage the unmanageable. Then he closed the briefcase and looked out the window at the East China Sea, two hundred kilometres of blue water stretching to a horizon he could no longer see without thinking of the Silver Line beyond it.

The treaty was signed. The coalition was formed. The work was about to begin.

### Week 2 — The Beachhead — Day 15–21

The first Landing Craft Air Cushion crossed the Gate at 03:17 on Day 15.

The LCAC-47 was a US Navy hovercraft operating from the well deck of the [[USS Milius (DDG-69)|USS Milius]], which had been repositioned to the Gate threshold under a quiet revision of its original freedom-of-navigation orders. The LCAC carried a SEABEE construction detachment, a Korean engineering company, forty tons of prefab shelter modules, and one bulldozer. It rode its cushion of air across the Yellow Sea water, hit the boundary between worlds with a shudder that rattled every hull plate, and emerged on the far side in a different ocean under different stars.

The AOT side was dark. It was always dark at this hour — the Gate's threshold synchronised local time, and [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] had chosen 03:00 for all initial crossings, when the austral winter night provided maximum cover. The LCAC's crew wore night-vision equipment. The SEABEEs wore cold-weather gear. The bulldozer sat on the deck like a monument to a civilisation that had solved problems with hydraulics and diesel.

Fifteen kilometres to the west, the [[Inverted Alfred Faure Islands]] rose from the black water — volcanic silhouettes against a sky of unfamiliar constellations. The islands had been surveyed by drone on Day 9, a KUS-15 mapping every ridge, every cove, every patch of tussock grass in two hours of low-altitude flight. The survey had identified a landing site on the eastern shore of the main island: a pebble beach with a gentle gradient, sheltered from the prevailing wind, backed by a plateau of volcanic scree that could be graded into a runway.

The LCAC hit the beach at 03:31.

The SEABEEs deployed first — clearing the landing zone, establishing a security perimeter, marking the ground with infrared chem-lights visible only to their night-vision gear. The Korean engineers followed, manhandling the first shelter modules onto the volcanic rock. The bulldozer rolled off the LCAC under its own power and began scraping a path from the beach to the plateau, its diesel engine the loudest sound on an island that had never before heard a machine.

By dawn — a grey, cold dawn that broke at 05:52 local — the first three shelters stood. By noon, the runway survey was complete: 1,200 metres of compacted volcanic scree could support a C-130J with room to spare. By evening, the LCAC had made six more transits through the Gate, delivering fuel bladders, communications gear, a satellite terminal that locked onto a Japanese relay satellite orbiting over the western Pacific, and the advance team of the drone intelligence section.

Captain [[Marcus Rivera]] took command at 18:00 on Day 15. He was a forty-four-year-old Civil Engineer Corps officer who had built airstrips in Afghanistan and piers in the Philippines, and he looked at the volcanic island the way a carpenter looks at uncut timber. He saw a project. The ontological shock of standing on another Earth — the wrong stars, the wrong water, the wrong position on a globe that was identical and alien — he processed and filed. There was work to do.

"Runway first," he said to his engineering chief, a Korean Army major named Kim who had been assigned to the [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] construction element on twenty-four hours' notice. "If we can put a Herc on this island, we can put anything. Pier second. Shelters third. Everything else can wait."

"I need aggregate," Major Kim said.

"Basalt," [[Marcus Rivera|Rivera]] said. "The whole island is basalt. Crush it. Use the bulldozer. I'll have a rock crusher through the Gate tomorrow."

"Water?"

"Desalination unit on the next LCAC. Fuel first."

Major Kim nodded. He was thirty-eight, a combat engineer who had built bridges under fire near the DMZ, and he had never expected to be grading a runway on an alternate Earth. He had been told only that the mission was classified and that the location was not on any map. He had decided, somewhere between the Gate and the beach, that the classification was adequate.

---

[[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] was the second beachhead.

The advance team landed on Day 16 — a mixed detachment of Korean and Chinese engineers, deployed from the civilian research motherships [[R-V Isabu]] and [[Xiang Yang Hong 06]], which had been retasked to serve as floating staging bases for the construction effort. [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] was forty kilometres southwest of the Gate, a massive volcanic archipelago that in the terrestrial world was the Kerguelen Islands — a French subantarctic territory with a single research station and a population of fifty. On the AOT side, the inverted Kerguelen was empty. No research station. No population. No evidence that any human being had ever set foot on its shores.

The harbour was a deep-water fjord cutting into the eastern coast of the main island — sheltered on three sides by volcanic ridges, deep enough for a carrier group, large enough to anchor the entire [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] fleet. The survey team worked for three days, mapping the approaches with side-scan sonar, drilling core samples of the seabed for pier foundations, marking the sites for fuel farms and warehouse modules. The island was barren — tussock grass, moss, the occasional cluster of subantarctic seabirds nesting on cliff ledges. It was cold. It was wind-scoured. It was, in the assessment of the Chinese engineering officer who led the initial survey, "the most useful piece of empty rock in either hemisphere."

The pontoon causeway arrived on Day 18 — modular floating sections fabricated in a Korean shipyard, towed through the Gate in a chain that stretched half a kilometre, assembled by a combined crew of Korean and Chinese engineers working in twelve-hour shifts. When the first truck crossed the causeway — a five-ton flatbed carrying a prefab command module — the engineers on the shore cheered. It was the first time a vehicle had driven onto AOT soil under its own power. It would not be the last.

Lieutenant Colonel [[Tanaka Kenji]] arrived on Day 19, deploying from the [[Xiang Yang Hong 06]] aboard a Chinese utility boat that ferried him and his equipment across the twenty kilometres of open water to the [[R-V Isabu]], where the drone intelligence section had been established in a converted laboratory module. He was forty-six years old, a JGSDF intelligence officer who had spent the last decade analysing satellite imagery of North Korean missile sites and Chinese naval bases. He had been assigned to [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] on twelve hours' notice and told only that his target set was "undefined." He had been given a KUS-15 drone, a team of four analysts — two Korean, one American, one Chinese — and a directive: find the coast.

At 06:00 on Day 20, the first long-range reconnaissance sortie launched.

The KUS-15 crossed the Gate at a cruising altitude of 20,000 feet, climbing into the grey austral winter sky, its turbocharged engine a thin whine that faded to nothing within seconds. Its payload was a stabilised optical turret with infra-red and low-light capability, a synthetic-aperture radar for ground mapping, and a signals intelligence package tuned to the 200–400 megahertz band — the frequency range [[Nakamura Kenji|Nakamura]]'s intercept had identified.

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] sat in the drone control module aboard the [[R-V Isabu]], watching the feed on a wall of screens. The KUS-15's camera showed the [[Inverted Alfred Faure Islands]] receding to the south, then open water — grey, empty, featureless. The drone flew north for four hours, covering 600 kilometres, finding nothing. The AOT world's southern ocean was as empty as the hydrophone array had suggested.

On Day 21, the KUS-15 found the coast.

It was a rocky shoreline backed by low hills, roughly 900 kilometres north of the Gate. The drone's camera showed no cities, no ports, no roads — a wilderness of scrub forest and volcanic ridges. [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] cross-referenced the coastline against the terrestrial map of the same geography, inverted. It matched the southern coast of Africa. It was uninhabited. It was, his analysts noted, the first time any human being had seen this coast — the real version or its inverted twin — from above.

The coast was empty. But beyond it lay the continent. And beyond the continent lay the people.

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] ordered the drone to continue north.

### Week 3 — The Map — Day 22–28

The hydrophone array achieved initial operating capability on Day 22.

It was a network of forty passive acoustic sensors laid in a 100-kilometre arc north of the Gate, each node connected by armoured fibre-optic cable to a processing station on the seafloor near the [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] anchorage. The array was designed to detect engine noise from AOT-native vessels — steam engines, predominantly, whose acoustic signatures had been modelled using data from preserved terrestrial steamships. The array was sensitive enough to pick up a single reciprocating engine at 200 kilometres. It was quiet enough to operate without revealing its presence — the cables were buried, the nodes were bottom-mounted, and the acoustic data was transmitted through the undersea fibre to [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] for analysis.

The first contact registered at 14:53 on Day 22: a mechanical thumping at four beats per second, Doppler-shifted slightly north, consistent with a single-screw steamship running at eight knots. Bearing 0-2-7. Range: 185 kilometres. Duration: three hours, seventeen minutes. The track curved northwest and faded into the ambient noise floor. It was, the acoustic analysts concluded, a routine transit along a coastal shipping route — the first confirmed evidence of AOT-native maritime activity.

The same day, the first KUS-15 reached inhabited territory.

The drone had been flying for nine hours, covering over 1,400 kilometres of coastline, when its camera found a port city. The city spread along a natural harbour, its waterfront lined with stone quays and iron cranes. Steamships crowded the docks — flat-bottomed freighters, passenger vessels with tall funnels, and the low, lethal shapes of warships: broadside ironclads with gunports visible even from 20,000 feet. Flags flew from the harbour buildings — a red field bearing a cross device that the analysts would spend three days identifying.

The drone orbited at altitude, its camera recording everything. The streets were cobbled. The architecture was European — stone and iron and coal smoke, a London or Hamburg of the 1890s translated to an alternate continent. Railroads ran inland from the port, thin lines of smoke marking the path of steam locomotives. Military fortifications ringed the city — stone bastions and gun batteries pointing seaward, manned by soldiers in grey uniforms.

"This is not a pre-industrial civilisation," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said. He was standing in the drone control module, watching the feed, his analysts silent beside him. "This is an industrial civilisation at the edge of a technological frontier. They have steel. They have explosives. They have radio. They have warships. They are not harmless."

"Can they see us?" the American analyst asked.

"From that altitude, in daylight, with a composite airframe painted grey — no. They would need radar, optical tracking, or someone looking up at exactly the right moment with a telescope. They have none of those things." He paused. "As long as we stay high and quiet."

The drone crossed the coast on its second day of flight — Day 23 — and found the war.

---

The Mid-East Marleyan War was visible from 150 kilometres away.

The KUS-15's camera captured a burning port on the coast of a southern continent — the inverted equivalent of the terrestrial Middle East, though the political geography bore no resemblance. The port was under attack by a fleet of warships flying the red-cross flag [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]]'s analysts had catalogued the previous day. The defenders' ships burned at anchor. Smoke rose from the city in columns that the drone's sensors could measure — 300 metres, 400 metres, pillars of black carbon that drifted across the ocean on the prevailing wind.

In the harbour, a naval engagement was underway. Two warships — a broadside ironclad and a smaller, faster vessel — were exchanging fire at a range of less than 2,000 metres. The drone's camera captured the muzzle flashes, the smoke, the shell splashes. The ironclad took a hit amidships. Steam vented from its hull. It listed to port and began to settle. The smaller vessel — the attacker — turned away and ran for open water, pursued by the defenders' surviving ships.

"This is an ongoing war," the Chinese analyst said. "This is not a frontier skirmish. This is fleet actions, port bombardments, sustained operations. This is — "

"This is a powder keg," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said. "And we just walked into it."

The [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] quadripartite committee convened at 21:00 on Day 23 for an emergency session. The drone footage was transmitted to all four principals via encrypted video link, the burning port rendered in grainy black-and-white on the screens in Seoul, Beijing, Washington, and Tokyo.

[[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] watched in silence. [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] watched without expression. [[Elizabeth Cross|Cross]] watched with the specific tension of a diplomat who had just been handed a military problem. [[Nakamura Kenji|Nakamura]] watched with the quiet focus of a man who had anticipated this and was now confirmed correct.

"A conventional war," [[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] said. "Between two of the four major powers we have identified. Marley — the flag matches our signals intercept — and the Mid-East Allied Forces, the defenders. The war appears to be active. The Marleyans are on the offensive. The Mid-East is losing."

"This changes our threat assessment," [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] said. "We are not observing a world at peace. We are observing a world in the middle of an active military conflict. The belligerents have warships, artillery, and organised military commands. If either side detects our presence — "

"They will interpret it as a hostile incursion," [[Elizabeth Cross|Cross]] finished. "They will respond accordingly. And we will have no diplomatic channel to de-escalate."

"Do we have any diplomatic channel?" [[Nakamura Kenji|Nakamura]] asked.

"We have no diplomatic channel. We have no contact. We have no understanding of their command structures beyond what the drone can see from 20,000 feet. We are operating blind in a warzone."

The committee discussed the implications for forty-five minutes. The conclusions were unanimous:

First — the covert build-up was now a higher priority than the scientific survey. The FOB and logistics hub must be operational before detection occurred.

Second — drone operations would continue, but at increased altitude (25,000 feet) and with a renewed emphasis on emission control. No active sensors. No radio transmissions on frequencies Marleyan receivers could detect.

Third — the first-contact protocol must be accelerated. [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] needed a plan for what to do when — not if — the AOT powers discovered the Gate.

Fourth — the political geography must be mapped in full within fourteen days.

The committee adjourned. [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]]'s drones continued north.

---

On Day 25, the KUS-15 reached the Walls.

The drone had been flying for fourteen hours on a maximum-endurance sortie, its fuel tanks nearly dry, when the optical turret picked up a geometric anomaly on the northern horizon. Three concentric circles, perfectly regular, rising from a landscape of forest and plain. The outer ring was over fifty metres high. The inner rings were lower but no less massive. Within the rings: cities, farmland, roads, a population of approximately one million people living inside three concentric cages.

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] leaned forward. His analysts, hunched over their screens, went silent.

"What are those?" the American analyst asked.

"Walls," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said. "Somebody built Walls. Fifty metres high. Concentric. Enclosing — " he zoomed the feed, " — an entire civilisation. An entire country. A million people. Behind Walls."

"Why?"

The answer came thirty seconds later, when the drone's camera found the Titans.

They were on the open ground beyond the outermost Wall — the one the drone had catalogued as Wall Maria. Humanoid figures, naked, misshapen, ranging from three metres to fifteen metres in height. They moved with a purposeless, shambling gait, their faces blank, their bodies grotesquely proportioned. The drone captured one — a fifteen-metre giant with a face that was almost human, almost a caricature, frozen in an expression of mindless hunger — stumbling through a field of low grass, its arms swinging, its mouth working silently.

No one in the drone control module spoke for a long time.

"They are alive," the Korean analyst said. "They appear to be — biological. Organic. Moving under their own power. Not machines."

"They are not machines," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said. "But they are not human."

"What are they?"

"I do not know. I do not have a framework for this. I am sending the imagery to the committee."

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] filed the imagery. He did not try to understand it. The imagery reached the committee at 03:00 on Day 26. [[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] was woken to review it. [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] was woken. [[Elizabeth Cross|Cross]], sixteen hours behind in Washington, cleared her afternoon schedule. [[Nakamura Kenji|Nakamura]], who had already seen the preliminary intercept, called an emergency meeting of the JMSDF intelligence directorate.

*"The AOT world contains biological entities of unknown origin and undetermined capability, which the native population has chosen to contain behind walls 50 metres high — a measure that must be interpreted as evidence of existential threat."*

On Day 27, the drone found Liberio.

The internment zone was visible from altitude — a walled sector within a larger industrial city on Marley's southern coast. The buildings within the zone were smaller, the streets narrower, the population density markedly higher than the surrounding urban area. Armed guards manned the gates. The city matched the terrestrial geography of a major European port. The zone matched nothing. It was a prison district in a modern city — modern by AOT standards — and its existence, its deliberate visibility, its integration into the urban fabric, suggested a society that had normalised something monstrous.

[[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] saw the imagery first. His face, on the video feed to [[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]], was unreadable in the way that a diplomat's face is unreadable when the object of the observation is too dangerous to acknowledge.

"They are running a camp," he said.

[[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] said nothing.

"That argument will hold for approximately six more weeks," [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] said. "After which we will have no argument that anyone will accept."

The conversation ended without resolution. The Liberio imagery was filed alongside the Walls and the Titans and the burning port, a catalogue of horrors accumulating in the [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] intelligence archive faster than the committee could process. The drone flew on.

Hizuru was the last major power identified.

The KUS-15 reached the AOT world's eastern island chain on Day 28 — an archipelago that matched the terrestrial geography of Japan, inverted, populated by a civilisation with a distinct architectural tradition. The buildings were wooden, low, temple-like, set among terraced hillsides and small fishing harbours. The ship traffic was lighter than Marley's — smaller vessels, less industrial infrastructure, a society that had achieved sophistication without the heavy footprint of coal and iron. The drone's signals intelligence package detected structured radio traffic — diplomatic frequencies, encrypted but recognisable, the patterns of a nation communicating with its outposts.

Hizuru was the anomaly in the political map. It was the only nation not at war. It was the only nation with a technology base that matched Marley's without matching Marley's industrial footprint. And it was, according to the analysts' preliminary geopolitical assessment, the wildcard — the power that Marley feared and the Mid-East courted and Paradis did not know existed.

The map was complete.

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] stood in the drone control module at 22:00 on Day 28 and looked at the composite image his team had assembled — a high-resolution mosaic of the entire known AOT world, stitched together from seventy-two hours of drone imagery. Marley in the south, industrial and imperial and at war. The Mid-East Allied Forces contesting the southern continent. Paradis in the north, behind Walls that contained a mystery. Hizuru in the east, watching from an island that had chosen distance over entanglement.

"Four powers," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said. "Two at war. One under siege. One waiting. And us — in the middle of their empty ocean, on islands they do not know exist, with a fleet they cannot see and eyes they cannot feel."

### Week 4 — The Fleet — Day 29–35

The first [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] warship crossed the Gate at 05:00 on Day 29.

She was the [[JS Suzutsuki (DD-117)]], an Akizuki-class destroyer of the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force, and she crossed the threshold with her running lights dark and her crew at general quarters — not because she expected combat, but because the act of steaming a 6,800-ton warship through a hole in the universe was the kind of event that demanded formality. The destroyer's commanding officer, a captain named [[Saito Hiroshi]], had been briefed on the AOT world seventy-two hours earlier. He had absorbed the information in silence, asked three questions about navigational hazards, and boarded his ship without further comment. He was a professional, and professionals did not dwell on the impossible.

The [[JS Suzutsuki (DD-117)|Suzutsuki]] was followed over the next six days by a [[ROKS Chungmugong Yi Sun-sin (DDH-975)]], a Chinese [[CNS Xiamen (154)|Type 052D destroyer]], and an American [[USS Ralph Johnson (DDG-114)|Arleigh Burke Flight IIA]] — one warship from each [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] nation, deployed to the AOT side under the classified annex of the Four-Power Gate Protocol. They formed a standing naval force designated Task Group [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]]-Alpha, anchored at the [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] harbour, their crews rotating through patrol patterns that kept at least one vessel on station at the Gate threshold at all times.

By Day 30, [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] was no longer a construction site. It was a base.

The pontoon causeway had been replaced by a semi-permanent modular pier — steel and concrete sections craned into position by a floating derrick, capable of berthing two destroyers simultaneously. Fuel farms spread across the volcanic plateau behind the harbour — 500,000 litres of diesel and aviation fuel in bladder tanks. The command complex occupied a graded terrace above the pier: a grid of shipping containers and prefab shelters, connected by covered walkways, housing the operations centre, intelligence fusion cell, communications suite, and quarters for 200 personnel.

The [[Alfred Faure Forward Operating Base|Alfred Faure]] FOB had grown to 500 personnel in thirty days. The expeditionary runway was operational for C-130J Hercules cargo aircraft — turboprops that transited the Gate at 500 feet and touched down on the volcanic scree with a roar that sent the seabird colonies wheeling into the grey sky. The island now had a field hospital, a vehicle maintenance depot, a helicopter pad with two MH-60S Seahawks, and a signals station that linked the FOB to [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] via undersea fibre and to Earth via satellite relay. It was, [[Marcus Rivera|Rivera]] noted in his weekly construction report, the fastest expeditionary base build in US Navy history — and the classification of the location meant it would never be acknowledged publicly.

The hydrophone array continued to listen. The drones continued to fly. The map continued to sharpen.

And on Day 33, the hydrophone registered a contact that was not routine.

---

The engine signature was a single reciprocating steam engine running at an irregular cadence — a vessel operating beyond its designed endurance, burning bad coal, labouring against a sea it was not designed to handle. Bearing 0-1-5. Range 140 kilometres. Speed six knots. Course: south. Toward the Gate. Toward the islands.

The contact was detected at 03:12 local — the deep-watch hour, when the hydrophone analysts were down to a skeleton crew and the [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] patrol schedule had no vessels in the contact's immediate area. The duty officer, a Korean petty officer named Hwang, noted the contact in the log and ran a preliminary threat assessment. The engine signature was civilian. The speed was slow — too slow for a warship, too slow for any vessel on a scheduled transit. The course was anomalous: no known AOT-native shipping route passed within 500 kilometres of the Gate.

Hwang woke [[Marcus Rivera|Rivera]] at 03:20.

[[Marcus Rivera|Rivera]] reviewed the data. Asked two questions. Ordered the [[PKG 721]] — a Korean Gumdoksuri-class patrol boat on station at the [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] anchorage — to make best speed toward the contact. And then he called [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]].

"We have an inbound vessel," he said. "140 kilometres north. Course directly toward the [[Alfred Faure Forward Operating Base|Alfred Faure]] Islands. Civilian hull. Steam engine. No radio traffic detected. No escort."

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] was in the drone control module within twelve minutes. He launched a KUS-15 on an emergency sortie — no pre-flight checklist, minimal warm-up, the ground crew running through the launch sequence at a speed that violated every safety protocol and every regulation. The drone crossed the Gate at 03:38 and climbed to its loiter altitude, its camera scanning the dark ocean ahead.

At 04:06, the KUS-15 acquired the target.

The vessel was a steamship, roughly forty metres in length, with a hull that had been reinforced for ice — an icebreaker bow, heavier framing, the kind of design that Marleyan shipyards produced for polar expeditions. Marleyan script on the stern, below a name: ***PELIKAN***. A civilian registry number on the hull below the name, partially obscured by rust. Three lifeboat davits, one empty. Smoke from a single funnel, black and sooty, the sign of a boiler being fed low-grade coal. The ship was rolling heavily in the swell, labouring, its engine cadence faltering every few minutes as the stokers struggled to maintain steam.

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] watched the feed for thirty seconds. Then he called [[Marcus Rivera|Rivera]].

"It's a polar expedition ship," he said. "Marleyan. Civilian. Probably lost. Probably off course by a margin its navigator cannot calculate."

"How many souls?"

"Hard to say from this altitude. Crew of thirty, maybe thirty-five. The empty lifeboat is concerning. They may have lost people."

"Armed?"

"Unknown. If this is a civilian scientific expedition, they will have small arms at most. If they have a naval observer — " [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] paused. "Marley attaches naval observers to polar expeditions. The expeditions are cover for territorial mapping. If they have an observer, he will be armed and trained."

[[Marcus Rivera|Rivera]] absorbed this. "They're heading directly toward the islands. They will have visual on the FOB within four hours if they maintain course."

"They're not maintaining course. They're drifting. Their navigator is lost. But yes — they will eventually encounter the islands."

[[Marcus Rivera|Rivera]] looked at the hydrophone feed. The contact was 132 kilometres out and closing. The [[PKG 721]] was at 90 kilometres and closing. The intercept window would open at approximately 06:30.

He opened a channel to the [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] committee.

"Requesting authorisation for first-contact intercept," he said. "One civilian vessel, Marleyan. Polar expedition. Drifting toward [[Alfred Faure Forward Operating Base|Alfred Faure]]. I have a patrol boat en route. ETA intercept 06:30."

The committee convened within seven minutes. The decision was unanimous: intercept, isolate, inform, integrate. The [[First Contact Protocol]], drafted in abstraction on Day 19, was now operational. The theoretical had become actual. The ship was coming.

### Week 5 — The Capture — Day 36–43

The [[PKG 721]] intercepted the *[[Pelikan|Pelikan]]* at 06:48 on Day 36.

The Korean patrol boat approached from the southeast, its hull low against the grey morning water, its engines throttled back to minimise acoustic signature. The *[[Pelikan|Pelikan]]* was visible on the horizon at a range of 5,000 metres — a dark shape against a darker sea, its funnel smoke trailing flat across the water in the cold austral dawn.

At 2,000 metres, the [[PKG 721]]'s optical sensors acquired the vessel in full detail. Thirty-one souls, the thermal camera counted — clustered on the bow and the bridge wing, their body heat glowing white against the cold steel of the hull. Some were pointing. Some were shouting. One — a tall figure in a civilian greatcoat — was standing very still, watching the approaching vessel with the focus of a man who had just seen something that did not belong in any book he had ever read.

[[Elias Vogler]] was that man.

He was thirty-nine years old, a physical oceanographer who had published seventeen papers on the circulation of the world's oceans, and he was standing at the bow of a ship that had been lost for eleven days. The stars above him were wrong. They had been wrong for two days — the constellations slowly peeling away from every celestial chart he had ever memorised, sliding into arrangements that belonged to no ocean he could name. The water beneath him was wrong — colder than it should be, a different colour, a different salinity. And now, approaching from the southeast, was a vessel that was wrong in a way that transcended all the previous wrongnesses.

The vessel was grey. It was sleek. It was moving without sails and without funnel smoke — no mast, no stack, no visible means of propulsion — and it was moving at a speed that should have been impossible for its size. Its bow was a smooth wedge of grey metal, utterly unlike the clipper bows and ram bows of every warship Vogler had ever studied. The object on its foredeck was clearly a weapon — a cannon, or something cannon-adjacent — but it was housed in a low turret of unfamiliar design, and it was not aimed at them.

"Dr. [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]]."

The voice was Dreher's. Oberleutnant [[Klaus Dreher]] emerged from the wheelhouse, his Marleyan Navy greatcoat buttoned to the throat against the cold. He was thirty-four, Titan Security Division, assigned to the expedition to ensure it did not discover anything politically inconvenient, and he was staring at the approaching vessel with the cold, methodical attention of a man who had just identified a threat he had no capacity to counter.

"That ship," Dreher said. "Is not one of ours."

"I am aware," Vogler said.

"It is not Mid-East. It is not Hizuran. It is not — " Dreher stopped. His jaw tightened. "It is not made by any nation I know."

Vogler turned. "What are you saying?"

"I am saying we have been off course for eleven days. The stars changed nine days ago — they do not match any chart in the wheelhouse. The water temperature dropped to levels that should not exist at any latitude we could have reached. And now a warship of a design no shipyard on this planet has ever produced is approaching us at twice its hull speed. I am not a man who believes in the supernatural. But I believe in evidence. The evidence is that we are in waters no Marleyan has ever charted — and someone else got here first."

The [[PKG 721]] closed to 1,000 metres and held station. A voice came over the water — amplified, distinct, speaking in a language Vogler recognised as foreign but could not place. Then a second voice, this one in Marleyan, the grammar slightly stilted, the accent perfect:

*"UNIDENTIFIED VESSEL. YOU HAVE ENTERED RESTRICTED WATERS. YOU ARE NOT IN DANGER. YOU WILL NOT BE HARMED. IDENTIFY YOURSELVES."*

Vogler looked at Dreher. Dreher looked at the patrol boat.

"We are the Marleyan polar expedition vessel *[[Pelikan|Pelikan]]*," Vogler called back, cupping his hands against the wind. "We are a civilian scientific expedition. We have lost our bearing and our navigation. We request assistance."

A pause. Then:

*"PELIKAN. YOUR REQUEST IS ACKNOWLEDGED. YOU WILL BE BOARDED. THE BOARDING PARTY IS ARMED. DO NOT RESIST. DO NOT ATTEMPT TO SIGNAL. DO NOT ATTEMPT TO FLEE. YOU WILL NOT BE HARMED."*

A small boat detached from the patrol vessel — a rigid-hulled inflatable, moving fast across the grey water, eight armed sailors aboard. It came alongside the *[[Pelikan|Pelikan]]*'s boarding ladder in a spray of salt and cold. The sailors climbed aboard. They moved with a practiced efficiency that Vogler recognised — these were professionals, soldiers, men who had done this before and knew exactly what they were doing.

The officer in command was a Korean Navy lieutenant named Cho. He carried a tablet. He showed Vogler a text in Marleyan:

*"YOU HAVE ENTERED THE TERRITORIAL PERIMETER OF A MULTINATIONAL COALITION. YOUR VESSEL WILL BE ESCORTED TO A SECURE ANCHORAGE. YOU ARE NOT PRISONERS. YOU ARE DETAINEES PENDING STATUS DETERMINATION. COOPERATE AND YOU WILL BE TREATED FAIRLY. RESIST AND YOU WILL BE RESTRAINED."*

Vogler read the text twice. The Marleyan was correct. The tone was... not hostile. Clinical. The tone of an organisation that had planned for this eventuality and was executing a protocol.

He looked at the patrol boat. At its crew. At the alien flag — four nations' colours on a single field, an arrangement of symbols that meant nothing to him.

He looked at Dreher.

"We cooperate," Vogler said.

Dreher's jaw was tight. "We do not know who they are."

"We know they are more powerful than us. We know they speak Marleyan. We know they have not harmed us. We cooperate."

Dreher said nothing. But he did not resist. He submitted — the pragmatic submission of a soldier who understood that the tactical situation was unwinnable. He would watch. He would learn. He would wait. And when the opportunity came — if it came — he would act.

The *[[Pelikan|Pelikan]]* was escorted to [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] at four knots, the [[PKG 721]] riding its port quarter, its crew watching the alien coastline resolve out of the morning mist — volcanic ridges, grey-green tussock grass, the geometric shapes of a military base that had been built on an island that no map recorded.

Vogler stood at the bow and watched the harbour open before him. Modular piers. Warships from four different navies, their designs utterly foreign, their flags unfamiliar. Fuel tanks. Warehouse buildings. A command complex spreading across the tussock grass in a grid of grey rectangles. And beyond it all, in the anchorage, a fleet.

"Thirty-one souls," he said to no one. "Who have seen the end of the world."

[[Elias Vogler]] was interviewed first.

The room was a converted shipping container in the [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] command complex — soundproofed, climate-controlled, furnished with a conference table and four chairs that had been carried through the Gate at considerable expense. Lieutenant Colonel [[Tanaka Kenji]] sat across from him. Between them, on the table, a tablet displaying images Vogler had not yet been shown: the Gate, the Silver Line, the research motherships on the Yellow Sea, the drones that had mapped his entire world without anyone knowing.

"This is going to be difficult to believe," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said. His Marleyan was imperfect but functional — eight days of linguistic work by [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] analysts, cross-referenced with radio intercepts and the ship's own logbooks.

"At this point," Vogler said, "I am willing to believe a great deal."

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] told him everything. The other Earth. The four-power coalition. The Gate that had opened in their ocean — a permanent seam between two worlds. The five weeks of survey. The drones. The hydrophones. The fleet. The fact that [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] had been here, on his world, for thirty-six days, and no one — not Marley, not the Mid-East, not Paradis, not Hizuru — had known they existed.

Vogler listened without interrupting. His hands, resting on the table, were steady. His eyes, when [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] finished, were wet.

"You have been here for five weeks," he said. "You have mapped my entire world. You have seen our wars. You have seen our — " he stopped. "You have seen the Walls."

"Yes."

"And the Titans."

"Yes."

"And Liberio."

"Yes."

"I want you to help us understand your world. Your politics. Your technology. Your geography. You are a scientist. "I have been at sea for forty-seven days," he said. "I have seen the stars change and the water change and the world change. And now a man from another planet is telling me that my Eldian crew — whom I have been defending in small, quiet ways for seven years — will be free. I do not know whether I am dreaming or whether my entire career has been a dream from which I am only now waking."

"You are not dreaming," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said.

"Then show me your world," Vogler said. "And I will show you mine."

[[Frieda]] was interviewed last.

She had been brought to the [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] intelligence section aboard the [[R-V Isabu]] — the research mothership, chosen because it looked less military than the destroyers at the [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] anchorage. The officer who spoke to her was an American Navy ensign named [[DeSilva]], assigned to the interview because she was the youngest and least threatening officer on the [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] personnel roster, and also because someone had decided that a woman in uniform might seem less like a Marleyan overseer. [[DeSilva|DeSilva]] spoke through a translator — a Japanese linguist named Saito who had spent seven days constructing a functional Marleyan vocabulary from radio intercepts and the ship's logbooks.

*"You are no longer subject to the laws of the Marleyan Empire. You are now under the protection of the Combined Anomaly Task Force. You are not prisoners. You are not property. You are people. You will be treated as such. You are free."*

[[Frieda]] read the words on the translator's tablet. Read them again. Read them a third time.

She had been told she was a devil for as long as she could remember. She had been told she was cursed, contaminated, the heir of an ancient evil that her ancestors had inflicted on the world. She had been told that her only value was her labour, and that her labour was a privilege, and that the privilege could be revoked at any time by any Marleyan who wished to revoke it. She had never been told she was free.

[[Frieda]] looked around the room — the white walls, the fluorescent lights, the tablet on the table, the uniformed woman who was looking at her with an expression that was not pity and not curiosity but something else, something [[Frieda]] could not name and was not sure she trusted.

"I am an engineer," she said. "Show me your engines."

| [[DeSilva|DeSilva]] blinked. "I'm sorry?"

"You have ships that move without sails and without smoke. You have lights that glow without flame. You have a base on an island that did not exist on any map. You arrived here from another world. I want to understand how. Show me your engines."

[[DeSilva|DeSilva]] looked at the translator. The translator looked back. They had not anticipated this — the interview subject taking control of the interview, the tool asserting agency. But then, they had never met an Eldian who had survived Liberio. Survival in Liberio required a kind of strength that no military protocol could anticipate.

"I'll see what I can arrange," [[DeSilva|DeSilva]] said.

[[Frieda]] nodded. She did not smile. But something in her posture shifted — a tension releasing, a wall lowering, though only slightly.

She was not free. She did not believe in freedom. But she was being offered something that looked like it, and she had learned, in Liberio, to take what was offered and ask questions later.

The [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] quadripartite committee convened for the final time in this chapter's span at 19:00 on Day 38. The agenda was the integration of thirty-one Marleyan nationals into [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] operations.

[[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] chaired from Seoul. [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] from Beijing. [[Elizabeth Cross|Cross]] from Washington. [[Nakamura Kenji|Nakamura]] from Tokyo. On the screen before each of them: the profiles of the crew of the *[[Pelikan|Pelikan]]* — twenty-two Marleyan sailors, eight Eldian engineers, one Marleyan naval observer. All now housed at [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]]. All now aware of the Gate.

"Vogler has accepted employment," [[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] said. "He is a physical oceanographer. He knows the political geography of his world intimately. He has agreed to work with Dr. [[Ahn Jae-hyun|Ahn]] and Dr. [[Wang Lijuan|Wang]] on the survey. He is, by all indications, genuine in his cooperation."

"The Eldian engineers have accepted sanctuary and employment," [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] said. "All eight of them. They are now, technically, [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] civilian employees. The lead engineer, [[Frieda]], has requested — " he glanced at his notes, " — a tour of our engine rooms. I recommend we grant the request. Her technical aptitude appears to be considerable."

"And Dreher?" [[Elizabeth Cross|Cross]] asked.

"Dreher has submitted. He has not cooperated. He is watching. He is learning. He is waiting. He will attempt to escape or to signal his government at the first opportunity. We have isolated him accordingly." [[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] paused. "He is not a threat now. He may become one later."

"This changes our strategic position," [[Nakamura Kenji|Nakamura]] said. "We are no longer an unobserved presence. We have made first contact. We have native informants. We have a naval observer from a hostile power who will, eventually, find a way to communicate what he has seen. The window of secrecy is closing."

"The window was always closing," [[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] said. "The question is what we do with the time we have."

"We accelerate," [[Elizabeth Cross|Cross]] said. "We finish the build-up. We deepen our intelligence. We establish a diplomatic posture before the contact is made for us. The *[[Pelikan|Pelikan]]* was an accident — a polar expedition that drifted off course in an ocean where there should be nothing to drift into. But it is also a warning. If one ship can find us by accident, others will find us by design. Marley is a naval power. It will eventually notice that ships are going missing in its southern ocean — or that a base has appeared on islands that were supposed to be empty."

"When it does," [[Zheng Weimin|Zheng]] said, "we must be ready. The first-contact protocol was designed for this eventuality. We are executing it. The question is whether the next contact is voluntary — initiated by us, on our terms — or involuntary, forced on us by a Marleyan cruiser that stumbles into the perimeter and opens fire before we can explain who we are."

"Then we accelerate," [[Lee Jun-ho|Lee]] said. "And we prepare for both."

The committee adjourned. The profiles of the thirty-one Marleyans remained on the screen — their names still foreign, their faces still impossible, their presence still a problem no one had anticipated in the abstract language of the [[First Contact Protocol]].

The undetected era was over.

### Scene: The Silver Line — Day 43, 06:00 KST

Six weeks.

The sun rose over the Yellow Sea at 05:52, the same sun that had risen on the first morning, the same pale light catching the same silver seam on the same blue water. The Gate had not changed. It did not pulse. It did not hum. It simply was — a fact of geography now, as permanent as the sea itself, as permanent as the world on the far side that had just become larger.

On the AOT side, the sun rose over [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]] at the same moment — a cold grey dawn, the austral winter sky thin and pale. The harbour was full. Four destroyers at anchor. Patrol boats on station. The hydrophone array listening. The drones circling. The FOB on [[Alfred Faure Forward Operating Base|Alfred Faure]] glowing with electric light, its runway cleared, its fuel tanks full, its 500 personnel beginning the morning shift. The base was no longer a construction project. It was a permanent installation. It was, for all practical purposes, a nation.

[[Elias Vogler]] stood at the window of his quarters in the science module — a prefab shelter with a view of the harbour — watching the warships catch the dawn. He had been a [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|CATF]] employee for seven days. He had spoken to [[Ahn Jae-hyun|Ahn]] and [[Wang Lijuan|Wang]]. He had seen the data from the KUS-15 drones — the map of his own world, rendered with a precision that Marleyan cartographers would have killed for. He had seen the footage of Liberio from above, the armbands visible on the figures in the narrow streets, and he had felt something he could not name — shame, perhaps, or grief, or the specific regret of a man who had spent his life serving a nation he was only now learning to see clearly.

He was not a traitor. He was a scientist. And scientists, he had decided, told the truth. Even when the truth was uncomfortable. Even when the truth was devastating. Especially then.

[[Klaus Dreher]] stood at the edge of his designated area on the eastern ridge above the command complex. He was not guarded. He was not restrained. But he knew he was watched. He could feel the drones overhead, the sensors embedded in the perimeter fence, the silent attention of a power that had mapped his entire world before anyone in it had known they were being observed.

He looked north, toward Marley. Eight thousand kilometres of ocean and continent. He could not see it. He could feel it. He had been here for seven days. He had learned the patrol schedule. He had counted the ships. He had mapped the base. He had not found a way to transmit. He had not given up. He was, after all, a soldier. And soldiers waited.

[[Frieda]] was in the engine room of the Japanese destroyer [[JS Suzutsuki (DD-117)|Suzutsuki]], standing beside a JMSDF chief petty officer named Sato, staring at the gas turbine with an expression that was not wonder but hunger — the hunger of an engineer who had just seen something she needed to understand. Sato was pointing at the turbine's compressed air intake, trying to explain the Brayton cycle using hand gestures and a diagram on a clipboard. [[Frieda]] was nodding. She was not understanding everything. She was understanding enough.

She was no longer a tool. She was an employee. She was paid. She was housed. She was not called a devil. She did not yet believe this would last. But she was willing to act as though it might.

---

On the Yellow Sea, two hundred kilometres southwest of Incheon, [[Park Do-hyun]] stood on the bridge wing of [[KCG 506]] with coffee that was hot. It was his first hot coffee in three days — the galley's water heater had failed, and the replacement part had only arrived on the morning resupply vessel — and he drank it with the specific gratitude of a man who had learned that small victories were the only kind available.

[[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]]'s voice came over Channel 16, as it did every morning, in the strange ritual of two men who had learned to trust each other because there was no one else who understood.

"KCG 506. Haijing 2306. Good morning, Lieutenant [[Park Do-hyun|Park]]."

"Good morning, Captain [[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]]."

*"I have heard about the ship. The Marleyan vessel. They say the scientists have been employed."*

"They have. Dr. [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] is working with KIOST and SOA. The Eldian engineers are working at [[Grande Terre Logistics Hub|Grande Terre]]. The naval observer is being held."

*"It is a strange thing,"* [[Chen Shaoqiang|Chen]] said, *"to capture a ship from a nation you did not know existed six weeks ago."*

"It is a strange thing," Park said, "to be alive right now."

A pause. Then: *"You think this will end in war."*

"I think this will end in something," Park said. "I do not know what. But something."

The Silver Line caught the morning sun — the silver seam on blue water, the doorway between two worlds. Park watched it for a moment, then turned back to his bridge.

The coffee was hot. The Gate was still. The world on the far side had just become populated with people who knew they were not alone.

The quiet war, Park suspected, was about to become louder.
