# Hugh Renwick

## Description

Grey man — grey suit, grey hair, grey voice. Director General of MI5, representing the Joint Intelligence Committee at the COBR meeting. His words cut; his face betrays nothing. Carries a slim black folder and opens it only when necessary. The sort of intelligence officer who has spent his career in the space between what is known and what can be said.

## Background

Career intelligence officer. Rose through MI5 over three decades, serving in counter-terrorism, counter-espionage, and — in his later years — the grey zone where domestic security meets geopolitical instability. Appointed DG of MI5 two years before the National Unity Government. His primary concern at the COBR meeting is the domestic dimension: what happens when Faithful theology enters a spiritually unvaccinated British public.

## Stance on the Anomaly

Renwick is not concerned with theology per se. He is concerned with what theology does to a population that has no frame of reference for it. The Faithful delegation's reports — the ark, the blank souls, the visible God and the tangible Adversary — are, in his view, a form of information contagion. If they spread into the British public without preparation, they could destabilise the social order faster than any conventional threat. He advocates for tight information control and a gradual, managed release of the facts, stripped of theological framing.

## Relationships

- [[Stephen Davenport]] — Briefs the PM directly. Trusts Davenport's pragmatism but suspects the Prime Minister underestimates the explosive potential of Faithful theology in a secular society.
- [[Peter Chisholm]] — Wary respect. Renwick sees Chisholm as an asset for managing the religious dimension of the crisis, but also as a potential vector for the very theological contagion he is trying to contain.
