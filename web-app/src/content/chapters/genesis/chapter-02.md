# Chapter 02 – The Inquisitor's Briefing

## Summary

[[Alistair Pembroke|Captain Pembroke]] writes his despatch. The report travels via signal relay and wired telegraph to the [[Fortress of the White Cliffs]], where [[Caius Blackwood|Inquisitor-Admiral Blackwood]], Grand Inquisitor-Admiral of the Faithful Fleet, convenes his war council. What begins as a military report becomes a theological crisis. [[Father Beauchamp]]'s findings — that the world beyond [[The Anomaly]] possesses neither the touch of Heaven nor the stain of Hell — unsettles the Faithful command more than any enemy fleet could. By order of the Crown, the [[Office of the Propagation of Virtue]] and the [[Holy Science Academy]] are mobilised. The investigation begins.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: *[[HMS Resolute]]* — The Captain's Despatch

**[TIME]: Day 1 — 1914-09-14 TC, 15:40 hours / 2026-06-18, 21:40 Zulu**
**[POV]: Captain [[Alistair Pembroke]]*

The priest's launch had returned. [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] was aboard, his [[Orichalcum]] crucifix still in hand, his face unreadable. The oarsmen pulled with the silent exhaustion of men who had seen something they could not yet name. [[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] watched them hoist the launch, the falls creaking, the salt wind cutting across the quarterdeck. The [[The Anomaly|portal]] still shimmered behind them — a mile-wide scar that had not dimmed, had not flickered, had done nothing but exist with the insolence of a fact.

He turned and walked to his sea cabin. The yeoman had already laid out his writing case: ink, steel nib, the heavy cream paper watermarked with the Crown and Cross of England. The chaplain had sanctified the ink this morning, as he did every morning. [[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] sat, dipped the nib, and began.

*From: Captain Alistair Pembroke, HMS Resolute, Flagship Atlantic Picket Line*
*To: The Lords of the Admiralty, by way of Inquisitor-Admiral Caius Blackwood, Fortress of the White Cliffs, Dover*
*Classification: MOST URGENT — BY HAND OF SIGNALS OFFICER ONLY*

*My Lord Inquisitor-Admiral,*

*At approximately 06:20 hours this day, the Feast of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross, this vessel and her escorts — HMS Lancelot and HMS Halberd — encountered an apparition of unprecedented nature at position 49°51′N, 8°24′W, on the Atlantic approaches. The apparition takes the form of a vertical curtain of light, one mile in breadth and two hundred feet in height, bisecting the sea. It is not an infernal glamour. It is not a weapon of the Adversary. It is, by all discernment I can apply, a doorway to another world.*

He paused. The nib hovered. How did one report a thing that would sound like madness if it were not witnessed by three hundred and seventeen souls?

*On the far side of this curtain, we have observed and communicated with vessels of a British navy that does not serve our King. Their ships are of a design I cannot classify — devoid of masts, devoid of holy protection, propelled without coal or sail. They fly a Union flag and their officers speak English of a dialect I find intelligible but stripped of liturgical form. They claim to serve a King Charles of the House of Windsor. They do not know the Adversary. They do not know the war.*

*At 14:00 hours, Chaplain-Lieutenant Father Beauchamp of the Sacred Order of the Dragon crossed to the threshold under flag of parley. He met an officer of the other navy — a woman bearing the rank of Lieutenant Commander — and conducted the Rite of Discernment at the boundary. His preliminary finding: the world beyond is not infernal. It is not sanctified. It is* absent. *Absent of the Adversary, absent of the divine presence we know. A world untouched by 1099.*

*I await your orders. I have ordered the escorts to maintain station. No shots have been fired. No holy relics have been expended. The portal remains stable. The strangers remain cautious. For now.*

*I remain your obedient servant,*
*Alistair Pembroke, Capt. RN*

He sanded the ink, folded the despatch, and sealed it with the wax of the Faithful Crown — a cross over an anchor, pressed hard. Then he climbed to the bridge and handed it to the signals officer.

"Relay to Land's End Station by lamp. Then by telegraph to Dover. No cipher — this one goes in the clear. Let the Heretics listen. They won't believe it either."

### Scene 2: [[Fortress of the White Cliffs]] — The Inquisitor-Admiral's Tower

**[TIME]: Day 1 — 1914-09-14 TC, 21:15 hours**
**[POV]: Inquisitor-Admiral [[Caius Blackwood]]*

The chamber was built into the chalk itself — a commandery carved from the white cliffs of Dover, its windows facing south toward the distant, Heretic-held rock of Gibraltar. [[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] stood at the great map table, his hands clasped behind his back, the black wool of his admiral's coat heavy with silver embroidery: anchor, cross, dragon coiled around a sword. The uniform of an Inquisitor-Admiral was not merely naval. It was ecclesiastical. The collar was a priest's collar. The rings on his fingers were seals of office and of ordination. The man was both shepherd and war commander, and the two roles had never felt more inseparable than tonight.

The telegraph chattered in the signals room below, a frantic cadence of clicks that had not stopped for six hours. Despatches were stacking on the oak sideboard: from the Land's End signal station, from the picket line, from the Admiralty in London, from the Palace itself. The King had been woken from his evening prayers. The Crown could not afford to be surprised.

[[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] read [[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]]'s despatch a second time, then a third. He was a tall man, sixty-three years old, with the gaunt face of a monk and the eyes of a man who had ordered ships to their deaths and blessed the souls he sent. He had commanded the Faithful Fleet at the Battle of Cordoba in 1910 — a bloody stalemate that cost him three dreadnoughts and half his destroyer screen. He had seen the [[Heretic Legion|Heretic]] submarines surface with hulls of fused bone and metal, had watched demon-engineered torpedoes track his ships by the heat of their prayers. He had thought himself past the capacity for astonishment.

He was wrong.

"Summon them," he said.

The aide — a young lieutenant of the [[Office of the Propagation of Virtue]], a man who wore a scholar's robe over a naval tunic — bowed. "All of them, my Lord Inquisitor-Admiral?"

"All of them. The Holy Science Academy. The engineering corps. The Order's diviners. The Propagation Office's doctrinal analysts. Every mind that can read a gauge or cast a Rite. I want them in this chamber within the hour."

The lieutenant left at a near-run. [[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] turned back to the map. The Celtic Sea. A mile-wide portal. A world without Hell.

He had spent forty years fighting a war that was theology made manifest. The Adversary was not a metaphor — he had seen the Tyrants with his own eyes, felt the pressure of their presence on his mind, watched a [[Grail-spore]] bloom inside a rating's chest and turn him to something that bubbled and screamed for six hours before the chaplain could grant him mercy. He knew the smell of sanctified promethium and the sound of a church-blessed twelve-inch shell tearing through a Heretic cruiser's infernal armour. He knew that the war was eternal and righteous and that the Faithful would win not because they were stronger but because God was on their side — or so he had believed, every day of his life, without question.

Now a hole had opened in the sea and on the other side was a world where God had apparently done nothing at all, and the Devil had never arrived, and the British Empire had somehow existed for eight hundred years without either of them.

What did that make the war? What did that make his faith?

He did not ask that question aloud. He was an Inquisitor-Admiral. His duty was not to ask. His duty was to investigate, and if necessary, to destroy.

But the question sat in the room with him, silent and patient, waiting for its hour.

### Scene 3: [[Fortress of the White Cliffs]] — The War Council

**[TIME]: Day 1 — 1914-09-14 TC, 22:45 hours**
**[POV]: Inquisitor-Admiral [[Caius Blackwood]]*

They filed into the chamber like the disparate limbs of a body that had never fully learned to coordinate. First came the Holy Science Academy delegation — three men in the grey cassocks of the techno-clergy, their hands stained with orichalcum filings and chemical reagents. Their chief, **Doctor-Abbot [[Edmund Lassiter]]*, was a hunched man of seventy who had spent his career reverse-engineering captured Heretic machinery to find the devil in the gears. He carried a leather satchel of instruments: aetheric resonance gauges, sanctified galvanometers, a portable spectroscope blessed by the Patriarch of New Antioch.

Behind them came the [[Sacred Order of the Dragon|Order]]'s diviners — two chaplains in the white-and-crimson of the Order, their faces prematurely aged by the cost of casting true discernment. One of them, [[Morgan Pell|Chaplain-Lieutenant Pell]], was assigned to the Fortress directly; the other had been summoned from the [[Moving Fortress of Britannia]] by priority despatch. They carried no instruments. They carried only their crucifixes, their reliquaries, and the weight of having looked into the immaterial and been looked back at.

The engineer corps colonel, a bull-necked man named **Trenchard**, came last, still wiping grease from his hands. He had been overseeing the installation of a new orichalcum-alloy turret on the fortress's seaward batteries. He looked annoyed at being pulled away from actual work.

[[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] did not sit. He never sat during councils. He stood at the head of the table, his hands flat on the wood, the map of the Celtic Sea spread before him with the anomaly marked in red ink — a single line that crossed no latitude, no meridian, no boundary known to cartography.

"You have all read [[Alistair Pembroke|Captain Pembroke]]'s despatch," he began. "I will not summarise it. I will tell you what it means. It means that for the first time since 1099, we are not alone in creation. It means there is another England, another Crown, another humanity — one that has never known the war, never known the Adversary, never known the Rites that keep us breathing. It means everything we understand about the divine order of the cosmos is, at minimum, incomplete."

He let the silence hold. No one contradicted him.

"We have three tasks," he continued. "First: determine whether this portal is a threat. Doctor-Abbot Lassiter, you will assemble a research contingent and proceed to the threshold aboard the fast cruiser HMS *Herald*. You will take every instrument of measurement the Academy possesses. I want to know what that curtain is made of, whether it is stable, whether it can be closed, and whether anything can pass through it in either direction without being… changed."

[[Edmund Lassiter|Lassiter]] inclined his head. "The Academy has a mobile laboratory prepared for the Cordoba front. It can be rerouted within the hour."

"Second," [[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] said, turning to the Order's chaplains. "You will determine whether this absence — this world without God or Devil — is a theological danger. If their souls are neither saved nor damned, what are they? Are they lost sheep, or are they something we have no doctrine to classify? I want a full theological assessment, ratified by the Propagation Office, before any formal diplomatic contact proceeds beyond the parley."

[[Morgan Pell|Pell]] spoke for the first time, his voice thin but steady. "My Lord Inquisitor-Admiral, the implications for doctrine are… profound. If these people have lived eight hundred years without the Fall of Jerusalem, without the Rites, without the Church Militant — and they are not damned — then the nature of salvation itself may be broader than the Synods have taught."

"Then the Synods will learn," [[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] said flatly. "Third: Colonel Trenchard, you will prepare the fortress for the possibility that this is the most elaborate Heretic deception in eight centuries. I do not believe it is. But I have been wrong before, and the dead of Cordoba do not forgive my errors. The seaward batteries will be brought to full readiness. The [[Orichalcum Cuirass|orichalcum cuirasses]] will be distributed to the garrison. If something comes through that portal that is not human, I want it to meet the full weight of the Faithful Crown before it reaches the white cliffs of England."

Trenchard nodded curtly. "The new batteries can range to the horizon. If it's a demon, it won't like what it meets."

[[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] straightened. The weight of his coat, of his office, of forty years of holy war, settled across his shoulders like an old friend. "Then go. All of you. In the name of King Robert the Longsword, by the authority vested in me as Inquisitor-Admiral of the Faithful Fleet, and before the eyes of Almighty God — find out what has opened in our sea. And pray that it is not the beginning of the end."

They dispersed. The chamber emptied. [[Caius Blackwood|Blackwood]] remained at the map table, staring at the red mark on the Celtic Sea.

In the morning, he would send his own despatch to the Palace. He would tell the King that the world had just become larger than anyone had ever imagined, and that the Crown of England might need to be more than a shield against Hell. It might need to be a bridge.

He was not certain the Crown was ready for that. He was not certain he was ready for that.

But duty did not wait for certainty.

### Scene 4: *[[HMS Resolute]]* — Beauchamp's Testimony

**[TIME]: Day 2 — 1914-09-15 TC, 03:10 hours / 2026-06-19, 09:10 Zulu**
**[POV]: [[Father Beauchamp]]*

The middle watch was the hour of ghosts. The ship creaked in the darkness, her coal-fired heart beating slow beneath the waterline. On the bridge, the officer of the watch stared at the shimmering wall of [[The Anomaly]] with eyes that had grown accustomed to the impossible. Below, in the wardroom, [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] sat across from [[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]], a cup of cold tea between them, a single oil lamp swinging gently with the sea.

"You haven't slept," [[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] said.

"Neither have you."

[[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] had removed his armoured vestments. Without them, he was a smaller man — a priest in a stained cassock, his hands still bearing the faint silver residue of the chrism he had cast at the threshold. He looked older tonight. The Rite of Discernment always aged the caster.

"I need to tell you what I felt," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] said. "Not what I told the yeoman to write. What I *felt*."

[[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] waited.

"The Rite of Discernment is not subtle," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] said. "It searches for traces. Hell leaves a stain — brimstone, a pressure in the mind, a cold that has nothing to do with temperature. Heaven leaves a warmth, a sense of order, of divine presence. Every soul in our world carries one or the other. Even the most depraved Heretic still bears the mark of the Adversary's touch. Even the most doubting Faithful still carries the faint echo of the Creator. I have cast this Rite a hundred times. I know what the two signatures feel like."

"And the other side?"

"Nothing." [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] looked at his hands. "The woman I met — [[Rebecca Osei|Lieutenant Commander Osei]]. She was human. Utterly human. No infernal taint, no divine blessing. Her soul is… clean, [[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]]. It is a blank page. As if the war between Heaven and Hell simply never touched her world at all."

He paused. The lamp flickered. "I did not know that was possible. I do not think anyone in the Church knows that is possible. The Synods teach that creation is a battlefield — that every soul is contested. But these people are not being contested. They are simply living."

[[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] said nothing for a long moment. Then: "What does that mean for us?"

"I don't know." [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]]'s voice cracked slightly. "It might mean that God is larger than we were taught. It might mean that the Adversary was never a universal law, only a local catastrophe. It might mean that our eight hundred years of holy war were…" He stopped. He could not finish the sentence.

"It might mean they were still necessary," [[Alistair Pembroke|Pembroke]] said quietly. "Hell opened in our Jerusalem, not theirs. We did not imagine the Tyrants. We did not imagine the [[Grail-spore]] or the Corpse Wars. Whatever their world is, it does not erase ours."

"No," [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] agreed. "It does not. But it does raise a question I cannot answer, and I suspect the Inquisitor-Admiral cannot answer it either."

"Which question?"

[[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] met his captain's eyes. "If God was never here, and the Devil was never here — who has been answering our prayers for eight hundred years?"

### Scene 5: *[[HMS Dragon]]* — The Waiting Game

**[TIME]: Day 2 — 1914-09-15 TC, 05:45 hours / 2026-06-19, 11:45 Zulu**
**[POV]: Captain [[James Hawthorne]]*

The dawn on the TC side was a violent thing — crimson and bruised purple, the clouds low and heavy as if the sky itself were in mourning. Through [[The Anomaly|the portal]], [[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]] could see it bleeding across the other ocean, illuminating the dreadnought *[[HMS Resolute|Resolute]]* in hues of blood and old bronze. The contrast with his own grey Atlantic morning — flat light, clean horizon, the sterile glow of his bridge displays — was almost nauseating.

The night had brought reinforcement. A fourth Type 45, HMS *Dauntless*, had arrived from Portsmouth at 04:00. An Astute-class submarine was now on station somewhere below the thermocline, its presence known only to him and [[Sarah Vance|Commander Vance]] at [[Northwood Headquarters|Northwood]]. A fleet auxiliary was two hours out with fuel and stores. The Royal Navy was building a makeshift forward operating base around a hole in reality.

And on the other side, the Faithful were doing the same. [[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]]'s optics had tracked three new shapes arriving during the night: a fast cruiser, smaller than *[[HMS Resolute|Resolute]]* but newer, her lines sharper; a pair of what looked like torpedo boats, their decks crowded with men in robes rather than uniforms; and most curiously, a vessel that flew no naval ensign but a white banner with a golden cross. A church ship. The priests were coming.

"Sir," Lieutenant Calloway said, handing over an intel summary. "GCHQ has been monitoring the other side's signal lamp traffic. They're using Morse with archaic call signs, but the volume has tripled in the last six hours. They've been relaying to a shore station at what we think is Land's End, and from there by landline telegraph east. Destination: Dover."

"Dover?"

"Their naval headquarters, apparently. A place called the Fortress of the White Cliffs. We're pulling archive imagery now — the geology should be identical to our Dover, but the structures are… well, fortified cathedrals is the closest analogue."

[[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]] absorbed this. "So they've told their high command. Which means someone on their side is about to make a decision." He looked at the church ship, now holding station near the threshold. "And they're sending their best people to understand it."

On the bridge wing, a signal lamp from *[[HMS Resolute|Resolute]]* began to flash. The yeoman — a young Leading Hand who had been taking crash courses in archaic English for eighteen hours straight — scribbled furiously.

"Message reads: 'His Majesty's Government has authorised full scientific and theological exchange. We shall send a delegation aboard HMS Herald under flag of truce. Request permission to cross threshold with unarmed party of twelve. Repeat: unarmed. In the name of the Almighty and King Robert. Await your response.'"

[[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]] let out a breath he hadn't known he was holding. Twelve men. Unarmed. From a world that had fought Hell for eight centuries, a delegation of priests and scientists was volunteering to step into the unknown without a weapon between them.

He thought of [[Rebecca Osei|Osei]], who had done the same from his side. Different worlds. Same courage.

"Reply: 'Request granted. Welcome to our world. We will receive your delegation with honour.' And, Calloway — add 'God save the King.' It seemed to work last time."

### Scene 6: [[The Portal Threshold]] — The Crossing

**[TIME]: Day 2 — 1914-09-15 TC, 08:00 hours / 2026-06-19, 14:00 Zulu**
**[POV]: Lieutenant Commander [[Rebecca Osei]]*

The threshold had become a place.

In the sixteen hours since [[Rebecca Osei|Osei]] had first faced [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] across the shimmering curtain, both sides had improvised a neutral zone. A pair of buoys — one modern, one an iron cask painted with a cross — marked the exact midpoint of the portal. Boats from both worlds now shuttled back and forth along its length, never crossing, maintaining the invisible line as if it were a border drawn in blood. It was, in a sense, the most important border ever created.

The Faithful cruiser HMS *Herald* had anchored two thousand yards on the far side, her boats already in the water. [[Rebecca Osei|Osei]] watched through her binoculars as the first Faithful party disembarked: twelve figures in a motley of uniforms and vestments. Three in grey cassocks — the scientists, she guessed. Two in white-and-crimson — more priests of the [[Sacred Order of the Dragon|Order]]. Six in the dark blue of the Royal Fleet, unarmed as promised, carrying only notebooks and measuring instruments. And at their head, a man she recognised: [[Father Beauchamp]], his [[Orichalcum]] crucifix held before him like a talisman.

Behind them, on the deck of *Herald*, a signal lamp flashed one final message: "WE CROSS IN FAITH."

[[Rebecca Osei|Osei]]'s radio crackled. [[James Hawthorne|Hawthorne]]'s voice: "They're coming through. You're the receiving officer. The Prime Minister is watching. The King is watching. Half the world is watching. No pressure."

She almost smiled. "Understood, sir."

The Faithful boat — a wooden cutter, its oars dipping in perfect unison — nosed toward the shimmering boundary of [[The Anomaly]]. The oarsmen paused. For a long moment, the boat simply sat there, rocking on the swell, the faces of its passengers lit by the unearthly glow. Then [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] made the sign of the cross, the oars bit the water, and the boat slid through.

The curtain rippled. For an instant, the twelve figures were silhouettes of light, their forms stretched and refracted as if passing through a sheet of falling glass. Then they were through — solid, wet with spray, blinking in the grey Atlantic morning of a world that had never known Hell.

[[Rebecca Osei|Osei]]'s RIB pulled alongside. She stood in the bow, one hand on the grab line, the other raised in the universal gesture of welcome.

"On behalf of His Majesty's Government of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland," she said, her voice carrying across the water, "I welcome you to our world."

[[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] rose, unsteady on the unfamiliar swell but refusing any aid. His eyes swept the horizon — the sleek grey shapes of the Type 45 destroyers, the helicopter circling at a respectful distance, the clean hard sky devoid of coal smoke and prayer. He looked, [[Rebecca Osei|Osei]] thought, like a man who had just been told that gravity was optional.

"We accept your welcome," he said, his archaic English ringing across the water. "In the name of the Almighty God, of King Robert the Longsword, and of the Faithful Crown of England. May this meeting be the first stone of a bridge between our worlds, and may God — in whatever form He takes in your universe — bless it."

A pause. Then, quieter: "Your world is very strange to me, Lieutenant Commander. It is like a room from which all the incense has been swept. I cannot smell the war here."

[[Rebecca Osei|Osei]] thought of the news feeds she had watched before deployment — the conflicts, the suffering, the long and bloody histories that her world had written without any demon's help. "We have our own wars, Father. They just smell different."

[[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] nodded slowly, as if this answer was exactly what he had expected and exactly what he had feared. "Then we have more in common than I hoped."

Behind him, the scientists of the [[Holy Science Academy]] were already setting up their instruments on the deck of the cutter — an aetheric gauge humming faintly, a spectrometer aimed at the portal's edge, a quill scribbling notes in a hand that shook with excitement and terror. The investigation had begun.

On both sides of the curtain, the world was about to get much larger.
