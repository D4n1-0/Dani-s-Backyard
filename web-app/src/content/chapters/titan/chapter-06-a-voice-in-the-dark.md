# Chapter 6 – A Voice in the Dark

## Summary

A month passes. CATF consolidates its foothold at Grande Terre, its surveillance network unchanged, its strategic calculus unresolved. The Quadripartite Committee uses the time to debate a question that has no comfortable answer: do you reach out to the fascist empire or the walled island? The empire is stronger, more industrially developed, and already in diplomatic contact with itself — but it runs internment zones and turns people into weapons. The island is weaker, poorer, and isolated — but its leadership wants to live, not conquer. The committee votes to contact Paradis through the Survey Corps: the one faction on the AOT world that has demonstrated a capacity for curiosity and survival beyond ideology. A pod is dropped on a known patrol route along Paradis's new railway. Two scouts find it. By the chapter's end, helicopters have landed in a dark field outside the Walls and two civilisations have spoken for the first time.

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The Railway — Day 103, Dawn

[[Heinrich Vogt]] had been a soldier for seven months and had killed nothing except time.

He was eighteen years old, fresh out of the Training Corps, assigned to a Garrison auxiliary unit that patrolled the new corridor between Wall Rose's eastern gate and the coast. The railway — the iron spine of Paradis's frantic militarisation — ran parallel to the patrol route, narrow-gauge track still gleaming where the work crews had spiked the last section three days earlier. It was the third railway on Paradis. It would not be the last. The Survey Corps had captured a Marleyan cruiser and sunk another, and the Volunteers had brought schematics for naval artillery, and the Queen had ordered every able body on the island to build, and build, and build. [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] was not a builder. He was a rider, a watchman, a pair of eyes on a horse. He patrolled the same twelve kilometres every morning with Corporal [[Lukas Strieder]], a man twenty-three years old who had survived the Titan purges of Wall Maria and talked about it as though it had happened to someone else.

The dawn was grey. The mist hung low over the grassland, the kind of mist that turned the world into a watercolour sketch. [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] rode with his rifle slung across his back, his collar turned up against the cold, his horse picking its way along the packed-dirt patrol road with the mechanical patience of an animal that had walked this route three hundred times.

The glint caught his eye at thirty metres.

It was small. Metallic. Lying in the short grass ten paces off the patrol road, not buried, not hidden — placed, or dropped, or fallen. It caught the grey dawn light in a way that grass did not. In a way that nothing on Paradis did.

[[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] reined his horse. [[Lukas Strieder|Lukas]] pulled up beside him.

"What?"

"There. In the grass."

Corporal [[Lukas Strieder|Lukas]] dismounted. He was not a cautious man — the Titan purges had burned caution out of him the way a forest fire burned the undergrowth, leaving only the things that could not burn — but he approached the object slowly, the way a soldier approached anything that did not belong on a patrol route he had ridden three hundred times.

The pod was the size of a man's palm. Smooth. Seamless except for a thin line around its equator where two halves met. Warm — [[Lukas Strieder|Lukas]] felt the heat before his fingers touched the metal. Not hot. Warm. Alive, in the way a machine was alive when current was running through it.

"Is it Marleyan?" [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] asked.

"No."

"How do you know?"

"Because Marley builds with iron and steam. This is not iron. This is not steam." [[Lukas Strieder|Lukas]] turned the pod over in his hands. No markings. No script. No manufacturer's stamp. Just smooth metal and that thin seam and the faint, steady warmth of something that was waiting.

"I'm going to call for someone," [[Lukas Strieder|Lukas]] said.

"Who?"

"The closest officer who can think faster than we can. That eliminates most of the Garrison." [[Lukas Strieder|Lukas]] stood. "Corporal [[Armin Arlert|Arlert]] has been inspecting the coastal battery positions. He's half a day's ride east. If we push, we can have him here by dusk."

[[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] looked at the pod in [[Lukas Strieder|Lukas]]'s hand. It had not moved. It had not made a sound. But the warmth was still there, steady, patient. The warmth of something that was not finished.

"You go," [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] said. "You're faster. I'll stay with it."

[[Lukas Strieder|Lukas]] looked at him for a long moment. Then he nodded, handed [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] the pod, and mounted his horse. The hoofbeats faded into the mist. [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] was alone with the warm metal and the grey dawn and the railway that led to the sea.

### Scene 2: Armin — Day 103, Dusk

[[Armin Arlert]] arrived at dusk, riding hard with two soldiers from the coastal battery detail, his blond hair wind-scoured and his expression already moving faster than his horse. He was sixteen years old and carried the Colossal Titan inside him, and he had learned — across ten months of war and revelation and the weight of inheriting a dead man's strategic genius — that small things in the grass mattered more than large things in the capital.

[[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] showed him the pod.

[[Armin Arlert|Armin]] took it. Turned it over. The warmth registered on his face — a flicker of recognition, not of the object but of its implications. The seam. The material. The steady heat of a powered circuit. Marley did not build with circuits. Marley built with steel and coal and the bones of a stolen continent. This was something else. This was something from further away than any sea on any map.

"There's a seam," [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] said. "It opens."

"You can't open it," [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] said. "We tried."

[[Armin Arlert|Armin]] did not try. He held the pod in both hands and waited, because if the object was meant to be found — and it was, the placement was too deliberate for accident — then the object would speak when it was ready.

It spoke.

The seam split without a sound. The two halves of the pod separated along a hinge [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] had not noticed, and a small, flat screen lit up between them. The screen showed a map — a map of Paradis, rendered in a precision that no Paradisian cartographer could match, annotated with grid lines and coordinates in a script that was not Marleyan and not Eldian and not anything [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] had ever seen. A red dot pulsed at a point in the open grassland, roughly thirty kilometres from where they stood.

Then the voice began.

It was a man's voice. He spoke Eldian with an accent that was careful and imperfect, the accent of someone who had learned the language from a book and then from conversation with native speakers. The voice was calm. The voice was not hostile.

*"To the people of Paradis. My name is Dr. Elias Vogler. I was a scientist of the Marleyan Empire, and I am now an advisor to the Combined Anomaly Task Force. We are not Marleyan. We are not your enemy. We come from a world beyond the sea — further than any sea you know. We have watched your world for months. We know about the Walls. We know about the Titans. We know about the world that wants you dead. And we know that you have been told, by everyone who has ever spoken to you, that you are alone."*

[[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] stared at the pod. His mouth was open. He did not remember opening it.

*"You are not alone. There are people beyond the horizon who do not want to kill you. We want to talk. We want to help. We want to understand what you are facing, because what you are facing may one day threaten us as well. This is not a demand. It is an invitation. If you accept: go to the coordinates on this map. Wait in the dark. Come in peace. We will find you."*

The voice stopped. The map continued to pulse. The pod was silent.

[[Armin Arlert|Armin]] did not move for a long time.

"Someone else needs to hear this," he said. "Someone who can make a decision I am not authorised to make."

---

[[Lukas Strieder|Lukas]] rode for [[Hange Zoë|Hange]]. The journey was half a day at full pace — east to the coast, then north along the supply road toward the old Survey Corps headquarters, where [[Hange Zoë|Commander Hange]] had been coordinating the naval fortification effort. He left at first dark with the pod in a saddlebag and a message in his head that he had not been trained to deliver. He was a garrison corporal, not a Survey Corps messenger, and the thing he was carrying was not a report. It was a door. And doors, once opened, could not be closed.

[[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] stayed with [[Armin Arlert|Armin]]. They built a small fire — not for warmth, but for light, because the dark had become larger than it had been at dawn. They sat in the grass and watched the pod. The map still pulsed. The red dot still blinked. [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] did not sleep. He was thinking. [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] could see him thinking the way he had seen an engineer calculate the load on a bridge: piece by piece, stress by stress, the whole structure held in the mind until it either stood or collapsed.

"What do you think it is?" [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] asked.

"A message," [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] said. "From people who want us to know they exist before we discover it ourselves."

"Is that good or bad?"

"That depends on why they want us to know." [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] looked at the pod. "If they wanted us dead, they wouldn't have announced themselves."

### Scene 3: Hange — Day 104, Midday

[[Hange Zoë]] arrived with [[Levi Ackerman]], [[Mikasa Ackerman]], and six soldiers of the Survey Corps. She had ridden through the night, switching horses at the relay stations [[Lukas Strieder|Lukas]] had flagged, arriving at the patrol post at dawn and continuing east without rest. Her goggles were pushed up on her forehead. Her eyes were bright with the particular intensity of a scientist who had just been handed a puzzle that rewrote every assumption she had made about the world.

She played the recording twice.

The first time, she listened in silence. Her expression was unreadable — the Commander's face, the mask she had learned to wear when receiving intelligence that could be a gift or a trap or both.

The second time, she took notes. She had [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] freeze the recording at the map display and copied the grid lines, the coordinates, the labelling convention. She asked [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] to describe the placement of the pod — every detail, every angle, the exact distance from the patrol road. She listened to the recording a third time with her eyes closed, focusing on the accent, the word choice, the cadence. The scientist in her was cataloguing. The Commander in her was waiting.

When the recording finished the third time, she opened her eyes.

"It's not Marleyan," she said.

"We established that," [[Levi Ackerman|Levi]] said.

"The accent. The speaker is a Marleyan — or was. He learned Eldian recently. His pronunciation is book-acquired, but the Marleyan phonetic structure is native. He said he was a scientist of the Marleyan Empire. That is either the truth or a very specific lie. And it is not the kind of lie Marley would tell — they would send a soldier, not a scientist." She looked at [[Armin Arlert|Armin]]. "Your assessment?"

"They know too much to be guessing," [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] said. "The map alone — the precision of the coastline, the topography, the grid system that we don't use — that takes months of survey work. They've been watching us for a long time. And they chose to reveal themselves now. Something changed on their side. Something made them decide the time was right."

"Or they want us to think something changed." [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] looked at the pod. "If it's a trap, what do they gain?"

"Us," [[Levi Ackerman|Levi]] said. "The Commander of the Survey Corps. The Colossal Titan. Humanity's strongest soldier. The Ackerman bloodline. If Marley captures us at a fake rendezvous, the war is over."

"And if it's not a trap?"

Silence. The kind of silence that followed a question no one had a framework for.

[[Armin Arlert|Armin]] spoke first. "If it's not a trap, it's an opportunity that changes everything. An outside power — not Marleyan, with technology Marley doesn't have — that wants to talk instead of kill. That wants to understand the Rumbling instead of trigger it. That can give us things we can't build ourselves." He paused. "The basement told us the world wants us dead. This is the first evidence we have that the basement was incomplete."

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]] looked at the red dot pulsing on the map. Thirty kilometres. Open grassland. Wait in the dark.

"We go," she said. "Not all of us. Not [[Eren Yeager|Eren]]. Not [[Historia Reiss|the Queen]]. But enough of us to show we are serious. Enough to fight if it turns. And enough to listen if it doesn't."

### Scene 4: The Council — Day 104, Afternoon

The council convened on horseback — [[Hange Zoë|Hange]], [[Levi Ackerman|Levi]], [[Mikasa Ackerman|Mikasa]], [[Armin Arlert|Armin]], [[Jean Kirstein]], [[Connie Springer]], two senior Survey Corps officers, and two representatives from the Garrison command. Ten soldiers on ten horses, circled around the pod on the patrol road, the railway gleaming in the afternoon sun behind them.

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]] laid out the plan. Leave at dusk. Ride to the coordinates. Arrive before midnight. Wait in the dark. No torches. No signals. No weapons visible unless the situation required them. And if the situation required them —

"We fight," [[Levi Ackerman|Levi]] said. "We know how to do that."

"We also know how to lose," [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] said. "If this is a Marleyan trap, we are vastly outnumbered in contested territory. If this is not a Marleyan trap, we are meeting a power that has technology we cannot match. In neither scenario does a firefight end well for us. So we talk. We listen. We think. The blades are for if thinking fails."

[[Jean Kirstein|Jean]] shifted in his saddle. "And [[Eren Yeager|Eren]]? [[Historia Reiss|Historia]]?"

"[[Eren Yeager|Eren]] stays at headquarters. He is the Founding Titan. If this is a trap, we cannot let them capture the Rumbling in a single night. [[Historia Reiss|Historia]] stays for the same reason — our head of state does not attend a negotiation with an unknown power."

"Does [[Eren Yeager|Eren]] know we're doing this?"

"He will. After we return." [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] looked at each of them in turn. "If we return. If we don't, the letter I left at headquarters explains what we found. By then, the decision will be out of our hands."

The council dissolved into quiet preparation. Horses were fed and watered. ODM gear was checked and strapped. Blades were sharpened. [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] watched the ten soldiers prepare, and for the first time since dawn the previous day, he felt the specific fear of a person who had accidentally become involved in something larger than himself. He had been a patrolman. He had found a warm piece of metal in the grass. And now, because of that piece of metal, the Commander of the Survey Corps and the Colossal Titan and the strongest soldier in human history were riding toward a dot on a map that pulsed in the dark.

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]] noticed him watching. She walked over — the Commander's walk, tired but still sharp, the walk of a woman who had not slept in forty hours and would not sleep for forty more.

"Private Vogt," she said. She had learned his name from [[Armin Arlert|Armin]].

"Commander."

"You found this. You called for help. You stayed with it through the night. That is more initiative than most soldiers show in a year." She paused. "I am not bringing you to the rendezvous. That is not a punishment. It is a precaution. If we do not return, someone needs to report what happened. Someone who saw the pod. Someone who heard the recording. Someone the brass will believe because they were there."

[[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] nodded. He was relieved and ashamed of his relief and also, in some part of himself he had not known existed, disappointed.

"Yes, Commander."

"Stay at the patrol post. Wait three days. If we are not back by the fourth morning — ride to headquarters. Tell them everything." She looked at the pod in [[Armin Arlert|Armin]]'s saddlebag. "Everything except the coordinates. If this fails, the next attempt should be someone else's decision."

She mounted her horse. The ten soldiers rode east, toward the red dot that pulsed in [[Armin Arlert|Armin]]'s hands, toward the dark grassland where someone — or something — was waiting.

[[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] watched them go.

### Scene 5: The Rendezvous — Night of Day 104

The grassland was a black sheet stretched between two dark horizons. No moon. No stars visible through the low cloud that had rolled in from the sea at dusk. The ten soldiers sat on their horses in the absolute dark, the coordinates confirmed by [[Armin Arlert|Armin]]'s map and the pod's display, and waited.

No one spoke. The horses stamped and snorted, their breath steaming in the cold night air. [[Levi Ackerman|Levi]] had positioned himself at the group's western edge, his blades sheathed but his hands restless on the reins. [[Mikasa Ackerman|Mikasa]] flanked the eastern edge, her posture identical — the Ackerman instinct, the body's refusal to relax when the mind could not calculate the threat. [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] was at the centre, still as stone, her goggles pushed down over her eyes to protect them from the rotor wash she did not yet know was coming.

Minutes passed. [[Connie Springer|Connie]] shifted in his saddle — a small sound, leather on leather, but in the dark it was loud.

"How long do we wait?" he whispered.

"As long as it takes," [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] said.

More minutes. The darkness pressed against them. The horses grew still. The cold seeped through wool and leather and the thin barrier of training that separated patience from fear.

Then — a sound.

Faint. Distant. Rhythmic. A beating, like a heart wrapped in metal, coming from somewhere above and behind them. [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] was the first to look up, because [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] had learned that the most important threats on a battlefield came from somewhere you were not looking.

"There," he said.

The sound grew. The beating became a roar — two roars, interleaved, the sound of machines pushing air, of blades spinning at speeds that no Paradisian engineering could match. The horses reared. Soldiers fought for control. The sound was everywhere now, above them, around them, pounding through the dark like a storm that had condensed into a single point in the sky.

And then the light came.

It was not gradual. It was not a dawn, a torch, a signal flare. It was the sun — two suns, three suns, a constellation of lights that snapped on simultaneously, turning the grassland into a white-lit stage. Navigation lights. Landing lights. Spotlights. The beams converged on the ten soldiers, pinning them in a circle of blinding brightness so intense that [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] threw her arm across her goggles and [[Jean Kirstein|Jean]] swore and [[Connie Springer|Connie]]'s horse nearly threw him.

The shapes behind the lights resolved slowly — two machines, hovering above the grass, each the size of a house. They had no wings. They had no tethers. They hung in the air on a column of noise and force, their rotors turning the grassland into a hurricane of flattened grass and flying debris. They were metal and glass and engineering from a world that did not exist. They were impossible. And they were landing.

The first machine touched down a hundred metres away. The second followed, settling beside it like a bird of prey folding its wings. The rotors did not stop. The noise continued — a physical pressure against the chest, a vibration that [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] could feel in his teeth.

A door opened in the side of the nearest machine. A figure emerged — tall, civilian-clothed, hands raised and empty. He walked forward into the light, his shadow stretching twenty metres behind him across the flattened grass. His mouth moved. The sound of his voice was swallowed by the rotors.

"Who is that?" [[Jean Kirstein|Jean]] shouted.

[[Armin Arlert|Armin]] squinted against the light. "The voice. The recording. It's him."

[[Elias Vogler]] stopped halfway between the helicopters and the horses. He stood in the light, hands still raised, waiting. He did not move forward. He did not move back. He waited, because the rotors were too loud for words, and because the first rule of contact with an unknown power was to demonstrate patience before power.

The rotors slowed.

The noise dropped — first to a hum, then to a whine, then to the ticking of cooling metal. The silence that followed was deeper than the silence before the helicopters had arrived, because it was a silence that had been earned.

The lights stayed on. The ten soldiers stood bathed in white, their shadows pooled at their feet, their horses trembling but still. [[Elias Vogler]] lowered his hands.

"We came because you came," he said, in careful Eldian. "Thank you for trusting us enough to be here."

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]] dismounted. She walked forward — past [[Levi Ackerman|Levi]], past [[Mikasa Ackerman|Mikasa]], past the edge of the light pool that marked the boundary between the known world and whatever lay beyond it. She stopped three paces from [[Elias Vogler]], close enough to see his face, close enough to see that he was tired and unarmed and not a soldier. A scientist. Like her.

"How long have you been watching us?" she asked.

[[Elias Vogler]] met her eyes. "A little over three months. Since the anomaly that brought us to your ocean opened. We mapped your country. We watched your war. We saw your Volunteers arrive. We saw you capture the Marleyan cruiser. We saw your railway being built." He paused. "We saw a great deal. Most of it made us want to help. Some of it made us afraid. The fear is not of you. The fear is of what you are carrying inside your Walls."

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]]'s expression did not change, but something behind her eyes shifted. The scientist in her had just received confirmation of a hypothesis she had not dared to formulate aloud.

"You know about the Wall Titans."

"We know. We mapped them. We counted them. The number is between three and six hundred thousand Colossal Titans embedded in Walls Maria, Rose, and Sina. We know they can be activated. We know the activation mechanism is the Founding Titan. We know the Founder is a fifteen-year-old boy who recently learned that the entire world wants his species exterminated." [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] did not blink. "We know the Rumbling is real. It is the reason we are standing here tonight."

### Scene 6: The Meeting

The delegation assembled between the helicopters and the horses — two groups of humans, twelve in total, facing each other across a patch of flattened grass on an island that had never been a meeting ground before.

[[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] introduced his companions. Lieutenant Colonel [[Tanaka Kenji]], an officer of the Japanese Ground Self-Defense Force and the intelligence chief of the [[Combined Anomaly Task Force (CATF)|Combined Anomaly Task Force]] — a quiet man with watchful eyes, standing slightly behind [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] in the posture of a soldier who had learned that the best intelligence came from listening. Dr. [[Ahn Jae-hyun]], a biologist of the Republic of Korea and the lead scientist of CATF's biological analysis division — a man with glasses and the specific exhaustion of someone who had been awake for two days preparing for a meeting that might end in violence or science or both.

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]] introduced her people. [[Levi Ackerman]]. [[Mikasa Ackerman]]. [[Armin Arlert]]. [[Jean Kirstein]]. [[Connie Springer]]. The names meant nothing to [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] — his expression did not flicker — but they meant something to [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]], who had studied the drone imagery of the Battle of Shiganshina and knew that the short man with the grey eyes was the deadliest close-quarters combatant on either Earth.

"Where are you from?" [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] asked. "You said a world beyond the sea. What does that mean?"

[[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] looked at [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]]. [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] nodded. This was the moment they had rehearsed. The moment the committee had authorised, after a month of debate, in a compromise that pleased no one.

"We are from another Earth," [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] said. "A parallel world. Identical in geography to your own, but inverted — your north is our south, your east our west. Our world is called Earth. Our year is 2026. Our technology is approximately one century ahead of Marley's, and we crossed into your world through a stable portal — a Gate — that opened in what would be your southern ocean. The Gate is 500 metres across. It is permanent. It is not closing. And we have spent the last three months building a base on the far side of it, watching your world, trying to decide whether to make contact and who to make contact with."

The silence that followed was the silence of ten soldiers absorbing the fact that everything they knew about the nature of reality had just been rewritten. [[Jean Kirstein|Jean]] looked at [[Armin Arlert|Armin]]. [[Armin Arlert|Armin]]'s expression was unreadable — not because he was hiding anything, but because his mind was moving too fast for his face to keep up.

"You could have contacted Marley," [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] said. "They are the dominant power on our world. They have the industry. They have the military. Why us?"

"Because Marley is a fascist empire that runs internment zones and turns human beings into weapons," [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] said flatly. "I was a Marleyan scientist. I know exactly what my government would do with access to our technology. They would use it to finish the Mid-East war, to exterminate the Eldian population on the continent, and to invade Paradis with weapons that Marleyan industry cannot produce but our industry can. We did not come to this world to arm a genocide."

"And we?" [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] asked.

"You want to survive. You want to live. You want to understand the world that has been trying to kill you for a century. Those are goals we can work with. Those are goals that do not end with corpses stacked to the ceiling and a Rumbling marching across the planet."

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]] studied him. The scientist's assessment, running alongside the Commander's calculus, weighing every word against the possibility of deception and finding — impossibly, against every instinct she had developed across five years of war — that the words held. Or that the deception was too elaborate for anyone to maintain.

"What do you want from us?"

It was [[Tanaka Kenji|Lieutenant Colonel Tanaka]] who answered. His Eldian was less fluent than [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]]'s, but his tone was the tone of a man who had delivered strategic assessments to four governments and was not intimidated by an audience of ten soldiers in a dark field.

"Three things," he said. "Stability. We want the Rumbling to never be triggered. The Wall Titans mapped by our drones represent the largest existential threat in either of our worlds. We want a relationship with Paradis that ensures the Founding Titan is never used as a weapon. That is our first priority."

"Security," he continued. "We want a secure operating environment on the southern continent where our Gate is located. That requires a stable northern neighbourhood. Your conflict with Marley threatens to destabilise this world. We want to understand that conflict and, if possible, help resolve it in a way that does not produce a global extinction event."

"And research," [[Ahn Jae-hyun|Dr. Ahn]] said. He had not spoken before. His Eldian was heavily accented — learned in a month, from materials that [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] and [[Frieda]] had prepared, in sessions that ran late into the Antarctic night. "Our coalition has significant scientific resources. We want to study Titan biology, the nature of the transformation, the mechanism that produces Titans from human subjects. We want to understand what your bodies can do — not to weaponise it, but to prevent it from being weaponised against you. And — " he stopped. [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] made a small gesture: go on. " — and we want to understand the physics of what happens when a Subject of Ymir transforms. The transformation creates mass from nothing. It violates the most fundamental laws of our universe. If we can understand how, we can — "

"You can replicate it," [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] said. "You can use us the same way Marley uses us. As a resource."

"No." [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]]'s voice was sharp. "Marley turns Eldians into Titans and drops them on enemy formations. We have no interest in Titans. We have interest in the mechanism — in understanding how mass can be created ex nihilo. That understanding could benefit everyone. Including you. Including your people, who have been turned into weapons by an empire that does not care whether you live or die. We are not Marley, Commander. If we were, we would not be standing in a field asking for permission. We would have landed soldiers on your coast and taken what we wanted."

"Ex nihilo." [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] said it slowly, testing the unfamiliar syllables. His voice was not suspicious — it was curious, the voice of a boy who had spent his childhood reading books about a world outside the Walls that no one believed existed. "What does that mean?"

"It is Latin," [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] said. "An old Earth word. It means *from nothing*. When a Subject of Ymir transforms into a Titan, the mass of the Titan is not converted from the mass of the human body. It is added. A sixty-five-kilogram soldier becomes a thirty-two-tonne Titan. The extra thirty-two tonnes do not come from fuel, from food, from any energy source we can detect. The matter appears. It is created. Ex nihilo."

[[Armin Arlert|Armin]] was silent. The other scouts — soldiers who had watched Titans rampage through their cities, had killed them, had been killed by them — absorbed the implication. The creatures they had fought for five years were not transformations. They were creations. Something was adding thirty tonnes of flesh and bone to the world every time an Eldian was injected with spinal fluid. The source of that addition existed beyond the reach of science.

[[Connie Springer|Connie]] spoke into the silence. His voice was quiet, the voice of a soldier who had learned to accept the impossible because the alternative was paralysis.

"What about resources? The man said resource extraction. What does that mean?"

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] answered. His Eldian slowed when he reached technical vocabulary — he had practiced this section of the briefing more than any other, because it was the section most likely to be mistaken for a demand.

"Your world has resources — minerals, fuels, biological compounds — that our world either lacks or has already depleted. We would want access to those resources, under a negotiated framework that compensates Paradis. But there is one resource in particular that we believe only Paradis possesses."

He glanced at [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]]. [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] picked up the thread, the handoff practiced across a dozen rehearsal sessions in the briefing room at Grande Terre.

"Your soldiers fight with a device we have never seen before," [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] said. "Blades — but also cables. Grappling hooks. Compressed-gas propulsion. Our drones have watched your Survey Corps use this equipment against Titans, against Marleyan warships, against terrain that no infantry should be able to traverse. The propulsion mechanism requires a fuel source that produces far more thrust per volume than coal or steam. We do not have that fuel. We do not know how to make it. But we know it exists."

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]]'s expression sharpened. The scientist in her had just been handed a question, and the Commander in her knew that the answer was a strategic asset. "Iceburst stone."

"Iceburst stone," [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] repeated. "I know the Marleyan term. The empire has wanted Paradis's iceburst stone deposits since before the Warrior Unit breached Wall Maria. Marleyan military intelligence estimated that control of the Paradisian iceburst fields would eliminate their dependence on Hizuran optics and give them a propellant technology that no rival power could match. The estimate was accurate. Marley's interest was — " he paused, " — acquisitive. Ours is not."

"Then what is yours?"

"Scientific. Strategic. The same as our interest in Titan biology — we want to understand it. Iceburst stone is a naturally occurring propellant that does not exist on our Earth. Its energy density — the amount of thrust it produces per unit of mass — appears to exceed anything our chemistry has produced outside of rocket fuels. And your engineers have been refining it in forges, not laboratories. If we can understand how it works — its chemical structure, its formation process, its vapour-expansion mechanics — we can learn things that our own world has not yet discovered."

"And in exchange?" [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] asked.

"Technology transfer. Engineering support. Medical infrastructure. Your people are building a railway with steam shovels and manual labour. We can give you earth-moving equipment that cuts the construction time by a factor of ten. Your hospitals are clean but primitive — we can give you antibiotics, surgical training, diagnostic tools. Your military is fighting a war against an empire with a century of industrial development behind it. We can give you radios, night-vision optics, early-warning systems — not weapons, at this stage, but the things that let you see a threat before it arrives. And in exchange: access to iceburst stone samples. Raw material. Survey rights to map the deposits. A percentage of annual extraction, negotiated at a rate that Paradis sets — not us."

[[Jean Kirstein|Jean]] spoke before [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] could. "You want our fuel. The one thing Marley can't make and Hizuru can't buy. You want the thing that keeps our soldiers alive against Titans."

"The Titans are nearly extinct on Paradis," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said. "Your own cleanup operation, monitored by our drones, has reduced the Pure Titan population to fewer than two dozen remaining individuals. Your soldiers' mobility advantage is shifting — from Titan-killing to fighting human enemies with artillery and firearms. Iceburst stone remains valuable. But its value to Paradis is evolving. And the value of what we are offering — the technology, the infrastructure, the strategic partnership — is not contingent on any single resource. It is contingent on a relationship. The stone opens a door. The door is what matters."

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]] was silent for a long moment. The lights of the helicopters hummed. The wind moved through the flattened grass. Somewhere in the dark, an animal — a Paradisian hare, or whatever equivalent filled the same ecological niche — rustled and fled.

"Before I agree to anything," she said, "I need to know something. You mapped the Wall Titans. You counted them. You know about the Rumbling. My question is not whether you can prevent it. My question is whether you have a plan to kill us if you cannot."

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] looked at [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]]. [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] looked at [[Ahn Jae-hyun|Ahn]]. The three of them exchanged a glance that was not hesitation but confirmation — the silent agreement of three people who had discussed this exact question, at length, and reached a conclusion none of them was comfortable delivering.

"Yes," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said. "We have a plan. It is a plan we hope never to use. It is a plan that would make us the monsters Marley claims you are. And it is a plan that becomes unnecessary the moment we have a diplomatic relationship with Paradis that guarantees the Rumbling will never be activated. That is why we are here, Commander. Because the alternative — for both of us — is a conversation we do not want to have."

The silence that followed was the silence of a truth that both parties had acknowledged. [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] did not flinch. She had asked the question for a reason, and the answer, however grim, was the answer she had expected.

"I believe you," she said. "Not because I trust you. Because a lie would have been softer. You told me the truth that makes you look worse. That is either honesty or a very sophisticated manipulation. And a manipulation that sophisticated would have started with a better lie." She turned to her people. "I am going to recommend that the Queen accept this relationship. The terms will need to be negotiated. The agreements will need to be written. But the principle — coexistence, cooperation, mutual survival — is a better offer than anything Marley has ever made us. Does anyone disagree?"

[[Levi Ackerman|Levi]] said nothing. [[Mikasa Ackerman|Mikasa]] said nothing. [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] — the strategist, the boy who had been Erwin Smith's heir and was now watching his most impossible hope become reality — shook his head.

"No," [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] said. "I don't disagree."

[[Jean Kirstein|Jean]] and [[Connie Springer|Connie]] nodded. The Garrison officers, silent throughout the negotiation, exchanged glances and deferred to the Survey Corps leadership. The vote was unanimous, in the way that a vote could be unanimous when the alternative was extinction.

"Then we have an agreement in principle," [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] said. She looked at [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]]. "I want a second meeting. Same location. Same conditions. One week from tonight — "

"One week," [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] agreed. "We will bring a formal proposal."

"And I want something else."

[[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] waited.

"You said you have an Eldian crew. You said you are not Marleyan. You said you are not our enemy. Prove it. Send one of your people to Paradis — not as a prisoner, not as a hostage, but as a liaison. Someone who can live among us, answer our questions, and report back to you on what we are actually like. And we will send one of our people to your base — same purpose, same freedom, same risk."

[[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] absorbed this. The concept of a liaison exchange — each side giving the other a hostage — was not part of the committee's authorisation. But the commander's request was reasonable. It was exactly what he would have asked for, if the positions were reversed.

He looked at [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]]. [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] almost imperceptibly nodded.

"We accept," [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] said. "We will send Captain [[Park Sung-ho]] of the Republic of Korea Marine Corps. He is an experienced officer who has commanded combat operations. He is not a diplomat, but he is honest, and he will give you a fair assessment of our capabilities and intentions."

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]] turned to her people. Her eyes moved across [[Levi Ackerman|Levi]], [[Mikasa Ackerman|Mikasa]], [[Armin Arlert|Armin]], [[Jean Kirstein|Jean]] — the obvious candidates, the ones who would volunteer, the ones she could not afford to lose.

"[[Connie Springer|Connie]]," she said.

[[Connie Springer|Connie]] straightened. "Commander?"

"You are the best soldier in this group who is not a Shifter, not an Ackerman, and not indispensable to our combat operations. I am asking you to go with them — to their base, to their world — as our eyes and ears. Not as a hostage. As a volunteer. You have the right to refuse."

[[Connie Springer|Connie]] looked at the helicopters. At the men who had flown them. At the world that had watched his island for three months without anyone knowing. He thought about his mother — the Titan in the barn in Ragako, the frozen face he had not visited in months, the question that no Paradisian science could answer. He thought about what a civilisation with flying machines might know about how to turn a Titan back into a human being.

"I'll go," he said.

### Scene 7: Departure — Night of Day 104

[[Park Sung-ho|Captain Park Sung-ho]] stepped out of the second helicopter. He had been waiting in the cabin, listening through the open cockpit door, observing the negotiation through the windshield. He was thirty-eight years old, a Marine Corps officer with two tours in Special Reconnaissance, and he crossed the flattened grass toward [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] with the gait of a soldier who had just been told he was being handed over to an alien military and had processed this information in approximately four seconds.

"I am Captain Park Sung-ho," he said, in heavily accented Eldian. He had learned fifty phrases in the month between Ch5's conclusion and this night, drilled by [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] and [[Frieda]] and a voice-recording app on a tablet. "I will serve as liaison and observer. I am here to talk, not to fight. I am honoured to be your guest."

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]] looked at him. He was shorter than her, built compact, with the watchful stillness of a soldier who had learned to read a room before the room read him. She nodded.

"Welcome to Paradis, Captain Park."

[[Connie Springer|Connie]] walked forward. He passed [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] without speaking — there was nothing to say that had not been said in the look they exchanged — and stopped in front of [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]].

"Private [[Connie Springer]], Survey Corps. I don't know what a liaison does. But I'll figure it out."

"We'll help you," [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]] said. "There are Eldians on our base. Engineers. Former Marleyan citizens, like me. They will be glad to meet someone from Paradis who isn't trying to kill them."

The handshakes that followed were stiff, formal, the gestures of two species that had just learned the other was not hostile. [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] shook [[Elias Vogler|Vogler]]'s hand. [[Levi Ackerman|Levi]] did not shake anyone's hand, but he did not reach for his blades, which was the closest thing to a handshake [[Levi Ackerman|Levi]] had ever offered a stranger. [[Armin Arlert|Armin]] shook [[Ahn Jae-hyun|Dr. Ahn]]'s hand and asked him, in careful Eldian, whether he had studied Titan regeneration rates. [[Ahn Jae-hyun|Dr. Ahn]]'s face lit up with the specific joy of a scientist who had just been asked the right question by the right person.

The rotors started.

The noise returned — the beating heart of the machines, the hurricane of flattened grass. The CATF delegation retreated toward the helicopters, [[Connie Springer|Springer]] walking between [[Tanaka Kenji|Tanaka]] and [[Ahn Jae-hyun|Ahn]], his silhouette small against the white lights. [[Park Sung-ho|Captain Park]] remained on the Paradis side of the light line, standing beside [[Hange Zoë|Hange]], watching his transport lift off without him.

The helicopters rose — first one, then the other, climbing vertically into the dark sky like impossible birds, their lights shrinking and then vanishing, their rotor noise fading to a hum and then to silence and then to memory. The dark returned. The wind stilled. The ten soldiers and one Korean Marine stood in the flattened grass, listening to the silence that follows a miracle.

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]] looked at [[Park Sung-ho|Captain Park]]. "Your people have been watching us for three months. How much do you know?"

"A lot," [[Park Sung-ho|Park]] said. "But not everything. And most of what I know, Commander — " he paused, searching for the Eldian word, " — is classified."

"Then you and I are going to have a very long conversation on the ride home."

She mounted her horse. [[Park Sung-ho|Park]], who had not ridden a horse since his childhood on his grandfather's farm in Chungcheong, climbed into the saddle of the horse that had carried [[Connie Springer|Connie]] to the rendezvous and was now carrying a stranger back.

The group rode west, toward the Walls, toward the railway and the port and the world that had just become larger than anyone on Paradis had ever imagined. The horses moved in single file through the dark, their riders silent, their shadows stretching ahead of them across the grass that was still flattened from the rotor wash.

Behind them, the rendezvous site returned to emptiness. The grass would rise again in a day or two. The hoofprints would fade in a week. The only lasting mark of what had happened would be the things that had been said — the voices in the dark, the agreement made under alien lights, the door that had opened and would never close again.

### Scene 8: The Patrol Post — Day 107, Dawn

[[Heinrich Vogt]] waited.

Three days. Three dawn patrols along the same twelve kilometres of road, the railway glinting in the morning light, the pod still warm in his saddlebag. He had listened to the recording seven more times — not because he needed to hear it again, but because the voice of [[Elias Vogler|Dr. Vogler]] was the only evidence he had that the previous night had not been a dream.

On the fourth morning, a column of riders appeared on the eastern horizon.

There were eleven of them. Ten in Survey Corps green, one in a uniform [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] had never seen — dark fabric, unfamiliar cut, a flag patch on the shoulder that was not Marleyan and not Paradisian. They were tired. Their horses were exhausted. But they were alive, and they were riding home, and the man in the strange uniform was talking to Commander [[Hange Zoë|Hange]] in a language [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] could not understand.

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]] saw him waiting at the patrol post and raised a hand — a gesture that meant nothing and everything, the casual acknowledgment of a Commander who had just negotiated the survival of her species and was too tired to make a speech about it.

"Private Vogt," she said, reining her horse. "Good news. The people with the flying machines want to be friends. And we have a guest."

[[Park Sung-ho|Captain Park]] inclined his head. He did not speak Eldian well enough to introduce himself, but he understood the word *guest*, and he understood that the young soldier in front of him, the one with the stiff posture and the saddlebag and the expression of someone who had been awake for three days, was the person who had found the pod.

"Thank you," [[Park Sung-ho|Park]] said, in careful, imperfect Eldian. "For finding it."

[[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] did not know what to say. So he said the thing that was true.

"I was just doing my patrol, sir."

[[Hange Zoë|Hange]] laughed — the first time [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] had ever heard her laugh, a short, exhausted sound that was half relief and half the specific absurdity of a soldier who had just been told that the most important discovery in human history began with a patrolman doing his job.

"That," she said, "is exactly the right answer."

The column rode on. [[Heinrich Vogt|Heinrich]] watched them go — the eleven riders, the eleven horses, the flag patch on Captain [[Park Sung-ho|Park]]'s shoulder catching the morning light. The railway gleamed behind them. The sea — the same sea that had delivered Marleyan warships and Volunteer defectors and, now, a message from another world — stretched grey and endless to the east.

He reached into his saddlebag. The pod was still warm.

He put it back. He would turn it in at headquarters, eventually. But for now — for the three-day ride back to Wall Rose — he wanted to carry it. Not because he needed it. Because he had earned it. Because a patrolman who found the future in the grass deserved to carry it home.

## Key Decisions

- CATF Quadripartite Committee votes to contact Survey Corps over Marley after a month of deliberation — weighting the Corps' demonstrated curiosity and survival instinct over Marley's industrial strength and moral bankruptcy
- First Contact Pod deliberately dropped on a known Paradis patrol route along the new railway
- [[Armin Arlert]] recognises the pod as non-Marleyan technology and elevates to [[Hange Zoë]]
- [[Hange Zoë]] leads a council; votes to attend the rendezvous with a force of ten, excluding [[Eren Yeager]] and [[Historia Reiss]]
- Agreement in principle reached: cooperation against existential threats (Rumbling), research exchange, and resource extraction — named specifically: iceburst stone deposits in exchange for technology transfer, engineering support, and medical infrastructure

- Liaison exchange agreed: [[Connie Springer]] to CATF, Captain [[Park Sung-ho]] to Paradis
- Second rendezvous set for one week later
