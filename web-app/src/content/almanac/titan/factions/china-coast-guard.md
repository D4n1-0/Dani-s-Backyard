# China Coast Guard

## Overview
Maritime security, law enforcement, and sovereignty assertion branch of the People's Armed Police of China. Formed 2013; reorganised 2018 under direct Central Military Commission authority. Operates 164 cutters ranging from small patrol boats to 12,000-ton vessels. Responsible for enforcing Chinese territorial claims — often contentiously — across the Yellow Sea, East China Sea, and South China Sea.

## Structure
- Headquarters: Beijing
- Three regional bureaus: North Sea (Qingdao), East Sea (Shanghai), South Sea (Guangzhou)
- 6 directly subordinate bureaus controlling large cutters
- Personnel: ~16,000

## Assets at [[The Gate]]
- [[Haijing 2306]] — Shucha II-class cutter — second on scene
- Commanding officer: [[Chen Shaoqiang|Senior Captain Chen Shaoqiang]]

## Relationships
- [[Korea Coast Guard]] — frequent operational friction; overlapping EEZ claims in the Yellow Sea
- [[People's Liberation Army Navy]] — operational control transfers in wartime
- [[Japan Coast Guard]] — tense; Senkaku/Diaoyu disputes
- [[United States Coast Guard]] — formerly cooperative, now frozen

## Current Status
Second responder to [[The Gate]]. Asserting presence and challenging the South Korean claim to first-responder status. Like the KCG, the CCG is not equipped for what it has found — but Beijing will not cede the initiative.
