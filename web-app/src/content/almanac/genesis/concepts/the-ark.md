# The Ark

## Summary
The theological concept — first articulated by [[Father Beauchamp]] — that modern Earth was deliberately hidden by God from the Adversary, preserving an untouched remnant of uncorrupted faith. An ark meant to survive the flood of holy war.

## Details
[[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] formulates the "ark" idea during his conversation with [[Daniel Foster|Reverend Foster]] aboard *[[HMS Dragon]]*. The logic runs: the Adversary entered the world at Jerusalem in 1099, and from that moment, every soul in the TC timeline has been contested between Heaven and Hell. Modern Earth diverged at the same moment — but Hell never touched it. Why?

The proposed answer: God, in His foresight, split creation. He allowed one world to bear the war and preserved another in silence — a world where faith could grow without the coercion of miracles or the terror of the Tyrants. This hidden world is the ark, kept safe until the flood recedes.

## Context
The idea is immediately dangerous. It casts modern Earth as a chosen people — a role its secular governments and skeptical populations did not ask for and do not want. It also implies that the TC world's suffering is sacrificial: they fight so the ark can float. This dual implication — reverence for modern Earth, resignation about the TC world's fate — will shape the Faithful Crown's diplomatic posture and create profound tension with modern governments who want partnership, not worship.

Within the Faithful Church, the ark theology is divisive. The Synod is likely to embrace it as divine Providence. The [[Sacred Order of the Dragon|Order]]'s more skeptical chaplains may resist it, seeing it as a theological overreach driven by desperate hope rather than sound doctrine.
