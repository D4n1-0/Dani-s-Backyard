# Daniel Foster

## Description
Royal Navy chaplain aboard *[[HMS Dragon|HMS Dragon]]*, mid-forties, Anglican. A compact, balding man with the quiet, unflappable manner of someone who has spent his career counselling sailors through crises of faith and conscience. He wears the standard-issue naval chaplain's uniform — no armour, no reliquary — and carries a small, well-worn copy of the New Testament rather than a crucifix.

## Background
[[Daniel Foster|Foster]] has served as a naval chaplain for eighteen years, postings ranging from Portsmouth barracks to the Persian Gulf. His faith is deep but deliberate — the faith of a man who has never seen a miracle and does not expect to. He has long made his peace with what theologians call the "hiddenness of God": the idea that the Creator's silence is itself an act of grace, preserving space for human freedom.

When [[Father Beauchamp|Beauchamp]] requests a "man of the cloth," [[Daniel Foster|Foster]] is the only chaplain on station. Their conversation becomes the first true theological exchange between the two worlds — and it shakes both men.
